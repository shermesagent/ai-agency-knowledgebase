# Superagency

## Core Idea
Superagency is the idea that AI can give more people access to expertise, leverage, creativity, and decision support that used to be scarce or expensive. It is not “AI replaces human judgment”; it is “more people can act with better information, faster feedback, and more capable tools.”

## Why It Matters
This wiki is organized around AI that expands human agency rather than treating AI as magic, inevitability, or replacement. The strongest evidence stack combines macro diffusion signals, concrete workflow studies, education/tutoring evidence, and risk-management frameworks.

A landmark moment arrived on May 25, 2026: Pope Leo XIV published *[[Magnifica Humanitas]]*, the first papal encyclical on artificial intelligence. The encyclical is arguably the most significant institutional articulation of Superagency principles to date — it reframes AI from "what can it do?" to "who controls it, with what interests, and according to what idea of the human being?" Its core concept of **"disarming technology"** (breaking the equation between technical power and the right to govern) and its "construction site" metaphor (AI's future is not predetermined but actively being built) are direct Superagency frames expressed in the language of Catholic social doctrine. The encyclical explicitly connects the digital revolution to the industrial revolution — the *Rerum Novarum* of our time.

## Best Supporting Sources
- [Magnifica Humanitas](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260525-magnifica-humanitas.html) — Pope Leo XIV, May 25, 2026. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. First papal encyclical on AI. Core concept: "disarming technology" — preventing AI from becoming a form of power that dominates human existence. Frames the digital revolution as the contemporary equivalent of the industrial revolution that *Rerum Novarum* addressed in 1891. The single most significant institutional Superagency document to date.
- [The 2025 AI Index Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report) — Stanford HAI, 2025. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Annual evidence base on AI technical progress, adoption, policy, education, and public attitudes. Useful as the broad context layer for whether AI capability and diffusion are actually becoming agency-expanding infrastructure.
- [2025 AI Adoption Report: Gen AI Fast-Tracks Into the Enterprise](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/special-report/2025-ai-adoption-report/) — Wharton / GBK Collective, 2025. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Tracks enterprise generative AI adoption patterns, common use cases, and the people/process levers needed to turn experimentation into durable ROI. Strong fit for practical adoption and organizational agency pages.
- [Future of Work with AI Agents](https://futureofwork.saltlab.stanford.edu/) — Stanford SALT Lab, 2025. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Audits where AI agents may automate versus augment tasks across the U.S. workforce. Valuable because it separates "can be automated" from "should be delegated," which is central to agency-preserving deployment.
- [Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) — NIST, 2024-07-26. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Cross-sector companion profile for applying the NIST AI RMF to generative AI. Provides concrete risk categories and governance actions for deploying useful AI without ignoring trust, security, privacy, bias, and measurement issues.

## Practical Examples
- A teacher uses AI to generate differentiated practice and feedback, then selects, edits, and sequences the final lesson.
- A worker uses an AI agent to gather context, draft options, and check constraints while keeping the final tradeoff decision human.
- A small organization turns expert workflows into repeatable checklists, AI-assisted drafts, and review loops so more people can perform higher-quality work.

## Risks / Limits
- Superagency collapses into hype if it ignores measurement, worker voice, student privacy, bias, or clear decision rights.
- Vendor adoption reports should be used as signals, not proof; pair them with independent studies and local outcome data.
- More capability can concentrate power if access, skills, and governance are unevenly distributed.
