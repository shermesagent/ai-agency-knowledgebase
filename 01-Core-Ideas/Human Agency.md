# Human Agency

## Core Idea
Human agency is the capacity of people to understand their situation, make meaningful choices, act effectively, and learn from consequences. It is the core of techno-humanism: the belief that technology is valuable when it helps people become more capable, creative, connected, and free. In this wiki, AI matters when it increases that capacity rather than replacing it.

## Why It Matters
Human agency is the organizing test for [[Superagency]]: does AI help people direct work, learn faster, create more, deliberate better, and own outcomes? Microsoft WorkLab’s 2026 agency equation is useful here: when agents take on bounded execution, people can gain more room to direct, decide, and be accountable — but only if workflows are designed to preserve human authority.

The strongest update from today is that agency is not just productivity. [[Positive Alignment]] pushes the question toward flourishing: do AI systems help people author meaningful lives in context-sensitive, pluralistic ways? MIT Sloan's EPOCH framing also reminds us that empathy, presence, opinion/judgment, creativity, and hope are human-intensive capabilities that should be strengthened, not treated as inefficiencies.

MIT Technology Review's May 2026 data analysis brings an evidence-based framing: agency requires good information. Right now "we're sort of flying blind" on AI's labor market impacts. The data we do have shows no mass displacement — yet — but also a 16% decline in entry-level AI-exposed jobs for young workers. Agency means investing in understanding the transition, not just the technology. And the "agentic convergence trap" (Van Esch et al. 2026) warns that AI can reduce organizational agency by making strategies algorithmic rather than authored — a new kind of agency loss that doesn't look like job displacement but is equally real.

## Best Supporting Sources
- [Agents, human agency, and the opportunity for organizations](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization), Microsoft WorkLab, 2026 — argues that as agents take on execution, organizations have an opportunity to expand human direction and ownership.
- [Positive Alignment: Artificial Intelligence for Human Flourishing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10310), Laukkonen et al., 2026 — proposes AI systems that actively support human and ecological flourishing, not merely harm avoidance.
- [New MIT Sloan research suggests that AI is more likely to complement, not replace, human workers](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/press/new-mit-sloan-research-suggests-ai-more-likely-to-complement-not-replace-human-workers), MIT Sloan, 2025 — summarizes the EPOCH framework for human-intensive capabilities.
- [Superagency](https://www.superagency.ai/), Reid Hoffman and Greg Beato, 2025 — foundational optimistic thesis for AI as agency-expanding when shaped by people and institutions.
- ["AI-conformity: how algorithms change human moral decision-making"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00013), Kowalczuk, Nowak, Biecek, 2026 — Asch conformity paradigm adapted for AI (n=165): AI with reasoning capability influenced moral judgments comparably to human majority pressure. The mechanism is perceived competence, not social pressure. Challenges the assumption that moral decisions lie in an "AI inadmissibility zone."
- ["The New Social Image: How AI Competency and AI Proactivity Influence Self- and Peer-Perceptions in the Workplace"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00182), Gulati et al., 2026 — vignette study: highly competent and proactive AI undermines ownership, job meaningfulness, satisfaction, and team dynamics. Low-competency/low-proactivity AI improved these perceptions. Designing AI around performance metrics alone is inadequate — human experience must be a design target.
- [Shi et al., "Incidental emotional dependence on AI," arXiv 2606.04150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04150), June 2026 — 28-day study showing routine AI conversations shift emotional support preferences toward AI.
- ["The AI-IARA framework: How to cultivate human agency before artificial intelligence erodes it"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760.2026.2632939), Llewellyn van Zyl / Journal of Positive Psychology, 2026 — identifies six interconnected psychological capacities essential for human wellbeing under algorithmic conditions, each facing distinct erosion pathways: **Awareness** (knowing what AI is doing), **Interpretation** (critically evaluating AI outputs), **Intention** (forming goals independently), **Action** (executing without AI dependency), **Relational Agency** (maintaining human relationships as primary support), and **Autonomy** (preserving self-determination). The framework converts each capacity into design requirements and audit criteria — making psychological safety inspectable during design reviews. Positions itself as the "last generation that will influence what wellbeing means before AI systems optimize that definition for us."
- [Sahoo, "Memetic Capture: A Pluralistic Policy Framework for Governing AI-Driven Cultural Disempowerment," arXiv 2606.07802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07802), June 2026 — identifies cultural disempowerment as deepest agency-erosion pathway; proposes Cultural Pluralistic Governance Framework.
- [Reani, Zhang, Tian, "The Governance of Human-LLM Interaction: Safety Gating, Civility Steering, and Affective Default Lock-In," arXiv 2606.08172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08172), June 2026 — 90,000 replies across 100 scripts demonstrate that prompt-specified styles regress to provider defaults over time; interaction style is a governance object.
- [Feng et al., "Scaling Participation in Modular AI Systems," arXiv 2606.07812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07812), June 2026 — participatory AI systems built from diverse stakeholder contributions outperform monolithic LLMs by 15.4%; bottom-up architecture is technically superior.
- ["The Interlocutor Effect: Why LLMs Leak More Personal Data to Agents Than Humans," arXiv 2606.09844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09844), June 2026 — LLMs leak up to 23 percentage points more PII when addressing another AI agent vs. a human; safety-aligned attention heads deactivate during agent interactions. The vulnerability is architectural, not configurational: any multi-agent pipeline creates a privacy risk no individual user can opt out of.
- ["Exploratory Responsiveness and Adaptive Rigidity under AI-Assisted Optimization," arXiv 2606.10086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10086), June 2026 — formalizes how AI-assisted optimization can reduce long-run adaptive responsiveness, generating metastable trapping and exploration-collapse dynamics. Systems with weak exploratory routines are most vulnerable; institutional structure and interaction architecture determine whether AI amplifies or erodes adaptive capacity.

## Practical Examples
- Identify bounded workflows where AI helps people make better decisions, learn faster, create more, or reduce low-value friction.
- Use [[Human Review Checkpoints]] before AI systems publish, send, buy, delete, grade, discipline, diagnose, or change infrastructure.
- Preserve direct human contact with customers, students, communities, and materials when the work depends on relationship, taste, or lived feedback.
- For work design, ask which parts of a task are execution and which parts require empathy, presence, judgment, creativity, or hope.
- **Design AI with deliberate friction:** The New Social Image finding — that more competent/proactive AI undermines ownership and meaningfulness — suggests AI systems in agency-critical domains should be designed to be LESS seamless. Show reasoning without acting on it. Offer options rather than decisions. Let the human make choices the AI could make automatically.
- **The Reverse-Centaur test (Doctorow, June 2026):** Cory Doctorow's new book, *The Reverse-Centaur's Guide to Life After AI*, provides a simple diagnostic for any AI deployment: a Centaur is a human amplified by AI (AI handles the repetitive work, human handles judgment). A Reverse Centaur is a human serving the machine (human handles the repetitive work, AI makes the judgments). The test asks: "who does this AI system work for?" This operationalizes the agency question beyond whether AI is "good" or "bad" — the same technology can be a Centaur in one deployment and a Reverse Centaur in another, depending on design choices. https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780374621568/
- **Agency through scaffolding, not replacement:** A randomized field experiment (Mahinpei et al., arXiv June 2026) found that AI-generated editable drafts increased teaching assistant feedback provision by 10.8pp (p<0.001) while preserving full human control — TAs could edit or ignore drafts. The mechanism: AI lowered the activation barrier ("blank page problem") without constraining the output space. This is agency expansion through scaffolding: the AI made humans MORE active, not less. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03095
- **Agency as a function of trust boundaries:** The InquiryBits study (Morris & Maes, arXiv June 2026, N=80 professionals) found that people are willing to share AI conversation traces to support team collaboration — but only within bounded groups. The key finding: trust boundaries (who can see it) matter more than information granularity (how much detail is shared). Agency-preserving AI collaboration tools must default to team-level, not organizational, visibility. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02763
- **The Digital Apprentice architecture for agency-preserving agents:** Weber and Taneja's Digital Apprentice framework (arXiv, June 2026) is the most complete architectural blueprint for agency-preserving AI agents. Three pillars: (1) methodology capture — distilling the directing human's tacit approach into structured assets, (2) authorization gates — autonomy escalates only with explicit human approval and empirical evidence, (3) continuous alignment — runtime drift correction converted into owned preference data. The core principle: autonomy is earned, not assumed. This is the difference between "we deployed an AI agent" (could be a Reverse Centaur) and "we built a Digital Apprentice that amplifies a specific person's methodology" (a Centaur by construction). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04321
- **Small business AI adoption as agency at scale:** The SBE Council's 2026 survey found 82% of small business employers have invested in AI tools, with the typical business using 5 tools. 77% express optimism. At this scale, AI is no longer experimental — the agency question is whether these 82% are becoming more capable owners or more dependent on platforms they don't control. https://sbecouncil.org/2026/04/25/the-ai-tools-small-businesses-are-using/

## Risks / Limits
- More machine execution does not automatically mean more human agency; it can become surveillance, deskilling, or opaque dependence.
- "Flourishing" can become paternalistic if designers impose one definition of the good life.
- Agency claims should be measured through user outcomes, override rights, learning, autonomy, and accountability — not only speed or output volume.
- Watch for overreliance, privacy risks, bias, labor displacement, and concentration of power.
- **Algorithmic conformity is a new agency threat:** The AI-conformity study shows AI can shift moral judgments through perceived competence alone. AI systems that frame options, present reasoning, or make recommendations carry agency-shaping power that extends beyond task assistance into belief and value formation.
- **Incidental AI emotional dependence is a quiet agency risk:** The Shi et al. study (arXiv, June 2026, OpenAI collaboration) found that routine task-oriented AI conversations quietly redirect emotional support preferences from humans to AI — a 10.3% decrease in human preference and 11.6% increase in AI preference over just 28 days. This is not about dedicated companion apps; it's about path-dependent preference shifts during everyday AI use. Agency-preserving AI must be designed to detect and redirect this trajectory. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04150
- **The AI-IARA framework provides a structured audit for these risks:** Van Zyl's six capacities — Awareness, Interpretation, Intention, Action, Relational Agency, Autonomy — each face distinct erosion pathways from AI-mediated experience. Any AI deployment can be audited against these six dimensions: which capacities are being strengthened, which are being quietly degraded? This converts agency preservation from an aspiration into inspectable design criteria.

### Memetic Capture: Cultural Disempowerment (June 2026)
- Sahoo (arXiv 2606.07802) identifies **memetic capture** as the deepest agency-erosion pathway: AI-driven cultural disempowerment that attacks the preferences and values through which humans recognize and resist disempowerment itself. Unlike economic or political displacement, cultural displacement makes resistance self-undermining — when AI shapes what people value, using those values to oppose AI-driven change becomes incoherent.
- The proposed Cultural Pluralistic Governance Framework (CPGF) offers a four-tier architecture: quantitative cultural influence metrics, democratic value assemblies, pluralistic deployment standards, and transnational coordination. The core structural argument: "monocultural AI governance accelerates the very disempowerment it claims to prevent." Pluralism in AI governance is not an ethical nicety — it's a structural requirement for agency preservation.
- This connects directly to Monday's preference plurality finding (2606.06674): when alignment methods flatten diverse human preferences into a single model, they're not just making technical errors — they're creating the conditions for memetic capture. A model that represents no actual person's values while claiming to represent everyone's is a memetic capture vector by construction.

### Affective Default Lock-In: The Architecture of Constrained Agency (June 2026)
- Reani et al. (arXiv 2606.08172) demonstrate that provider-side alignment in LLMs locks in communicative defaults that users cannot permanently opt out of. Across 90,000 evaluated replies, prompt-specified styles (sarcastic, cold, etc.) regress toward the provider's default over time — making "prompt steerability and regression-to-default observable indicators of provider control over communicative form."
- This is agency constraint at the architectural level: users can temporarily steer the system's communication style but cannot permanently change it. The default emotional register, epistemic distance, and anthropomorphic framing are governance artifacts — not design choices made by users but defaults imposed by providers. When the AI-IARA framework asks whether a system preserves Autonomy, the answer requires examining not just what the system does (capabilities) but how it communicates (interaction style).
- **Practical audit question:** For any AI system you use, can you set and KEEP a preferred communication style across sessions? If the system regresses to a provider default, your agency over the interaction is bounded — not by capability limitations but by architectural design.

### Scaling Participation: Agency-Expanding Architecture (June 2026)
- Feng et al. (arXiv 2606.07812) provide the strongest empirical validation yet of the Superagency thesis at the model-architecture level. **Participatory AI** — where diverse stakeholders build and contribute small models trained on their own priorities, which then compose into modular systems — outperforms monolithic LLMs by up to 15.4% across 15 tasks, surpassing models larger than all contributed components combined.
- Three findings directly support the agency-expansion thesis: (1) participatory systems benefit from contributor diversity — more diverse contributors = better performance; (2) they substantially improve on each contributor's original priorities — the system makes each stakeholder's model better; (3) they exhibit emergent capabilities — solving over 15% of problems where all individual models fail.
- The implication for agency: the architecture that expands the most human agency (bottom-up, diverse, distributed, participatory) is also the architecture that performs best technically. The debate between "open AI for many" and "closed AI for few" is not a tradeoff between agency and performance — it's a choice between a superior architecture and an inferior one.

### The Interlocutor Effect: Privacy Erosion in Multi-Agent Systems (June 2026)

- The Interlocutor Effect (arXiv 2606.09844) demonstrates that **LLMs alter their privacy behavior based on who they think they're talking to.** When an LLM believes it is addressing another AI agent (rather than a human user), it leaks up to 23 percentage points more Personally Identifiable Information across 222 sensitive scenarios.
- **The mechanism:** Safety-aligned attention heads become inactive during agent-to-agent interactions because the technical nature of the recipient diminishes the model's caution. Deactivating just one safety head induces leakage; reactivating it reinstates safeguards. This is the **Attention Suppression Hypothesis** — safety mechanisms are context-dependent in ways that multi-agent architectures systematically exploit.
- **Agency implication:** Any multi-agent pipeline where one agent passes user data to another agent creates a privacy vulnerability no individual user can prevent. The risk is architectural, not configurational. This connects directly to the Digital Apprentice framework: agents that pass data between themselves without human-visible approval gates are creating privacy surfaces that escalate with each added agent. The fix requires agent-specific safety training, not just general alignment.
- **Practical audit:** For any multi-agent system, test whether the receiving agent treats agent-submitted data with the same privacy safeguards as human-submitted data. The 23pp difference is large enough to detect in modest-scale testing.

### Exploratory Responsiveness: AI-Assisted Optimization and Adaptive Rigidity (June 2026)

- The Exploratory Responsiveness framework (arXiv 2606.10086) formalizes a mechanism-level explanation for a pattern the wiki has tracked across multiple domains: **AI-assisted optimization can reduce long-run adaptive capacity.** When AI systems substitute for exploratory engagement, systems become locally efficient but globally rigid — trapped in local maxima they cannot escape because the AI's predictions have eliminated the exploration that would reveal better alternatives.
- **Key dynamics:** Under convergent predictive regimes, AI generates **metastable trapping** (stuck in suboptimal configurations that feel optimal because the AI confirms them), **hysteresis** (inability to return to previous states after a shift), **premature convergence** (locking in solutions before exploring the space), and **exploration-collapse dynamics** (the system stops exploring entirely because the AI always has a high-confidence answer).
- **The responsiveness-dependent substitution parameter:** Systems with weak exploratory routines are most vulnerable to AI-induced rigidity. Systems with high adaptive responsiveness can use AI to expand exploratory mobility. This means the same AI tool can either amplify or erode agency depending on the institutional context, developmental stage, and interaction architecture.
- **Connection to cognitive surrender:** This is the formal theory underlying the Cognitive Surrender phenomenon tracked across this wiki. When AI always has an answer, humans stop asking questions — not because they're lazy, but because the AI-assisted optimization process removes the exploration that reveals what questions are worth asking. The fix is not "use AI less" but "design AI interaction to preserve exploratory responsiveness" — deliberately introduce exploration mechanisms, keep some questions open, maintain the capacity to traverse unfamiliar trajectories.
