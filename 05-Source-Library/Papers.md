## Accepted Sources
- **[AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)** — Scientific Reports / Nature Portfolio, 2025-06-03. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. RCT evidence for a research-designed AI tutor in an authentic educational setting, with transfer limits. Related: [[AI Tutors]], [[Education]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[Generative AI at Work](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)** — Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey R. Raymond, NBER, 2023. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Related: [[Work]], [[Future of Work]], [[Democratization of Expertise]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf)** — Dell’Acqua et al., 2023. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. - **[The Turing Trap: The Promise & Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04200)** — Erik Brynjolfsson, 2022. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. - **[The Organizational Behavior of Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30986)** — Canhui Liu, arXiv, 2026-07-01. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Reframes AI agent collectives as "partial organizational analogues" sustained by context architecture (prompts, memory, traces, schemas, tools, validators, permissions) rather than human motivation, identity, or trust. Introduces contextual transaction cost as the central mechanism. Finds shared-state and adaptive agent forms outperform human-imitation organizational forms (teams, managers, committees). Related: [[AI Agent Revolution]], [[Agentic Convergence Trap]], [[Agentic Workflow Patterns]], [[Home Server AI Agents]].
- **[Beyond Expert Users: CoPref/CoShop — Agents Should Help Users Construct Preferences, Not Just Elicit Them](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30863)** — arXiv, 2026-07-01. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Formalizes a fundamental agency blind spot: AI agents assume users have well-formed preferences, but real users lack domain knowledge to have fully specified preferences. Introduces CoPref (preference construction model) and CoShop (benchmark). No frontier model exceeds 56% accuracy. Failure mode is not finding items — it's that the interaction doesn't expand what users know about what they want. Related: [[Human Agency]], [[Superagency]], [[Co-Intelligence]], [[Democratization of Expertise]].
- **[AgentBound: Verifiable Behavioral Governance for Autonomous AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30970)** — arXiv, 2026-07-01. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Cryptographically verifiable governance receipts for autonomous AI agents. Three independent authorities (delegated authorization, owner-signed behavioral constitutions, site action contracts) composed through a formal decision model into permit/review/deny. Extends DeepMind's Control Roadmap with a verification layer — governance that can be independently audited. Directly applicable to EU AI Act enforcement (August 2026). Related: [[Responsible Deployment]], [[Balanced Governance]], [[Home Server AI Agents]], [[AI Agent Revolution]].
- **[AI Transparency: Governance Compliance or Stakeholder Requirements? The Transparency Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30652)** — arXiv, 2026-07-01. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Identifies the "transparency illusion" — AI transparency artefacts (model cards, compliance reports) satisfy governance requirements on paper but are calibrated to regulators (high Control/Involvement) rather than end users (high Risk/Need). Introduces RCIN framework (Risk-Control-Involvement-Need) for measuring transparency calibration. Related: [[Responsible Deployment]], [[Public Trust and AI]], [[Balanced Governance]].
- **[The Consistency Dilemma in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30653)** — arXiv, 2026-07-01. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Counterintuitive finding: more self-consistent models are MORE vulnerable to mistakes. Consistency is achieved by suppressing internal uncertainty signals — the very signals that would alert human reviewers. A model giving the same wrong answer 10 times is more dangerous than one giving varied answers. Deployment implication: prefer models that surface uncertainty; add deliberate perturbation to reveal hidden brittleness. Related: [[Responsible Deployment]], [[Case Against AI Doomism]], [[Strongest AI Risk Arguments]].
