# Papers

Curated source index for papers relevant to AI agency, Superagency, practical adoption, and responsible optimism.

## Accepted Sources
- **[AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)** — Scientific Reports / Nature Portfolio, 2025-06-03. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. RCT evidence for a research-designed AI tutor in an authentic educational setting, with transfer limits. Related: [[AI Tutors]], [[Education]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[Generative AI at Work](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)** — Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, and Lindsey R. Raymond, NBER, 2023. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Field evidence that AI assistance improved customer-support productivity, especially for less-experienced workers. Related: [[Work]], [[Future of Work]], [[Democratization of Expertise]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf)** — Dell’Acqua et al., 2023. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Field experiment with consultants showing large gains inside the AI frontier and worse performance outside it. Related: [[AI Use Case Evaluation Rubric]], [[Work]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[The Turing Trap: The Promise & Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04200)** — Erik Brynjolfsson, 2022. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Distinguishes augmentation from substitution and warns that human-like imitation can steer AI toward labor replacement and concentrated gains. Related: [[Work]], [[Future of Work]], [[AI as Copilot]], [[AI and Inequality]].
- **[The Cybernetic Teammate](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33641)** — Fabrizio Dell’Acqua et al., NBER, 2025. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Field experiment on AI reshaping teamwork and expertise, expanding the evidence base beyond individual productivity. Related: [[Frontier Firm]], [[Work]], [[Future of Work]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big?](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3442188.3445922)** — Emily M. Bender, Timnit Gebru, Angelina McMillan-Major, and Margaret Mitchell, 2021. Reliability 5/5; relevance 4/5. Foundational risk critique of bias, documentation gaps, concentration costs, and misleading language-model fluency. Related: [[Strongest AI Risk Arguments]], [[Responsible Deployment]], [[AI as Normal Technology]].
- **[Understanding Human-AI Augmentation in the Workplace](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-025-10591-5)** — Trinh Nguyen and Amany Elbanna, Information Systems Frontiers, 2025-03-04. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Open-access review synthesizing human-AI augmentation research across workplace and management contexts. Related: [[Work]], [[AI as Copilot]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[Positive Alignment: Artificial Intelligence for Human Flourishing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10310)** — Ruben Laukkonen et al., arXiv, 2026-05-11/revised 2026-05-14. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Preprint proposing alignment that actively supports pluralistic human and ecological flourishing. Related: [[Positive Alignment]], [[Human Agency]], [[AI and Human Flourishing]].
- **[AI in the Enterprise: How People Use M365 Copilot Chat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23958)** — Counts, Chen, Dong et al., arXiv / Microsoft Research, 2026-05-26. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Enterprise telemetry analysis of 5.5M sessions across 1M+ companies; documents shift from chat-as-search to content creation and communication. Related: [[Work]], [[AI as Copilot]].
- **[Human-AI Collaboration in Science at Scale: A Global Large-scale Randomized Field Experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24180)** — Wang, Liang, Xue et al., arXiv, 2026-05-26. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Global RCT across 31K preprints, 45K researchers, 133 regions; AI feedback increased revision rates by 12.55%, with strongest benefits for underrepresented researchers. Related: [[AI-Augmented Scientific Collaboration]], [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]].
- **[Catching The Correct Answer Trap: Characterising AI Tutor Blind Spots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23925)** — Imran and Bulathwela, arXiv, 2026-05-26. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Documents AI tutor failure mode where flawed reasoning that produces correct answers goes undetected; frontier LLMs achieve only 57% detection with 4:1 false-alarm ratio. Related: [[Education]], [[AI Tutors]].
- **[From Replacement to Orchestration: HARMONY Operating Model for Agentic R&D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24580)** — Boussaid, Heemskerk et al., arXiv, 2026-05-26. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Four-pillar architecture for agentic corporate R&D introducing the Sciencepreneur archetype and Orchestration Leverage metric. Related: [[Work]], [[Frontier Firm]], [[Agentic Workflow Patterns]].
- **[Explaining Too Much? Understanding How LLM Reasoning Traces Influence Performance and Metacognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25856)** — Fernandes, Buschek et al., arXiv, 2026-05-26. Reliability 5/5; relevance 4/5. Preregistered N=559 study: reasoning traces increase trust without improving (sometimes impairing) performance; traces are interface artifacts, not cognitive windows. Related: [[AI as Copilot]], [[Human Review Checkpoints]].
- **[Who Does Your AI Work For? Designing Conversational Agents as Digital Fiduciaries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28908)** — Erickson, ACM CUI '26 / arXiv, 2026-05-29. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Provocation arguing conversational AI agents should have fiduciary duty to act in users' best interest. Related: [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]], [[Balanced Governance]].
- **[Dissociative Identity: Language Model Agents Lack Grounding for Reputation Mechanisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30169)** — Hu, Rong, Van Kleek, FAccT 2026 / arXiv, 2026-05-29. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Shows agent dissociativity makes reputation-based governance structurally inapplicable; argues for observability-based governance. Related: [[Dissociative Agent Governance]], [[AI Agent Revolution]].
- **[Governing Technical Debt in Agentic AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29129)** — Hydari, Iqbal, Ramasubbu, arXiv, 2026-05-29. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Defines Agentic Technical Debt and Stochastic Tax as production governance concepts for agent deployments. Related: [[Agentic Technical Debt]], [[Human Review Checkpoints]].
- **[Offloading Score: Measuring AI Reliance Through Counterfactual Workflows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29392)** — arXiv, 2026-05-29. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Quantitative instrument detecting 43% offloading increase under time pressure where conventional metrics miss. Related: [[Co-Intelligence]], [[Human Review Checkpoints]].
- **[Political Neutrality as Balanced Approval (PARETO Dataset)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28911)** — Stray et al., arXiv, 2026-05-29. Reliability 4/5; relevance 4/5. Empirical demonstration that AI political neutrality is achievable (N=7,434, 208K evaluations). Related: [[Balanced Governance]], [[Public Trust and AI]].
- **["It's OK Because...": The Wild West of Student Rationalization of AI Use in Academic Writing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29090)** — Kim, Toyama, Kim, Carroll, arXiv, 2026-05-29. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Taxonomy of 20+ student rationalizations for AI-aided cheating; documents ethical slippery slope. Related: [[Education]], [[Agentic Literacy Debt]].
- **[Modularizing Educational LLM-Agency for Fostering Responsible Learning Assistance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30187)** — AISoLA 2025 / arXiv, 2026-05-29. Reliability 4/5; relevance 4/5. Argues for modular agentic architecture over monolithic LLMs for responsible educational AI. Related: [[Education]], [[AI Tutors]].
