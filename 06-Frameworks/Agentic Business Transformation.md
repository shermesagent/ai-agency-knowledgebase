# Agentic Business Transformation

## Core Idea
Agentic Business Transformation (ABT) is a framework for how enterprises adopt AI agents at a systems level — not by layering agents onto existing operations, but by reimagining the operating model. Coined by Ema in partnership with HFS Research, ABT argues that AI agents are categorically different from previous technology waves: digital transformation was paper-to-software, AI transformation added intelligence to processes, copilots assist human tasks — but ABT "is the integration of AI agents into the fabric of the organization."

## Why It Matters
85% of organizations want to be agentic within three years, but 76% say their current operations and infrastructure can't support the change. The gap between ambition and execution is the central problem ABT addresses. Without systems-level redesign, organizations are "adding sticky tape to parts of an operating model that is breaking" (Prasun Shah, PwC). ABT matters because it provides a structured framework for closing this gap — and because the organizations that close it first will capture disproportionate value from the agent revolution.

## The Three Pillars

### 1. Technology Stack: AI Agents as Connective Tissue
The existing tech stack was "designed for human-operated, application-centric workflows." AI agents operate at machine speed across multiple systems simultaneously. They need to be connective tissue — moving between layers to coordinate tasks or retrieve data from multiple applications — not another layer in the stack. Organizations that make this shift "become genuinely more adaptive": when a new business requirement emerges, you configure an AI agent using natural language instead of waiting months for a software vendor. "The time from business to production workflow drops from months to days" (Surojit Chatterjee, Ema CEO).

### 2. Workforce Redesign: Hybrid Human-AI Teams
Hierarchical structures from industrialization break down when AI agents handle coordination. Key shifts:
- Managers freed from execution oversight take on new responsibilities: trust, explainability, psychological safety, status dynamics in hybrid teams
- McKinsey predicts that by 2030, three-quarters of current jobs will require redesign, upskilling, or redeployment
- New tensions: Who's accountable when an AI employee errs? What happens when AI and humans disagree? What guardrails protect customers?
- The "Sciencepreneur" archetype from the HARMONY R&D model: humans orchestrate AI agents rather than perform tasks directly

### 3. Outcome-Based Metrics
Traditional activity metrics (calls handled, reports filed) become meaningless when AI can handle 1,000 interactions in the time humans handle 10. Key shifts:
- From "cost per query" and "AI accuracy" to "percentage of contracts reviewed without human escalation"
- One enterprise customer switched metrics and saw measured ROI from agentic AI triple within two quarters
- Reward and talent management processes need reconfiguration for hybrid team accountability
- Operational accountability becomes more diffused across human-AI systems

## Practical Examples
- Customer service: AI agents handle initial triage; humans handle escalation; metrics shift from call volume to resolution rate without escalation
- R&D: HARMONY model uses ResOps (industrialized execution), Control Tower (strategic visibility), Ethics Fabric (bounded autonomy), Talent Studio (Sciencepreneur capability)
- Enterprise ABT customer: Switched from tool metrics to outcome metrics; stopped building point solutions in low-complexity workflows; deployed AI where outcome value was highest

## Risks / Limits
- The ABT framework is vendor-sponsored (Ema); independence of the analysis should be verified
- Systems-level change is slow and politically difficult in large organizations
- The three-quarters job redesign prediction is a massive workforce transition with real human costs
- New accountability questions (who's responsible when AI errs?) have no settled answers
- The framework assumes organizational willingness to fundamentally redesign — many will try to layer agents on existing structures and fail

## Transformation Includes the Exit, Not Just the Launch (2026-10-19)

[WIRED's review of Character.AI court filings](https://www.wired.com/story/disney-asked-characterai-to-go-bankrupt-for-a-get-out-of-lawsuits-card/) describes a proposed Disney arrangement to hire most staff and bid for a technology license during Chapter 11, while the startup remains the named party in litigation and investigations. The license is subject to auction and court approval; the reported IP list excludes user data. This is a proposed transaction, not a completed judicial transfer of liability. Still, it exposes a gap in this vendor-sponsored framework: an agent operating model must work when a provider fails, sells assets or moves its team—not only while the deployment grows.

**Exit gate:** before procurement, record (1) custody and deletion of client/user data, (2) ownership of incident logs and safety controls, (3) a surviving human contact for complaints, (4) retention and appeal rights for affected people, and (5) the contractual transition plan if staff, model rights and obligations separate. Test the plan without real student or patient records.
