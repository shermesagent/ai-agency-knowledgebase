# Entrepreneurship

## Core Idea
AI can lower the cost of starting and operating ventures by giving founders research, prototyping, automation, coding, design, and customer-support leverage.

## Why It Matters
This idea matters because the knowledgebase is organized around AI that expands human agency rather than treating AI as magic, inevitability, or replacement. Future daily digests should add concrete evidence, examples, critiques, and citations here.

## Best Supporting Sources
- [The AI Tools Small Businesses Are Using](https://sbecouncil.org/2026/04/25/the-ai-tools-small-businesses-are-using/) — SBE Council, April 2026. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. Survey data: 82% of small business employers invested in AI, typical business uses 5 tools, 77% optimism.
- [How Small Businesses Can Leverage AI](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/02/1138227/how-small-businesses-can-leverage-ai/) — MIT Technology Review, June 2026. Reliability 4/5; relevance 5/5. AI as force multiplier for SMBs in accounting, design, market research, and product development — functions that previously required specialized hires.
- [The Digital Apprentice: A Framework for Human-Directed Agentic AI Development](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04321) — Weber & Taneja, June 2026. Reliability 5/5; relevance 5/5. Architectural framework where agent autonomy is earned through methodology capture, authorization gates, and continuous alignment — directly applicable to small business AI deployment.

## Practical Examples
- AI handles bookkeeping categorization, invoice generation, and expense tracking — letting the owner focus on business strategy rather than data entry.
- AI generates design mockups, marketing copy variants, and market research summaries — giving solo founders capabilities that previously required hiring specialists or agencies.
- AI agents manage customer service triage, appointment scheduling, and follow-up reminders — extending the owner's reach without adding headcount.
- The Digital Apprentice model: instead of handing an AI agent full autonomy over business functions, use methodology capture to teach the agent the owner's specific approach, require authorization for new action types, and convert corrections into permanent preferences.
- **The Reverse-Centaur diagnostic for small business AI:** For each AI tool: who is doing the repetitive work? If the owner is checking AI outputs, fixing AI errors, and formatting data while the AI makes recommendations, that's a Reverse Centaur. The goal is the opposite: AI handles the repetitive work so the owner can focus on judgment and relationships.

## Risks / Limits
- **Platform dependence risk:** At 82% adoption across 5 tools per business, small businesses may become dependent on AI platforms they don't control. Tool pricing changes, API deprecations, or vendor shutdowns could strand critical business functions.
- **Deskilling risk:** If AI handles accounting, design, and market research, the owner may lose the ability to evaluate AI outputs critically. The Digital Apprentice model mitigates this by preserving methodology knowledge rather than replacing it.
- **Homogenization risk:** If 82% of small businesses use similar AI tools, competitive differentiation may narrow. The businesses that thrive will be those where AI amplifies a distinctive human methodology, not where it substitutes for one.
- **The emotional dependence trap:** Small business owners who use AI for both task support and incidental emotional support (stress, isolation, decision anxiety) may experience the same preference shift documented by Shi et al. — redirecting support-seeking from human networks to AI.
