# Public Trust and AI

## Core Idea
The cultural and political legitimacy of AI is not guaranteed by its technical capability. Public trust in AI is shaped by visible signals — graduates booing AI pep talks, workers returning from leave to unrecognizable jobs, polls showing demand for regulation — that reveal a widening gap between elite AI enthusiasm and public anxiety. AI's social license to operate must be earned and maintained, not assumed.

## Why It Matters
Superagency cannot succeed if the people it's supposed to serve don't believe in it. The framing matters: "embrace AI or be replaced" generates backlash; "AI can make you more capable at what you care about" invites participation. The trust dimension is not a communications problem — it's a design problem. When AI deployment creates losers faster than it creates visible winners, trust erodes regardless of messaging.

## Best Supporting Sources
- **Caiwei Chen, "The AI Hype Index: AI Gets Booed in Graduation Season" (MIT Technology Review, May 28, 2026)** — Eric Schmidt booed at University of Arizona; similar jeering at multiple universities. Schmidt's concession: fears about disappearing jobs are "rational." https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/05/28/1138053/
- **Leena Mathur et al., "When Should AI Read the Room? Public Perceptions of Social Intelligence in AI Agents" (arXiv, May 29, 2026)** — Documents the "support-adoption gap": people support Social-AI for others far more than for themselves. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29938
- **Jonathan Stray et al., "Political Neutrality as Balanced Approval" (arXiv, May 29, 2026)** — Shows political neutrality is achievable but not default; default models lean liberal. Public trust requires demonstrable neutrality. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28911
- **Joel Khalili, "New Moms Are Returning to Coding Jobs Radically Reshaped by AI" (WIRED, May 28, 2026)** — The human face of AI-driven workplace disruption undermining trust. https://www.wired.com/story/women-parental-leave-return-office-ai/

## Practical Examples
- Graduation season 2026: students at multiple universities jeer at AI-focused commencement speeches. The backlash is not fringe — it's mainstream.
- The "support-adoption gap": people say AI assistants for the elderly are a good idea, but don't want one for their own parents. Abstract approval ≠ personal trust.
- Illinois SB 315 passed with bipartisan support and endorsements from OpenAI and Anthropic — suggesting the industry sees regulation as a trust-building mechanism, not just a constraint.
- Women returning from maternity leave to AI-transformed workplaces: a concrete trust erosion event where the system's failure to accommodate life transitions damages faith in AI-driven progress.

## Risks / Limits
- **Trust is slow to build and fast to lose.** Each "move fast and break things" deployment erodes trust for the entire industry, not just the deploying company.
- **The trust gap may be generational.** Younger people may have fundamentally different relationships with AI than older generations — booing at graduations may reflect expectations, not rejection.
- **Trust metrics are hard to operationalize.** Surveys capture stated preferences; actual behavior (adoption, reliance) may tell a different story.
