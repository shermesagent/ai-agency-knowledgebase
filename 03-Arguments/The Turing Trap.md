# The Turing Trap

## Core Idea
The Turing Trap is Erik Brynjolfsson’s warning that AI progress can be misdirected when society rewards machines for imitating or replacing humans instead of augmenting what humans can do.

## Why It Matters
This is one of the most important counterweights to shallow AI optimism. [[Superagency]] depends on design and incentives: the same technical capability can either expand human productivity and judgment or concentrate returns by substituting for workers.

## Best Supporting Sources
- [The Turing Trap: The Promise & Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04200), Erik Brynjolfsson, 2022 — distinguishes augmentation from substitution and warns against overvaluing human-like imitation.
- [[Work]] and [[AI and Inequality]] — wiki pages where this argument has direct implications.

## Practical Examples
- Evaluate a workplace AI pilot by asking whether it complements employees’ judgment or merely removes them from the loop.
- Prefer tools that make workers more capable, transparent, and accountable over tools that hide labor displacement behind “efficiency.”
- Use task-level analysis before deciding whether AI should automate, augment, or stay out of a workflow.

## Risks / Limits
- Some automation can be beneficial when it removes unsafe, tedious, or low-value work.
- The trap is not automation itself; it is optimizing around replacement without shared prosperity, human development, and institutional accountability.
