# Compute and Agency

## Core Idea
Compute and agency asks how access to chips, cloud infrastructure, models, and deployment platforms shapes who can build, audit, govern, and benefit from AI.

## Why It Matters
AI can democratize expertise at the user level while still concentrating power at the infrastructure level. A serious [[Superagency]] view has to track both: who gets useful tools, and who controls the compute, data, distribution, and policy chokepoints behind those tools.

## Best Supporting Sources
- [Computational Power and AI](https://ainowinstitute.org/publications/compute-and-ai), AI Now Institute, 2023 — argues that compute control shapes AI power and policy.
- [[AI and Inequality]] — broader page on who benefits from AI adoption.
- [[Balanced Governance]] — governance frame for access, accountability, and public-interest capacity.

## Practical Examples
- Prefer interoperable tools and exportable data when possible so users are not trapped in one vendor’s workflow.
- Track whether local, open, or smaller models are good enough for privacy-sensitive or community-scale use cases.
- Treat public-interest AI infrastructure as part of civic capacity, not only as a private market question.

## Risks / Limits
- Compute concentration arguments can become too abstract if they are not tied to concrete user harms and policy choices.
- Local/open models are not automatically safer or fairer; they still need evaluation and responsible deployment.
- Large-scale infrastructure can also enable beneficial services that smaller systems cannot provide.
