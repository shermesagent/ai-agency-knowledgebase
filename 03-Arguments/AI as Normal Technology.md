# AI as Normal Technology

## Core Idea
AI as normal technology means treating AI as powerful, socially important, fallible, and governable rather than as magic, destiny, or an alien superintelligence outside ordinary institutions.

## Why It Matters
This framing strengthens [[Optimism Without Naivety]]. If AI is normal technology, then the right questions become practical and empirical: what works, for whom, compared with what baseline, under what safeguards, and with what distribution of benefits and harms? That is more useful for [[Human Agency]] than both shallow hype and fatalistic doom.

## Best Supporting Sources
- [AI as Normal Technology](https://www.normaltech.ai/), Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, ongoing — argues for analyzing AI through evidence, institutions, incentives, and measurable deployment outcomes.
- [AI Snake Oil](https://www.aisnakeoil.com/), Narayanan and Kapoor, 2024 book/newsletter lineage — critiques predictive-AI failures, inflated claims, and unsupported deployment in high-stakes settings.
- [The Turing Trap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04200), Erik Brynjolfsson, 2022 — warns that the social value of AI depends on whether systems augment or substitute for human labor.
- [AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), NIST — offers a practical governance loop for turning normal-technology thinking into deployment practice.

## Practical Examples
- Evaluate AI tools against a real baseline: current human process, cost, error rate, time, and user experience.
- Separate demonstrations from deployment evidence; a viral demo is not a school, clinic, business, or civic system.
- Require human appeal and auditability when AI affects access to opportunity, services, money, or discipline.
- Ask whether a system broadens capability for many people or concentrates capability in a small group of vendors and gatekeepers.

## Risks / Limits
- “Normal technology” should not minimize frontier or systemic risks; it should make them easier to reason about.
- Overcorrecting against hype can become pessimistic inertia if institutions avoid useful experiments.
- Some AI systems may have unusual scale, speed, or opacity compared with older technologies, so normal governance may need upgrades.
