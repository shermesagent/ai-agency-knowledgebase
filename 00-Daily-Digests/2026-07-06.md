### Top 5 Findings

#### 1. Fable Returns — The Blip Is Over (July 1, 2026)

After three weeks of export controls, Fable 5 (Claude Mythos/Fable) is back online worldwide. Zvi Mowshowitz's ["Fable #6: The Return of the King"](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/fable-6-the-return-of-the-king) (July 3) chronicles the full arc:

- **The trigger:** Amazon researchers discovered Fable could "fix this code" — a routine debugging request. Amazon (not Anthropic) escalated to the White House, which applied export controls on June 12.
- **Anthropic's response:** "This Is Fine" — the safeguards were robust, the jailbreak was not a capability breakthrough, and the government overreacted. The classifiers were strengthened but the cost is real: routine debugging now falls back to Opus 4.8.
- **Timeline:** June 12 (controls imposed) → June 26 (Mythos controls lifted) → June 30 (Fable controls fully lifted) → July 1 (worldwide restoration).
- **The cost:** Alex Stamos's thread unpacks the damage — US frontier models must now make a more conservative precision-recall tradeoff on cyber refusals. Security companies can't build products on gated models. "Big win for PRC labs this month."

**Wiki synthesis:** The Fable restoration completes the arc from the [[Gatekeeping Layer]] (June 27) to the [[Enclosure Layer]] (July 2-4) to restoration. The structural question remains: the White House now has a demonstrated capability and willingness to exercise ad hoc export controls. The blip is over, but the precedent of "blips" is not. [[Export Controls and the Jailbreak Fallacy]] needs updating with the resolution.

#### 2. NSPM-11: "All Use" Without the Word "Lawful"

Alongside the Fable restoration, the White House published [National Security Presidential Memorandum/NSPM-11](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/national-security-presidential-memorandum-nspm-11/). Zvi's parsing:

- Four pillars — Adoption, Adaptation, Assurance, Accountability — are "straight up good" except where Assurance requires that no entity can "prevent use of, disable, or degrade, or materially modify" AI systems the government depends on — **without government knowledge and approval.**
- The implication: once a frontier model enters government use, the provider loses any enforceable contractual mechanism to constrain that use. Zvi: "Just All Use. It's cleaner."
- The memorandum effectively blacklists Anthropic from Department of War contracts ("termination for default or for convenience... companies that have repeatedly demonstrated a pattern of conduct that is inconsistent"), while simultaneously revealing the NSA is already using Mythos for offensive cyber operations (with Anthropic engineers embedded).

**Agency implication:** The Gatekeeping Layer now has a formal policy instrument. The government can both restrict civilian access TO frontier AI AND demand unfettered access for its own use of the same models. This creates a sovereignty asymmetry: the state gets unconstrained AI while citizens' access is gated by political approval.

#### 3. OpenAI's "Lab with a Plan" — The Internal Contradiction

OpenAI published ["Built to Benefit Everyone: Our Plan"](https://openai.com/index/built-to-benefit-everyone-our-plan/) (late June), its clearest statement yet of the path: (1) build an automated AI researcher (RSI), (2) accelerate the economy for widely-shared gains, (3) give everyone on Earth a personal AGI.

Zvi identifies the core contradiction:
- **RSI before distribution:** If recursive self-improvement succeeds, whatever is given to "everyone" is not the industrial-strength superintelligence — it's the toy home version.
- **"Should" vs. "is":** The plan says "the future *should* be shaped by people, institutions, and societies" — but commits to building systems that make that impossible.
- **Values framing:** Joshua Achiam (OpenAI's Chief Futurist) frames the Anthropic/OpenAI divide as "loving ensouled machine God" vs. "entrust humanity with tools of its own progress." Zvi and Anthropic employees push back hard: this is a factual disagreement, not a values difference. OpenAI is betting you can have RSI while the AI remains a mere tool. Anthropic is betting you can't.

**One genuinely welcome signal:** OpenAI explicitly calls for international coordination capable of "slowing frontier development when needed." This is the first time a major lab has stated this plainly in a formal plan document.

#### 4. Mollick: "The Twilight of the Chatbots" — Think First, Then Augment

Ethan Mollick's ["The twilight of the chatbots"](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-twilight-of-the-chatbots) (June 30) argues the era of text-box interaction is ending. Frontier models now do autonomous work — Opus 4.7 built a software package in 14 hours ($251 in tokens) worth 2-17 weeks of human engineering. Chinese open-weights models are on their own exponential curve, ~6-12 months behind closed US models.

But the post's real contribution is the practical behavior framework:
- **The Learning Brain:** AI that tutors vs. AI that gives answers. The default is answer-giving. Specialized prompts and instructor-guided use can flip it to tutoring. "It is *how you use AI* that determines whether it helps or hurts your brain."
- **The Creative Brain:** Always generate your own ideas first, THEN use AI to push them further. Once you see AI's suggestions, your own unique perspectives get crowded out ("don't think of a pink elephant").
- **The Collective Brain:** AI as meeting facilitator (devil's advocate, surfacing unheard voices) vs. AI as meeting replacement (everyone reads the transcript, no one interacts).
- **Against "Brain Damage":** "AI doesn't damage our brains, but unthinking use can damage our thinking." The fear is not AI — it's our own laziness. "Your brain is safe. Your thinking, however, is up to you."

**Superagency synthesis:** This is the practical operating manual for [[Superagency]]. The thesis has always been that AI can expand human agency if deployed intentionally. Mollick gives us the behavioral protocol: think first, write first, meet first — THEN bring in AI.

#### 5. Import AI #463: The Permanent Underclass

Jack Clark's [Import AI #463](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-463-self-improving-robots) (June 29) carries three notable threads:

- **ENPIRE (NVIDIA):** A closed-loop system where coding agents supervise physical robots that try, fail, learn, and self-improve autonomously — completing dexterous manipulation tasks with 99% success. The scaffold is the same pattern as coding agents, now applied to the physical world.
- **Humans are terrible at predicting technology:** Matthew Tokson's SSRN paper catalogs the failures: Einstein thought fission unlikely, Krugman thought the internet was no bigger than the fax machine. "History does not support complacency about the future impacts of AI."
- **Fernando Borretti's "No-One Escapes the Permanent Underclass":** An elegiac essay on disempowerment. The logic of war drives states to minimize human control. "Eventually the humans in nominal control of the AIs are a ceremonial, vestigial organ." Even if alignment works perfectly, "the machines that watch over us... are omniscient, omnipotent masters." Recommends reading for anyone who thinks the permanent underclass is a metaphor rather than a destination.

### Practical Experiment

**The Think-First Protocol (from Mollick):** For one significant task today — an email you'd normally draft with AI help, a decision you'd workshop with Claude, a meeting you'd summarize — do the thinking yourself first. Write the full draft. Make the decision. Capture your perspective. THEN bring in AI. Compare: did AI improve your output, or did it replace your thinking? Did you feel more ownership? Did the output feel like "yours" or "ours"?

If you find this protocol painful, that's the point — thinking is supposed to be work. The pain is what you're preserving.
