# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-10-15

## The Definition-of-Success Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Today's four directly checked sources ask the same question from different angles: **who decides that an AI-mediated experience is good?** A benchmark can score the wrong users; feedback can change reliance without improving team performance; a child's video app can reject watch-time optimization without yet demonstrating learning gains; and a safety critic can demand operational culture rather than promises. Five findings below come from **four sources** (two preprints, one reported article, one opinion essay). Finding 5 is a second implication from the WIRED story, not a fifth study. Web search returned no results on two queries; publisher feeds, directly accessible articles and arXiv RSS abstracts supplied evidence. None is a Texas K–12 outcome trial.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[AI Use Case Evaluation Rubric]]: a score is not agency-expanding when its definition of success excludes the intended user's language, context or judgment.
- [[AI Tutors]]: feedback should cultivate a learner's capacity to challenge advice, not merely reward agreement. This is a proposed transfer from non-education research, not an established classroom finding.
- [[Public Trust and AI]]: trust needs inspectable criteria, bounded sessions and a credible stop/review path; marketing and criticism alike require verification.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Exact-match metrics are useful for reproducibility; the paper tests their brittleness on particular equivalent answers, not a case for abandoning standardized assessment.
- Balanced feedback may preserve reflection in one decision task, but could be confusing or burdensome for young learners; test with educators and measure unaided learning.
- A paid, curated child-media service may be more accessible to some families and less to others. No ads and no autoplay do not alone establish developmental benefit; equally, criticizing algorithmic media does not mean all AI-generated content is harmful.
- Zvi's argument for a stronger safety culture is not proof that every allegation about each company is correct; evaluate independently and retain deployment-specific evidence.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. For an authorized AI pilot, write down **source user, target user, scoring rule, decision owner and stop condition** before using a benchmark to justify procurement. Never put identifiable student records in an unapproved evaluation system.
2. For a teacher-reviewed tutoring prototype, compare one-sided versus balanced feedback on publicly shareable or approved test items; record a later unaided answer and whether the learner challenged a deliberately questionable hint. Do not infer classroom impact from the N=244 adult/general decision study.
3. For child-facing content or school-recommended apps, check ad/autoplay defaults, session limit, review criteria, data retention and an accessible correction/removal path; price and access are part of the assessment.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**The metric does not own the mission.** A system earns agency gains only when the people it serves can inspect how success is defined, challenge an answer and stop an experience that fails them.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Run a 25-minute tabletop review of an existing, non-sensitive AI pilot. Ask one person to show a benchmark result, another to supply an equivalent answer from the intended user's context, and a third to identify who can reject the metric and halt deployment. Record the scoring discrepancy, review time and resulting decision. For a school workflow, use an existing publicly available, non-identifiable task to test the control; do not upload student or staff data or represent a tabletop exercise as student-outcome evidence.
