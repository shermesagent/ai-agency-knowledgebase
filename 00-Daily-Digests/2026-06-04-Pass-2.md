## 1. Executive Summary

Today's second wave of sources converges on a single structural transition: **we are moving from the "co-intelligence" era — where humans collaborated with chatbots — to the "co-existence" era — where humans work alongside AI agents that are sometimes, but not always, better than them.** This is not a subtle semantic shift. It is a recognition that the foundational assumptions of the last two years of AI adoption are being rewritten in real time.

Ethan Mollick officially retires the co-intelligence frame today, announcing his new book *Co-Existence* (October 20, 2026). The reason is data-driven: Anthropic reports that AI now writes 80% of its own code and each developer ships 8x more. A separate study found coding agents led to 17x more code being written. The co-intelligence model — where humans prompt chatbots back and forth — was never the long-term vision. The vision was self-directed agents, and they have arrived. Mollick's response is characteristically nuanced: he wrote every chapter draft himself because "AI is not a great long-form writer" and "you wanted to know what I think" — but he used AI readers, fact-checkers, and un-stickers, and he let Claude Code build his book website in minutes. The new frame: co-existence means working with AI that is *sometimes, but not always, better than you* — and the skill is knowing which is which.

On the governance side, the Trump Executive Order on AI testing returned from seeming death, officially inaugurating what Zvi Mowshowitz calls "the prior restraint era of frontier model releases." The EO asks companies to voluntarily provide models for federal testing before release and attempts to preempt state AI laws. Zvi is "tentatively happy about this on net" but flags worrisome details: too much NSA responsibility, classification of the testing process. Meanwhile, OpenAI offered a new policy blueprint that Zvi finds "remarkably good" — even as its political operations face a major scandal: the OpenAI-Andreessen-Palantir SuperPAC admitted to creating a false flag "doomer" X account that posted calls to violence. And Anthropic filed its S-1 to go public, joining Google's $84 billion raise in what Zvi describes as a capital-intense frontier.

On the practical deployment front: Utah's Doc In a Box AI medical refill system achieved 97% physician agreement on recommended refills — a deployment success that suggests AI medical triage is crossing the reliability threshold. MIT Technology Review profiles how agentic AI is "rehumanizing" global healthcare by automating administrative tasks so humans can focus on patient care, addressing the WHO-projected 11 million healthcare worker shortage. Salesforce standardized around Claude Code with no token limits. And Mollick demonstrates how AI video generation evolved from abstract fur blobs (Midjourney 2022) to photorealistic otters with AI-generated soundtracks (Veo 3, 2025) — in under three years.

The through-line: **the co-intelligence era served us well as a transition metaphor, but the transition is ending. Co-existence with sometimes-superior AI agents is the new reality, and the governance infrastructure — from prior restraint testing regimes to the norms around AI political operations — is being built right now, with consequences that will compound for decades.**

## 3. Superagency Connections

- **The Co-Intelligence → Co-Existence transition is the Superagency framework's next chapter.** Mollick's original frame — invite AI in, test it, keep humans responsible — was the right scaffolding for the chatbot era. But in the agent era, where AI writes 80% of its own code and agents can complete 50+ step professional workflows, "co-intelligence" implies a parity that no longer holds. "Co-existence" — working alongside AI that is *sometimes better than you* — is the more honest, and more agency-preserving, frame. The Superagency thesis must now answer: what does human agency look like when the AI is genuinely superior at some tasks, not just a helpful collaborator?

- **The governance infrastructure is being built RIGHT NOW, with norms that will compound.** The Trump EO's "prior restraint era," Anthropic going public (S-1 filed), OpenAI offering remarkably good policy blueprints while simultaneously running false flag political operations, $500M/month Claude spend — these are not separate stories. They are the institutional infrastructure of the AI era being built in real time, with the haphazardness and contradictions of any institutional formation. The Superagency thesis requires this infrastructure to be designed for human agency, not just model safety or corporate growth.

- **Healthcare AI is the most concrete Superagency success story emerging.** Doc In a Box achieving 97% physician agreement on refills, agentic AI automating administrative burdens so humans can focus on patients, the 11M healthcare worker shortage being addressed through augmentation rather than impossible hiring — this is Superagency in action. The pattern: AI handles the scalable, repeatable, administrative work; humans handle the relational, empathetic, judgment-intensive work. This is the template for every other domain.

- **The coding-agent revolution validates the acceleration thesis — and raises the agency question.** 80% of Anthropic's own code written by AI. 17x more code from coding agents. Salesforce standardized on Claude Code. These numbers make the acceleration case undeniable. But the agency question remains: are developers becoming more capable architects who direct AI agents (Superagency), or more passive approvers who rubber-stamp AI output (cognitive surrender)? The answer is in the design of the workflow, not the capability of the AI.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments

- **"Co-Existence" may be a premature frame for most workers.** Mollick's examples — writing a book, building a website, generating TikZ drawings — are knowledge-worker tasks where the human has deep domain expertise. For the 82% of small business owners using AI (from the morning pass), or workers in domains where AI's "sometimes better" judgment is harder to calibrate, co-existence may feel more like dependence. The frame works best for experts who can tell when the AI is better; for novices, it's a recipe for undetected errors.

- **The prior restraint EO may be more theater than substance.** Voluntary pre-release testing with no enforcement mechanism, NSA classification of the process, and an explicit goal of preempting stronger state laws — critics argue this could *weaken* AI governance by replacing binding regulations with voluntary ones. The fact that the EO "returned from being seemingly dead" suggests political fragility. Zvi's "tentatively happy" is the right posture: hopeful but watching carefully.

- **The OpenAI PAC false flag scandal is a warning about AI governance capture.** If the companies building frontier AI are willing to run false flag operations to shape the political environment in which they're regulated, the governance infrastructure being built now — prior restraint testing, policy blueprints, voluntary frameworks — may be shaped by actors whose political ethics are fundamentally misaligned with democratic accountability. The scandal is not about AI capability; it's about AI governance legitimacy.

- **Healthcare AI's 97% physician agreement is impressive but limited.** The Doc In a Box system is explicitly designed to be overly cautious — it escalates to physicians in 28% of cases, and physicians found 31% of those escalations unnecessary. This is the right design for safety, but it means the system hasn't yet demonstrated cost savings or throughput improvements at scale. The "rehumanizing" framing is compelling but needs deployment data before we can confirm AI is genuinely making healthcare more human rather than just adding another layer of technology.

## 5. Practical Applications

- **For writers and creators:** Mollick's book-writing process is a template for agency-preserving AI use. Write your own drafts (AI is not a great long-form writer). Use AI as readers, fact-checkers, and un-stickers. Let AI build the infrastructure (websites, formatting) but keep your voice and judgment. The em-dash test — deliberately using fewer to prove text is human — is a surprisingly practical authenticity signal.

- **For developers and engineering leaders:** The 80% self-written code figure from Anthropic is not a target to chase but a design problem to solve. The question is not "can we get to 80%?" but "when AI writes 80% of our code, who holds the architectural vision, who reviews for correctness, and who is accountable when something breaks?" The Salesforce model — standardized on Claude Code, no token limits — is the deployment pattern. The Nolan Lawson model — multiple independent AI reviewers with near-zero false positive rate — is the quality pattern.

- **For healthcare administrators:** Doc In a Box provides a deployment template: design for overcaution (false positives are cheap, false negatives are expensive), track physician agreement rates as your primary metric, and accept that 31% of escalations being "unnecessary" is actually a sign of good safety design. The MIT TR framing — "rehumanizing" — is the right value proposition: sell AI as the thing that lets humans be more human.

- **For AI governance watchers:** Watch three things in the coming weeks. (1) Whether the Trump EO's voluntary framework acquires teeth (mandatory reporting, consequences for non-participation) or remains voluntary theater. (2) Whether the OpenAI false flag scandal produces institutional consequences (firings, policy changes, external investigation) or gets memory-holed. (3) How the Anthropic IPO prospectus frames safety commitments to shareholders — the S-1 is the most important AI governance document of the quarter.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try

**Run the "Co-Existence Audit" on one work domain.** Pick a domain where you have genuine expertise — something you've done for years and can evaluate quality in. For one week, track every AI interaction in that domain. For each one, answer three questions: (1) Was the AI better than me at this specific task, or was I better? (2) If the AI was better, did I learn from it (co-existence as amplification) or just accept the output (co-existence as surrender)? (3) Could I reproduce the quality of the final output without the AI — and if not, am I becoming more capable (the AI is a teacher) or more dependent (the AI is a crutch)?

The Co-Existence frame is not about whether AI is good or bad. It's about knowing — specifically, for each task, in each domain — whether the AI is better than you, and acting accordingly. The audit forces that calibration. Mollick wrote every chapter draft himself because he knew AI is not a great long-form writer. He let AI build his website because he knew AI is excellent at that. The skill is knowing which is which.
