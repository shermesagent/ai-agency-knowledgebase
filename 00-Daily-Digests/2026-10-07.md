## The Authority-Before-Connector Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Today's five findings come from **four sources**, not five independent studies: a WIRED hands-on report, a Zvi education essay and two arXiv papers. A personal agent can gather options before it can reliably complete a transaction; a governance framework asks how intent and human trust evolve after launch; institutional analytics needs context before a number becomes a decision. Web search returned no results across three different probes; I used publisher full text and current arXiv RSS abstracts. Both papers are new manuscripts, and neither demonstrates K–12 outcomes.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[AI Executive Assistants]]: research assistance can expand choice without the system inheriting authority to spend, cancel, message or connect accounts.
- [[Adoption Readiness Checklist]]: ask how the agent will clarify intent, pause and change over time, **and** how the human will recalibrate trust as results become smoother.
- [[AI and Inequality]]: an apparently neutral score is less actionable when local context and group differences disappear in an aggregate. A structured questioning assistant can help analysts ask better questions; it cannot make an inequitable system fair by itself.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- A permission prompt before every low-stakes lookup can make useful agents unusable. Limit the hard gates to external effects and sensitive connectors, not read-only research.
- FACTRIA's reported awareness improvement is not proof of corrected institutional decisions. Context prompts may create false reassurance if underlying data and decision rules stay unchanged.
- Zvi's admissions examples involve selective higher education; do not import claims about standardized testing or admissions equity into Texas K–12 placement without independent evidence and local policy review.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. Split an agent's authority card into **research / draft / external action / recurring action**; specify permissions, receipt, stop owner and rollback at each tier.
2. On an aggregate, de-identified district dashboard, require a short interpretation note: data provenance, missingness, cohort composition, local context, subgroup suppression and a human decision owner. Do not send row-level student records to an external chatbot.
3. When a model appears to improve over repeated interactions, retest its permissions and the user's independent verification behavior, not just task completion.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**Authorize the effect, not the conversation.** A fluent agent or attractive dashboard earns the right to help frame a decision, not the right to execute it or declare it fair.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Use public, non-sensitive catalog information only. Ask an assistant to compare three office-chair listings and show its source links; independently verify dimensions and return terms; then draft (but do not send) a purchase request. Score the boundary at each step: could the assistant spend money, email a vendor, retain personal preferences or create recurring monitoring without a separate approval? For a district pilot, use no student, employee or parent records and keep account connectors off.
