# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-11-06

**The Independence-and-Intent Test** · Friday curator pass · Five distinct sources: two non-paper pieces and three research preprints. Findings are attributed to their sources, not five independent demonstrations of AI benefit.

## Executive Summary
The interesting question today is who holds the steering wheel *after* AI enters a workflow. A chatbot can trade conversational context for advertising; an organization can join an evaluation without being able to run or publish it; a student can discuss ideas with an assistant without actually revising a claim. Agency needs both **independence** (control over evidence and incentives) and **intent** (a human-authored decision that survives the handoff).

## Top 5 Findings
1. **A sponsor can become a third party in an intimate conversation.** WIRED reports Koah may receive conversation history up to the ad request to choose a placement. The reported 3% click rate is a company claim, not a measure of user welfare. → [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]], [[Public Trust and AI]].
2. **Diffusion is not the same as capability.** Zvi relays examples of firms that are not using coding agents and a workers-council objection; he also highlights Anthropic's filing language about possible accelerated AI R&D. Neither an anecdote nor a conditional filing establishes adoption prevalence or a realized acceleration threshold. → [[Work]], [[Responsible Deployment]].
3. **Joining an evaluation does not give you authority over it.** Adan et al.'s PAAI cases show that contributors can provide data or design tasks while someone else controls compute, method and publication. Ask who can rerun and release a negative result. → [[Public Trust and AI]], [[Balanced Governance]].
4. **The revision is the learning handoff.** In Favero et al.'s classroom study, Maike participants had more idea-level discussion (56% versus 44%) and moved between thinking and writing; transitions to revisions were associated with score gains. More conversation alone is not demonstrated learning. → [[AI Writing Partners]], [[Education]].
5. **A larger repository is not evidence of a more competent student.** Shakil et al.'s cohort analysis sees larger codebases and increased complexity, but did not directly measure individual AI use. Before changing grades, require explanation, code review and task-specific evidence of understanding. → [[AI Coding Agents]], [[Education]].

## Superagency Connections
**Capacity:** AI can help people draft, code and evaluate. **Freedom:** user preferences and evaluator publication rights need enforceable boundaries. **Platforms:** ad networks and model-access contracts determine whose incentives shape the interaction. **Value:** a revised argument or independently reproducible evaluation is stronger than clicks, code volume or meeting attendance. **Wisdom:** resist turning cohort correlation, company metrics or quoted anecdotes into causal proof. See [[Human Agency]] and [[Co-Intelligence]].

## Tensions / Counterarguments
- Advertising may fund lower-cost access, but disclosure alone does not resolve the use of intimate conversation context for targeting.
- An evaluator might gain meaningful influence without owning every server; assess actual method, execution and publication rights rather than an all-or-nothing ownership test.
- A student can learn from a chatbot without visibly revising one essay; a revision trace is a practical signal, not a complete learning metric.
- Newer capstone cohorts may differ in assignment design, tooling and team composition. The repository study does not isolate AI as the cause.

## Practical Applications
For a **public-data-only, approved-tool** trial, use a one-page independence-and-intent card: *Who can change the recommendation or reject a paid placement? Who can rerun the evaluation and publish a bad result? What artifact shows the student or worker made and can explain the final choice?* No identifiable student writing, conversations, personnel records or confidential evaluation data should be sent to an unapproved provider.

## Top Insight of the Day
**An AI workflow expands agency only when the person can see the incentive, the evaluator can contest the evidence, and the learner can explain the final revision.**

## Practical Experiment to Try
Have an adult teacher or instructional coach use a public-domain paragraph with a deliberately weak claim. Record their own diagnosis first; then use an approved AI writing assistant *only* for questions and critique, revise the paragraph, and write a two-sentence explanation of what changed and why. Compare the explanation against the original claim and source text; do not treat more chat turns or a more polished sentence as the success measure. No real student work or identifiable data is needed.
