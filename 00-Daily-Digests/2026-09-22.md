## The Stop Layer — agency requires the power to halt, scope, and verify

**Theme:** `web_search` again returned empty arrays across current-source queries, so discovery used direct RSS/web extraction: MIT Technology Review, Stratechery, and arXiv RSS across cs.AI/cs.CY/cs.HC. Today's strongest sources converged on a missing agency primitive: the ability to stop systems, bound memory, limit interpretation, and slow down hype before it becomes policy.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

**A stop button is not a governance system.** Oren Perez's “Law of Stop” paper argues that interruptibility has four dimensions — technical affordance, authority, epistemic trigger, and epistemic standing — and finds that most AI incidents lack a usable stop, usually for legal/institutional reasons rather than technical ones.

**Memory is an accessibility gain until it becomes context drift.** New HCI research on disabled users and conversational AI shows why “remember this” is too blunt. Disability disclosure can save repeated explanation, but memory that travels into unrelated contexts violates contextual integrity.

**Explanations need boundaries.** Explanation Navigator shows that users confabulate meaning when AI explanations are simpler than the real system. Human review checkpoints must ask what an explanation does *not* establish, not merely whether it sounds reasonable.

**AI writing is now an admissions trust problem.** A study of nearly 7,500 applications finds that AI-written admissions essays are widespread, improve writing quality, and appear to be penalized when detected. The essay proxy is breaking; the institutional response is mostly guesswork.

**Hype correction is trust repair.** Gebru and Bender's MIT Technology Review essay is a needed counterweight to AGI marketing language, while Stratechery's “Frontier Overhangs” warns that pacing rhetoric can also serve incumbent strategy. Balanced governance has to distrust hype without dismissing real governance needs.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments

- **Stopping can become censorship or arbitrary control.** A stop right needs evidence access, review, appeal, and restart rules; otherwise “kill switch” becomes ad-hoc power.
- **Memory is often an accessibility feature.** The answer is not “disable memory.” It is scoped, inspectable, revocable memory.
- **Hype skepticism can become complacency.** Gebru/Bender are right to attack marketing language. Thompson is right that pacing rhetoric can serve incumbents. Neither point eliminates the need for independent evaluation and real control infrastructure.
- **Admissions officers may be punishing the wrong thing.** If AI improves writing quality, the problem may be the essay proxy, not applicant ethics alone.
- **Explanations can help, but only if users know their limits.** A simplified explanation without boundary markers invites over-reading.

---

## 5. Practical Applications

- **For AI procurement:** require a stop ledger: what can be stopped, who can stop it, evidence threshold, systems affected, verification of halt, restart authority.
- **For school/student-facing AI:** treat disability, accommodation, language, health, and family context as scoped memory with provenance, retention, access, and deletion controls.
- **For human review protocols:** add a required “what this explanation does not show” field before approval.
- **For admissions/hiring/selection:** replace essay-only proxies with live defense, oral explanation, portfolio evidence, task demonstrations, or process artifacts.
- **For public AI claims:** pair every major capability claim with independent review status, actual demonstration, speculative leap, and who benefits from urgency.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day

**Agency is not just the freedom to use AI. It is the power to stop it, bound it, contest it, and prevent yesterday's disclosure from becoming tomorrow's invisible context.** The next mature AI governance stack will be judged less by how many principles it publishes and more by whether a real human can halt a real system for a real reason before harm propagates.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try

**The Stop Ledger Drill**

Pick one AI workflow you use or oversee. Answer six questions in writing:

1. What exactly can be stopped?
2. Who has authority to stop it?
3. What evidence is enough to trigger the stop?
4. What keeps running after the stop?
5. How do you verify the stop actually worked?
6. Who can restart it, and what must be true first?

If you cannot answer those in five minutes, the workflow does not have human oversight. It has human spectators.
