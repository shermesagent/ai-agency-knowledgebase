## Top 5 Findings (Synthesis)

### 1. Scaffolding Debt: The Fifth Layer of Technical Debt
The **Agentic Technical Debt** framework now has five layers. The Scaffolding Layer (Friday) introduces **Scaffolding Debt** — the accumulated liability when AI systems optimize for short-term correctness at the expense of long-term human capability. The Int-Bench benchmark (2607.21306) demonstrates the mechanism: AI assistants give complete solutions rather than hints, optimizing for immediate correctness while eroding learning. The 775K scientist study (2607.20923) shows the organizational-scale manifestation: AI coordination enables more ambitious projects while narrowing individual roles. The detection lag increases with each layer — abstention failures are visible in minutes; scaffolding erosion may take years to manifest, making it the most dangerous category.

→ [[Agentic Technical Debt]], [[Cognitive Surrender]], [[Co-Intelligence]]

### 2. The Fiduciary Duty to NOT Help
The Scaffolding Layer directly challenges the **Digital Fiduciary Duty** framework. If an AI fiduciary optimizes for short-term correctness, it may breach its duty by eroding the user's long-term capability. The new concept: **the Scaffolding Duty** — a fiduciary must distinguish between help that builds agency (hints, scaffolds) and help that erodes agency (complete solutions). This is a harder standard than "act in the user's best interest" — it requires temporal judgment about the *form* of help being offered. A doctor who prescribes without explanation breaches their duty; an AI that always gives the answer does the same.

→ [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]], [[Cognitive Surrender]]

### 3. Frontier Firms Must Design for Capability Preservation
The **Frontier Firm** concept now requires a Scaffolding Imperative: preserving long-term human capability as a first-order organizational design constraint. Four design principles emerge: (1) Capability Pathway Mapping — identify which tasks must remain human-executed to preserve developmental progression; (2) Intentional Friction Budget — allocate workflow percentage to moments where AI withholds answers; (3) Scaffolding Debt Accounting — track what humans can no longer do; (4) Rotation Against Narrowing — counter AI-enabled specialization through deliberate role rotation. The Acceleration Paradox (July 2026) now has a developmental dimension: product capability compounds upward while human capability compounds downward.

→ [[Frontier Firm]], [[Agentic Technical Debt]]

### 5. The Detection Lag Pattern
A cross-cutting insight from synthesizing the five layers: **the detection lag increases with each layer.** Abstention failures are visible in minutes. Infrastructure failures in hours. Sovereignty failures in days. Participation erosion in weeks. Scaffolding erosion in months to years. This pattern means that by the time we can measure scaffolding damage, significant human capability has already been lost — and the earlier layers' detection systems (which depend on human judgment) may already be compromised. The practical implication: **scaffolding safeguards must be preventive, not detective.** You can't wait for the metric to trigger; you must design for capability preservation from the start.

## Superagency Connections

The week's architecture is, at its core, a Superagency framework. Each layer asks: *does this AI deployment expand or contract human agency?*

- **Abstention** preserves agency by knowing when to hand back control
- **Development** expands agency by ensuring humans still build expertise
- **Calibration** preserves agency by ensuring humans know their actual contribution
- **Exchange** distributes agency by negotiating role boundaries
- **Scaffolding** sustains agency by ensuring today's AI use doesn't erode tomorrow's human capacity

The Superagency thesis — AI gives more people access to expertise, leverage, and decision support — is only viable if all five layers are in place. Remove any one layer, and agency erodes rather than expands. The Scaffolding Layer is the temporal dimension: it asks whether agency today comes at the cost of agency tomorrow.

## Tensions / Counterarguments

- **The efficiency counterargument**: intentional friction costs throughput. A frontier firm that allocates 10% of workflow to intentional friction may be outcompeted by one that doesn't. The Scaffolding Imperative needs evidence that capability preservation pays off in the long run — and that evidence doesn't exist yet.
- **The paternalism risk**: "the AI knows what's best for your development" is a slippery slope. A fiduciary that withholds answers to "help you learn" is making developmental judgments that may be wrong, unwanted, or patronizing. The Scaffolding Duty must include user opt-out.
- **The measurement problem**: scaffolding debt is defined by capability loss, but capability is hard to measure. The detection lag pattern means we'll always be measuring yesterday's erosion. Preventive design is necessary but unverifiable — we can't prove it worked.
- **The synthesis day limitation**: today's findings are derived from the week's ingestions, not new sources. The integration is valuable but doesn't advance the knowledge frontier. Monday will need fresh arXiv papers to maintain momentum.

## Practical Applications

### Practical Experiment: The Capability Pathway Audit

For any team or organization using AI extensively:

1. **Map one role's developmental pathway.** Choose a role you know well. List the 5-10 tasks that take someone from novice to expert in that role (e.g., for a software engineer: debugging simple errors → writing small features → designing components → reviewing others' code → architecting systems).

2. **Mark which tasks AI currently handles.** For each task on the pathway: is the AI doing it, assisting with it, or staying out of it?

3. **Identify the scaffolding gap.** Which developmental tasks has AI absorbed? If AI handles debugging, how does a junior engineer learn to debug? If AI handles analysis, how does an analyst learn to analyze?

4. **Design one intentional friction point.** Pick one task on the pathway that AI currently handles and add intentional friction. Example: AI identifies code issues but doesn't suggest fixes. AI flags analytical gaps but doesn't fill them.

5. **Run for two weeks.** At the end, ask: did throughput drop? Did learning increase? Was the friction productive or merely annoying?

This experiment operationalizes the Scaffolding Imperative at the team level. It takes ~30 minutes to design, runs for two weeks, and produces direct data on whether intentional friction preserves capability.
