## 1. Executive Summary
Three directly read, newly published pieces ask a common question: who actually controls the inputs and institutions on which useful AI depends? MIT Technology Review distinguishes downloadable model weights from affordable, participatory access; its separate reporting follows workplace archives into a new market after startups close. A WIRED book excerpt describes volunteers resisting a shift from building Wikipedia to cleaning up AI-generated text. **Top 5 findings below are drawn from three sources, not five independent studies.** Web search returned empty on multiple queries, so publication RSS discovery and full publisher text supplied the evidence. ## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[AI Enclosure]]: ownership of weights only expands agency when people can run, inspect and shape the system and participate fairly in improvements.
- [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]]: entrusted context must remain governable when the firm that held it fails. A corporate asset sale is not the end of the human relationship.
- [[The Cognitive Commons]]: a faster answer can deplete the human community that maintains the source knowledge. The valuable institution is not only its output but its people and process.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Local open models may reduce dependence on a single provider and protect some sensitive processing, but released weights are hard to recall and remove provider-side abuse controls. Closed access is not automatically safe either.
- Liquidating lawful company assets may pay creditors; that economic interest does not settle worker, customer, contractual, privacy or third-party rights. The article documents a market, not a blanket legal conclusion.
- Restricting AI-authored encyclopedia text can add moderation work and close off some helpful uses; an institution should distinguish source verification, drafting and bounded proofreading rather than ban every assistive workflow.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. Before choosing a model for a district or civic project, record **weights access / actual runtime affordability / data provenance / permitted use / release risk / fallback provider** as distinct fields. Do not assume local inference is affordable, private or approved for student records just because weights are downloadable.
2. Before a vendor or nonprofit shutdown, require a data disposition review: source and purpose, employee/customer/third-party content, transfer authority, buyer, training scope, retention, deletion and challenge route. Keep student and personnel data out of exploratory AI-data transactions.
3. For a public knowledge project, compare a small human-authored baseline with an AI-assisted proofreading workflow on verified citations, editor cleanup minutes and willing human contributors; do not submit AI-written articles to Wikipedia contrary to its current rules.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**The agency test follows the asset after the handoff.** A model file, a workplace archive and an encyclopedia page all look reusable. What matters is whether the humans who built, supplied or maintain them retain meaningful authority over access, reuse and correction.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Using **only public, non-sensitive materials**, take one proposed AI-enabled knowledge workflow and draw three columns: *who contributed the input*, *who can change the reuse rule*, *who bears correction costs*. Assign a named owner and an evidence check to each column. If contributors cannot contest reuse or maintainers cannot reject bad output, change the workflow before scaling it. No student, employee or customer records in this exercise.
