## 1. Executive Summary
Four directly inspected pieces—three reported/analytical articles from WIRED (one originally published by Quanta) and a Stratechery conversation—show four different limits on the promise of AI: the bystander cannot choose a wearable's data policy; a private tool still needs a viable legal privacy environment; genomic prediction is not biological understanding; and faster production does not settle who controls creative work. **Top five findings below come from four sources:** #5 is a second, separate implication of the HTC report. `web_search` returned empty on two distinct searches; publisher RSS and full article text supplied the evidence. These are reports and expert views, not controlled demonstrations of improved human outcomes.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Government and Civic Life]]: protective design must be legible and actionable to the *person being recorded*, not just the purchaser. Privacy controls should be tested with the bystander, including in protest or school settings.
- [[Home Server AI Agents]]: local storage limits one exposure path; messenger encryption and metadata minimization require a legal and service environment that permits them. Local control is not universal protection.
- [[Healthcare]]: use genomic models to prioritize questions to investigate, not to infer disease mechanisms or make clinical decisions from sequence alone. Context, validation and clinician judgment remain central.
- [[AI and Creator Rights]]: AI may reduce production cost while making rights, bargaining and distribution more consequential. More output is not automatically more creator agency.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- A visible light and voice stop can improve practical consent, but may fail amid noise, disability barriers, delayed discovery or downstream reposting. A direct test beats a product slogan.
- Investigators have legitimate needs, but forced retention or exceptional access creates new risks for ordinary users and potential misuse; the exact scope of any Canadian requirement remains contested.
- Sequence-based AI predictions can be scientifically useful even before mechanisms are understood. Mechanistic uncertainty is a reason for targeted experiments, not a reason to reject computational biology.
- Talent intermediaries may preserve human negotiating leverage, but incumbents may also defend their own rents against independent creators. Examine the creator's actual contracts and options.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. For any camera/voice AI device in a public-facing pilot, test physical recording indication, an accessible stop path, local/cloud transfer, retention and third-party deletion with someone *not* wearing the device. No student, patient, employee or bystander recording without approved consent and data terms.
2. For private agents, diagram data destinations and metadata before claiming a workflow is local or end-to-end private. Keep sensitive content out of unapproved remote connectors.
3. For a genomics or creative-production AI pitch, ask two different questions: **what does the model predict or produce?** and **who can validate, control and benefit from its result?**

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**A safeguard is only agency-preserving when the affected person can exercise it at the real boundary:** the bystander at capture, the user at data transfer, the clinician at inference, the creator at a deal.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Run a 20-minute *paper-only* boundary audit of a proposed AI workflow. Draw four boxes—capture, transfer, inference and release—and name for each: the affected person, data destination, evidence needed and effective stop or appeal. Have a second reviewer challenge one boundary using public material only. Record the first place a safeguard exists in policy but cannot be used in practice. Do not upload identifiable records or record bystanders for this experiment.
