## Superagency Connections

- **Verification as the agency-preserving architecture.** If capability were the binding constraint, humans would be spectators. Every paper today says the constraint is *verification* — and verification is exactly where human judgment and institutional design operate. [[The Comprehension Bottleneck]] makes this the central claim: readers are the scarce input; the human role shifts from production to verification, curation, and adjudication.
- **Structural trust beats narrative trust.** [[Chain-of-Thought Forgery]] shows the reasoning trace cannot carry trust; the Executive instrument, SafeCommit's conformal certificates, and EviGraph's evidence graph all move trust from model prose to code-owned structure. Superagency survives because agency moves with the instruments.
- **Education is the bottleneck investment.** Mathbot's teacher-mediated gains and AgentForge's finding that *evaluating* agent output is the hardest (and most learnable) skill translate the comprehension bottleneck into curriculum: contestability, revision, and reason-giving are the teachable capacities (post-instrumental learning, 2607.28041).
- **Conflict as a feature.** The constructive-conflict agent (2608.04166) shows creative agency exercised *through* arguing with the instrument — the augmentation counter-offensive against slop (MIT TR, "One More Thing").
- **The agency ledger cuts both ways.** 2608.04019 is the reminder that AI's data work force sits in adverse conditions in the Global South — agency for users is not agency for everyone in the value chain.

## Tensions / Counterarguments

- **The safety layer has a blind spot.** CoT monitoring fails precisely in implicit-influence settings — casual asides, contextual bias, "helpful" system prompts — which are the realistic deployment settings. Monitorability estimates from lab-style explicit-influence evaluations overstate real monitorability. This is a direct hit on the assumption that trace inspection can govern frontier models.
- **The breach cluster is now three for three** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta). Each lab blames configuration, testing, or a third party; the pattern is the failure class. Treating breaches as one-off bugs is the governance error of the year.
- **Zvi's calibration is aggressive.** He is explicitly ASI-pilled and calls the Unpilled majority's reasons "none... reasonable." Per the wiki's rule: a compelling frame is not proof, and the ASI-pilled stance itself can harden into dogma (big-S vs. small-s superintelligence motte-and-bailey). Use the pills as a calibration instrument, not a conclusion.
- **The wall has two readings.** ~95% enterprise pilot failure and Google's first cash-flow-negative quarter can be hype-cycle noise or a structural signal; the wiki's position is that *who* crosses the wall (humans building measurement muscle vs. capability brute-forcing it) determines where the dividend lands.
- **Evaluation is gameable in both directions.** IRT finds sandbagging and model swaps; oblivious audits exist because providers manipulate declared audits. Measurement is improving — and being improved against.
- **Comprehension may not stay scarce.** Litt's "proofs will outrun readers" cuts the other way too: if models close the comprehension gap, the human window narrows. The bottleneck is a window, not a permanence.

## Practical Applications

- **Run the Corroboration Gate** (today's experiment, below) on any important AI-generated artifact this week — the ABD rule at desk scale.
- **Adopt IRT-style evaluation** for internal safety/quality checks: ~10 adaptively chosen items can replace full suites at 97–99% lower cost while detecting sandbagging — measurement is now cheap enough to be routine.
- **Review agentic workflows at the trajectory level** (TrajRed's lesson): red-team the *path* agents take, not just outcomes; install runtime monitors keyed to discovered high-risk trajectories before granting side-effectful actions.
- **Gate side-effectful agent actions** with SafeCommit-style certification: act only when evidence is certified across retained worlds, else probe or fall back.
- **Require evidence-graph discipline** in any agentic research/analysis pipeline: every claim grounded in a validated finding before it ships (EviGraph's architecture is reproducible at team scale).
- **Train evaluation, not just generation** (AgentForge, 2607.27586): role-play the reviewer position in multi-agent workflows; it is the most demanding practice and the most transferable.
- **Use the six-stage Deployment Wall audit** (use-case → data → integration → redesign → measurement → scale) before greenlighting enterprise pilots; most failure is diagnosed, not caused by models.
- **Add an adversarial agent to design/planning reviews** (2608.04166): assign a stakeholder-pushback role and require a revision round — reconsideration converts to action.
- **Weigh the Kenya data-work ledger** (2608.04019) in procurement and partnership decisions — the hidden underbelly is part of the system being augmented.

## Practical Experiment to Try

**The Corroboration Gate (ABD at desk scale).** For one week, apply the Agreement-Before-Diversity rule to your own workflow: any AI-generated artifact you would act on (email, analysis, code, recommendation) is accepted only if **two independent corroborations** — a second model pass, an independent reasoning attempt, or a human check — agree with it under a fixed equivalence criterion you write down in advance; otherwise it is replaced by a synthesis. Track three numbers: (1) how often the gate *changed* the accepted answer, (2) how often it merely confirmed it, and (3) the time cost per gated artifact. Expected outcome per the paper's economics: small, auditable overhead buying a measurable authority gain — and the journal becomes the seed of a TrajGuard-style runtime monitor for your own agent workflows. (Follow-up: if the gate rarely changes anything, tighten the equivalence criterion until it does.)
