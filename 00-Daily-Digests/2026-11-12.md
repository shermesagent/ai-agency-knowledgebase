# Daily AI Agency Digest — November 12, 2026

> **Bottom line:** Promising AI tools become more useful when their evidence trails, human review and evaluation baselines are designed in from the start. All five sources below are arXiv preprints submitted November 10; treat their findings as early evidence, not deployment approval. Today’s outlet searches did not yield a comparably verifiable, uncovered item from the preferred newsletters or publications.

## Top 5 findings

1. **Instructional design ≠ instructional impact.** [Perspicacious](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13549) reports a 17-run cost model and a formative usability study with eight instructional designers, partly Wizard-of-Oz. That is evidence about an authoring workflow, **not** student learning or Texas TEKS alignment. A school pilot should verify standards, accessibility and accuracy with teachers before considering outcomes. [[AI for School Districts]] · [[AI Tutors]]
2. **Agent memory needs receipts.** [Evidence-linked memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14320) connects concise lessons to rationales and original trajectories and reports benefits on five agent benchmarks. This suggests an audit-friendly alternative to opaque accumulated instructions; benchmark gains do not establish field reliability. [[Agentic Technical Debt]] · [[Agentic Verification]]
3. **Practice a hard conversation without handing the bot authority.** [Lilo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14260) used simulated group chats with 28 children aged 9–11; self-reported coping self-efficacy improved after one intervention. That is not observed long-term behavior change. Parent consent, child safeguarding, teacher oversight and strict data controls are prerequisites, not afterthoughts. [[AI Tutors]] · [[AI for School Districts]]
4. **Check what an AI research assistant cites, not just what it concludes.** An [audit of US/China social-science essays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14288) across five models found differences in cited sources and some question-specific favoritism, but no broad model-origin political bias in the authors’ analysis. This bounded result is neither a neutrality certificate nor proof of systematic home-country bias. [[Public Trust and AI]] · [[Government and Civic Life]]
5. **The judge can move while the models stay fixed.** In [1,260,967 blind Chatbot Arena votes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14333) from May 2023–August 2024 on fixed pairs, the both-bad share rose 1.71 percentage points per month while relative preference for the stronger model stayed flat. The authors interpret this as rising absolute standards; Arena ratings are not school-quality evidence. Maintain both fixed-case pass rates and reviewer calibration. [[AI Use Case Evaluation Rubric]] · [[AI Tutor Evaluation Checklist]]

## Practical experiment — a 30-minute evidence-trail check

Use **three existing, non-student, approved work products** from a curriculum or operations workflow (no student records or proprietary uploads). Ask the AI to make a narrow improvement and provide the source passage or original task evidence supporting each substantive change. Have a human reviewer classify each change as *verified / unsupported / needs subject-matter review* against the original material, then separately score usefulness. Record model version, prompt, reviewer, rubric version and date. **Stop condition:** any invented citation, unauthorized data use, or standards claim without a verified TEKS citation. Compare this fixed set on the next run rather than relying on a vague impression of improvement. [[Agentic Verification]] · [[Human Review Checkpoints]]
