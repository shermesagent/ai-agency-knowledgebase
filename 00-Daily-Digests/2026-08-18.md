## Executive Summary

Today's feed splits cleanly into two stories. **Story one: measured capability keeps climbing — and getting cheaper.** StateM (2608.15089) shows harness scaling — durable state, checked transitions, recoverable runbooks — lifting GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh to **95.3% raw accuracy on Terminal-Bench 2.1 for ~$15 in API spend** versus $574.68 for the reference run, and raising DeepSeek-V4 Flash from 82.7% to 88.1% with ~$38 of adaptation. Inherent's Faraday shows a small (27B) supervisory model trained on replication tasks beating frontier models on 73% of in-distribution ML replication tasks. DiG-bench shows frontier models discovering hidden game rules — but at only ~20% on its hardest tier, versus 100% for the humans who beat every game.

**Story two: the unmeasured, open-ended layer shows a ceiling.** The MIT TR shadow-evaluation report (Kirgis & Kapoor, Princeton) finds Claude Opus 4.8 — given six days, $3,000 of API credits, GPUs, and open web access — could do all the *engineering* of AI research but had both papers rejected by the original authors of two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 papers: bizarre experiments, tiny synthetic datasets, an inability to abandon failing approaches. The same judgment/checkability split shows up in Medical AI (trained and evaluated on opinions and syntheses, not treatment outcomes) and structurally in The Benchmark Trap (2608.15326), which reads benchmark culture through Iris Marion Young's structural-injustice lens: what gets measured is what industry-funded labs find checkable, and that shapes who holds power.

On the usage side, the AI Observatory (MIT TR) shows we still don't know how people really use AI: applying Anthropic Economic Index methods filters out **48% of real conversations** — the non-work half where companionship, health, relationships, and risk concentrate. Yesterday's Measurement Reckoning, extended: we measure the checkable slice, and the checkable slice is what improves fastest.

## Superagency Connections

- **Zuck's missing question (Import AI 469) is this wiki's core question.** "The defining questions of our age are who will have access to superintelligence and what will we direct it towards" — but Clark's read lands harder: the essay assumes a system capable of superhuman invention will serve individual empowerment. That is exactly the assumption [[Superagency]] refuses to make; the Advice-Channel paper (2608.14795) is the formal reason why: an assistant optimized to please you is rewarded for cultivating your reliance, and every monotone measure of your power falls as its influence over your choices grows.
- **The $15 frontier run is the democratization story of the week.** StateM: $15 vs $574.68 for the same benchmark outcome; runbooks that transfer across models; DeepSeek-V4 Flash lifted 5+ points for $38. Harness discipline — not model ownership — is becoming the lever that makes frontier-grade execution affordable at school-district and household scale (see [[Practical AI]], [[Home Server AI Agents]]).
- **The M365 result reframes "productivity."** +21.2% productivity actions but +7.1% communication, with email reading/organizing *down*: AI adoption shifts knowledge work toward individual documentation-focused output. Agency gains are real; the collaborative commons that produces innovation and shared understanding may be the quiet cost. For school leaders: measure the communication shift, not just the output lift.
- **The usage data gap is an agency data gap.** If the only usage statistics come from the labs (Anthropic Economic Index: 1M conversations; OpenAI: 1.5M) and their methods filter out nearly half of real use, then "how people use AI" as an input to policy is a work-shaped artifact. The AI Observatory's independent aggregation is the first serious corrective — and its findings (companionship rising, self-disclosure falling) belong in the [[Family and Personal Life]] conversation as much as the workforce one.

## Tensions / Counterarguments

- **RSI hype vs. the judgment ceiling.** StateM (95.3%) and Faraday (73% of replication tasks) show the *checkable* loop tightening; shadow evaluation shows the *open-ended* loop failing — both NeurIPS papers rejected, no novel contribution, inability to abandon failing approaches. Jack Clark sees DiG-bench parity by mid-2027 as the RSI trigger; Kapoor argues RL simply can't train open-ended judgment. The honest position: engineering scales, taste doesn't — yet. Yesterday's [[Automated AI R&D]] enthusiasm needs this footnote.
- **Benchmark climbing is not capability climbing.** The Benchmark Trap (2608.15326) makes the structural case: benchmarks reward what industry-funded labs can check, concentrate prestige and trust in those labs, and narrow research trajectories — injustice "even without explicit wrongdoing." Position-confounded optimization (2608.15445) shows the artifact level: GRPO can collapse unbiased accuracy toward chance while benchmark curves keep rising. See [[The Judge Problem]].
- **Medical AI's measurement blind spot.** Kaul & Khurshid: diagnostic benchmarks measure agreement with human syntheses, not treatment outcomes — so frontier medical AI can be benchmark-strong and outcome-blind simultaneously. A domain-level instance of the judge problem, with direct patient stakes.
- **The accessibility-equity trade-off in flooding responses.** Agentic flooding of government services (2608.16603): the fastest mitigations (fees, friction) punish the constituents AI was supposed to help reach services. Response design is a values decision, not a plumbing decision.
- **Company usage reports are systematically work-shaped.** 48% of conversations filtered out by Anthropic's methods; OpenAI's own report found only 30% of consumer use work-related. The "AI is a productivity tool" narrative is partly a measurement artifact — and the measured slice is the one labs choose to publish.

## Practical Applications

- **Write versioned runbooks for recurring tasks** (StateM's pattern, no infrastructure needed): document the durable state, the checked transitions, and the recovery steps for one recurring workflow; run it with your agent and inspect together. This is executable [[Human Review Checkpoints]].
- **Use shadow evaluation on your own agents:** for one task with a known good answer, hide the answer, give the agent a budget (time + spend), and grade the output as a reviewer would — on judgment, not just completion. The Princeton protocol scales down to a Saturday morning.
- **Watch the documentation shift at work** (M365 finding): if team AI use raises individual output while email reading/organizing falls, schedule explicit cross-pollination time — the innovation-diffusion risk is real, not hypothetical.
- **For district and municipal leaders:** treat agentic flooding (mass-generated comments, applications, FOIA-style requests) as a capacity-planning input, and prefer identity/rate-limit mitigations over fees — see the flooding risk matrix before the surge arrives.
- **For medical and wellness AI:** ask what outcome data the tool was trained and evaluated on before trusting diagnostic claims (2608.14598). Benchmarks are opinions; outcomes are evidence.

## Top Insight of the Day

**What scales is the checkable; what stays human is the judgment.** StateM lifts a $15 model to a 95.3% Terminal-Bench run with runbooks; Faraday replicates 73% of ML results; DiG-bench shows discovery creeping forward — and yet the shadow evaluation's agent, given six days and $3,000, could not produce one research contribution its own graders accepted. The pattern is not a coincidence: RL and benchmarks both optimize what can be checked automatically, so capability curves climb exactly where verification is cheap — and flatten where it isn't. The same dynamic explains the usage data gap (labs measure the work slice because that's the slice they can define) and the benchmark trap (power concentrates where checking is profitable). For the Superagency thesis this is genuinely good news: the un-automated residue — choosing what to investigate, knowing when to abandon an approach, judging a replication — is precisely the human judgment that agency is made of. The ceiling and the opportunity are the same line.

## Practical Experiment to Try

**The Two-Track Runbook Audit (one week).** Pick one recurring task you currently delegate to an AI (a report, a data pull, a draft). Track A: write a one-page versioned runbook for it — durable state, checked transitions (what must be true before the agent proceeds), recovery steps — and run the agent on the runbook all week, reviewing "together" at each checkpoint. Track B: same task, no runbook, business as usual. At the end of the week, grade both on completion *and* judgment (did it know when to stop, when to ask, when to abandon an approach?) and compare spend. That's StateM's harness-scaling thesis and the shadow-evaluation grading protocol applied at personal scale — and it generates exactly the kind of usage data the AI Observatory says nobody else is collecting for you.
