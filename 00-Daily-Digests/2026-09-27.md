## The Conditional-Action Test

## 1. Executive Summary
An agent can save you money on one task and lose it on the next. A live transcript can make delegation visible without making the outcome correct. A retailer can block an agent at the platform boundary, while a data center can deliver local economic benefits yet impose wider emissions costs. Five publisher-verified non-paper sources (one new Sunday report plus four recent, previously unindexed perspectives) point to a single design rule: **record the user's conditions, the external party's permissions, and the public costs before calling an action agency-expanding**. `web_search` returned empty on independent queries; direct publisher feeds and articles supplied the sources. ## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]] and [[AI Executive Assistants]]: successful delegation requires preserving *conditions* across tool calls, not just reporting that a task was completed. The refund condition is the test.
- [[AI Enclosure]]: users may authorize an agent, but platforms can refuse it. Access to a model is not access to every service or transaction.
- [[Compute and Agency]] and [[Public Trust and AI]]: a buildout can offer local benefits and still shift climate costs outward; agency includes communities asked to host the infrastructure.
- [[Pacing the Frontier]]: a product-testing norm needs accountable independent evaluation rather than either a promise of safety or indefinite delay.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Blocking shopping agents could protect a retailer's services and customers, not only its ad revenue. The roundup cannot establish Amazon's complete motivations.
- Users may rationally accept agent risk for real convenience; one reporter's personal tradeoff cannot establish a safe default for work or school accounts.
- Human takeover is valuable but may come too late after an erroneous cancellation or commitment. Some tasks require approval *before* the irreversible action.
- Data-center opponents foreground near-term bills and water for sound reasons; global emissions still deserve a separate ledger, not a competing slogan. Climate projections are conditional on buildout and power mix.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. **Conditional-action card:** for cancellations, purchases, bookings, and outbound messages record trigger, permitted action, non-negotiable condition, approval owner, receipt, and remedy owner. If a condition cannot be verified, stop rather than improvise.
2. **External-party check:** before an agent uses a retailer or government service, verify allowed access, authentication, rate limits, and who the service recognizes as the principal.
3. **Two-ledger siting review:** request local water/rates/land/jobs data and separately disclose incremental generation and emissions assumptions; label forecasts as forecasts.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**A delegated action is only as faithful as its least-visible condition.** Time saved does not erase an unapproved commitment, a broken refund rule, or a public cost booked elsewhere.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
**20-minute paper-only cancellation drill:** write an imaginary travel cancellation request with a refundable-only condition. Have one colleague act as the agent and another as reviewer. Before the mock cancellation, require them to state the evidence for eligibility, refund amount, explicit approval, receipt location, and recovery path if wrong. Score only whether the condition was preserved—not how quickly they acted. Use no real reservation, account connector, or student/personnel data.
