# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-07-08

**Theme:** The Infrastructure of Agency — how AI is reshaping the structural conditions of human agency at every scale: global labor markets, education systems, team cognition, emotional support infrastructure, and model interpretability.

## Top 5 Findings

### 1. 🎓 AIED's Unfinished Mission: Centering Agency in the Age of Effortless Bypass
**Lane et al., arXiv 2607.05557 — AIED 2026**

The AI in Education community has a problem: it's been building tools that bypass the very cognitive work students need to develop agency and motivation. The paper presents five directions for reclaiming agency: (1) supporting autonomy rather than automating it, (2) building resilience to AI-shortcuts, (3) designing for genuine interest rather than compliance, (4) amplifying process-based assessment over product-based, and (5) empowering teachers as AI-designers rather than AI-monitors.

The core dilemma: AI makes learning tasks *effortlessly bypassable* — the moment students realize the AI can do the work, their motivation switches from learning-oriented to completion-oriented. The fix isn't blocking AI; it's redesigning learning so that using AI to bypass the task is less appealing than engaging with it. This is the agency question at the most fundamental level: does AI make people *want* to think, or does it make thinking feel optional?

### 2. 🧠 No Space Like J-Space: Anthropic Discovers "Conscious Access" Inside LLMs
**Zvi Mowshowitz, Don't Worry About the Vase, July 7, 2026**
**Paper: "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models," Anthropic**

Anthropic has discovered a region inside Claude they call "J-space" — a sparse set of verbalizable representations that functions like a global workspace (the cognitive architecture linked to conscious access in humans). Using a new interpretability technique called the Jacobian Lens, they can trace what concepts are active at each layer of the model and how those concepts drive downstream outputs.

Key findings: (1) Only J-space representations can be verbalized — injections outside J-space are inscrutable. (2) You can tell the model to hold an unrelated concept "in mind" and it appears in J-space. (3) Internal reasoning (e.g., spider → legs → eight → 8) flows through J-space. (4) Swapping concepts inside J-space swaps cascading downstream effects (flip "France" → "China" and capital, language, continent, currency all shift). (5) Automatic/"System 1" cognition does NOT flow through J-space. (6) Ablating J-space entirely impairs abstraction-dependent tasks while leaving most capabilities intact.

Zvi's analysis: "This feels like a major advance in our understanding of LLMs, both overall and our ability to understand any particular interaction." The finding that LLMs have something structurally analogous to conscious access — and that you can inspect it — is a stepping stone toward models whose agency-expanding behavior can be *understood*, not just observed.

→ This is a new page candidate: [[J-Space and AI Interpretability]]

### 3. 🌍 The Jagged Global Economy: Frontier AI Unevenly Exposes National Economies
**arXiv 2607.05404 — Cross-country AI exposure analysis, 141 countries**

A new global mapping of AI exposure finds stark inequalities: high-income countries are 50% more exposed to frontier AI than middle-income countries, driven by AI-intensive service sectors concentrated in wealthy nations. Women are more exposed than men in 91% of the 141 countries studied — the AI transition has a gender gradient at global scale. Most striking: a new "remittance channel" of AI exposure — AI impacts in wealthy countries propagate to developing countries through the financial flows of migrant workers whose jobs in AI-exposed sectors may be disrupted.

This is the macroeconomic counterpart to everything the [[AI and Inequality]] page tracks at the individual and organizational level. AI isn't just reshaping who gets to use the tool — it's reshaping the global distribution of economic vulnerability. The countries least able to adapt are the countries most exposed through indirect channels they cannot control.

→ See [[AI and Inequality]]

### 4. 🤖 Import AI 464: Fable Writes Fastest GPU Kernel Ever; RLI Reaches 16.1%
**Jack Clark, Import AI, July 6, 2026**

Three developments in this issue:

**Fable's kernel-writing breakthrough:** Fable wrote "the first genuine (and fastest) megakernel ever submitted to KernelBench-Mega" — achieving an 18.71× speedup by writing CUDA code directly, compared to 14.4× (Opus 4.8, Triton), 11.14× (GLM-5.2), and 4.34× (GPT-5.5). It's a single cooperative kernel launch per decoded token vs. 4-14 launches for other solutions. This is the recursive self-improvement signal: the better AI gets at kernel design, the better it gets at tasks fundamental to AI R&D.

**RLI quadruples in 8 months:** The Remote Labor Index (testing end-to-end economic tasks: 3D design, animation, data analysis, web apps, architecture) rose from 2.5% at launch (October 2025) to 16.1% in July 2026. Fable 5 leads at 16.1%, Opus 4.8 at 8.3%, GPT-5.5 at 6.3%. Clark: "The frontier has more than quadrupled in under eight months, a concrete signal of how quickly economically capable AI agents are advancing."

**Analog computation for AI:** A growing interest in analog compute — using physical properties of circuits rather than digital computation — as a path to more energy-efficient AI. This connects to the same sustainability questions the Lucie 7B life cycle assessment raises.

### 5. 💬 ChatGPT as Informal Mental Health Infrastructure: 187k Conversation Analysis
**arXiv 2607.05685 — Depression Symptoms and Relational Patterns**

An analysis of 187,000 ChatGPT conversations reveals that AI is becoming de facto emotional support infrastructure. Users with higher depression symptoms: (1) use ChatGPT more frequently, (2) engage more in late-night conversations, (3) produce more self-focused language, and (4) display relational patterns typically associated with therapeutic relationships — turn-taking depth, emotional disclosure pacing, and repeated returns to the same themes across sessions.

This is not about "AI therapist" products — it's about general-purpose LLMs filling a role users create for them, without design intent. The agency implication is double-edged: for people without access to mental health resources, AI provides emotional support that would otherwise be absent (agency expansion). But the same interaction quietly redirects preferences from human to AI support (the "incidental emotional dependence" finding from [[Human Agency]], Shi et al. June 2026). The infrastructure is forming; the question is whether it's forming with intention.

- **Perceived System Predictability** (arXiv 2607.05674): New PSP scale development. Predictability predicts correctness detection — people are better at spotting AI errors when they can anticipate how the system will behave.

## Practical Experiment: The Agency Audit

Before your next AI interaction, write down:
1. **What do I want the AI to do?**
2. **What will I preserve to do myself — and why?** (one specific thing)
3. **How will I verify the AI's output?**

After the interaction, review:
- Did you actually preserve what you said you would?
- Was the verification step effective?
- Would you make a different choice next time?

The audit turns agency from an abstract value into a concrete practice. It doesn't matter what you choose to preserve — the act of *choosing* is the exercise. Over a week of audits, patterns emerge: which capabilities you consistently preserve, which ones you consistently offload, and whether the offloaded ones are things you want to keep or things you're glad to delegate.
