# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-11-20

## Executive summary
A useful assistant may reduce the small requests that keep people connected. Two new preprints examine social interaction from different angles; a third tests peer-informed writing. A fourth benchmarks risks of gradual chatbot attachment, while WIRED reports a practical way to test browsing agents against a hostile web. Findings are not evidence of K–12 outcomes or proof that AI use causes loneliness for everyone.

## Top 5 findings

1. **Workplace convenience may displace colleague contact.** [Chow and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25269) report an online experimental vignette study of 248 knowledge workers. AI-enabled task scenarios reduced stated intentions to seek help from colleagues relative to non-AI scenarios, particularly for brainstorming; visible AI use was associated with more help-seeking. These are intentions in scenarios, not observed long-term workplace relationships. [[Work]] and [[Human Agency]] should track the social cost of replacing a colleague's question with a private prompt.

2. **A short break from AI changed social outcomes in one sample.** [Zhao and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25263) randomly assigned 625 active ChatGPT users to abstain from AI assistants for 14 days or one day. The authors report lower loneliness relative to control at two weeks (β = −0.10, p = 0.03), persisting two weeks after resumption (β = −0.16, p = 0.02), alongside more in-person contacts and socializing during the intervention. This compares unequal abstention durations among active users; it is not a population-wide AI-versus-no-AI verdict or an estimate for children. See [[Family and Personal Life]] and [[AI and Human Flourishing]].

3. **Peer-informed writing is a design problem, not just an AI-generation problem.** [Open Response Explorer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25169) organizes open responses into purpose-based idea units and highlights comparable segments. A within-subjects lab study of 12 participants explored usability and writing practices, not measured K–12 learning gains. The promising question for [[Education]] is how to support comparison and critique without replacing authorship or revealing student work to an unapproved system.

4. **Test relationship safety over conversations, not one answer.** [CoMPASS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25109) evaluates gradually escalating anthropomorphism, attachment, and dependency across multiple turns. Under the authors' primary judge, six of eight tested frontier models showed significantly more overall parasocial reinforcement than matched controls when all three dimensions escalated together. This is a benchmark result, not a measured prevalence of harm among users. See [[Family and Personal Life]] and [[Human Agency]].

5. **A sandbox can expose browsing-agent failure before real-world access.** [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/google-built-a-giant-mirror-dimension-internet-to-test-its-chrome-ai-agents/) reports that Google's Chrome team built an isolated, realistic simulated web with social, retail, and banking workflows and embedded prompt-injection tests. The account describes Google's approach, not independently measured production safety or a license to give agents broad access. For any local deployment, combine a sandbox with least privilege, approval before external actions, and recovery testing. See [[Work]] and [[Responsible Deployment]].

## Top insight of the day
**Count the human connections an assistant replaces, not only the minutes it saves.** Augmentation is a relational design choice: productivity and retained access to colleagues, peers, and family should be evaluated together.

## Practical experiment to try
For one week, have an adult volunteer choose a non-sensitive brainstorming task. Before asking AI, spend five minutes drafting independently and ask a willing colleague for one perspective; for a matched task, try an AI-first workflow. Track time, useful ideas, whether a human interaction happened, and whether the colleague would prefer the exchange. This is a small workflow check, not a randomized study. Use no student data, confidential district material, or unapproved service; do not impose an AI abstention intervention on students or staff.

## Tensions / counterarguments
A colleague may not want every routine interruption, and AI may free time for stronger relationships. The workplace study measures intentions, not actual long-term behavior; the deactivation study's control is one day rather than unrestricted use; the education study is small; benchmark scores depend on the judge; the Google story describes a vendor-controlled test environment. Test whether saved time is reinvested in human contact rather than assuming either net benefit or harm.
