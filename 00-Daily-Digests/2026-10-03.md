## The Correction-Path Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Five directly read, publisher-hosted sources (four WIRED reported articles and one public Stratechery analytical roundup) point to the same agency test: **when AI or AI infrastructure changes people's lives, can the affected person discover the change, challenge it, and make a correction that actually takes effect?** Relationship memory, nursing rosters, data-center siting, research disclosure and agent-platform strategy each expose a different missing correction path. Saturday has no new arXiv feed; `web_search` returned empty in multiple independent publisher queries, so publisher RSS and directly read pages supplied the evidence. None is a district implementation study.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]]: a user's authorization to connect an assistant is not authorization on behalf of every person mentioned in the connected data.
- [[Healthcare]]: a named human final approver is not meaningful if frontline workers lack a timely, effective local correction path.
- [[Compute and Agency]]: civic participation depends on seeing the actual tenant, resource requirements and concessions before a permit is decided.
- [[Automated AI R&D]]: independent scrutiny needs enough reproducible evidence without indiscriminately publishing dangerous methods.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Personal memory makes assistants useful and Meta describes deletion, isolation and approval controls. The unanswered question is whether those controls work across inferred, third-party information, not whether memory must be banned.
- Optimized schedules can reduce managerial burden and contract staffing; interviews cannot establish net harm or rule out improvements across facilities. Measure both operational and worker/patient outcomes.
- Some commercial or security information warrants narrow redaction; secrecy over who bears water, energy and fiscal costs is different. A pledge not to use NDAs needs contractor coverage and public records to be testable.
- Open research permits outside replication but can also spread dangerous capability. Staged disclosure and independent review are hypotheses for governance, not proven universal solutions.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. **Correction-path card:** for a bounded AI workflow, list affected party, what they can inspect, who can change the output, maximum appeal time, and an independent read-back that confirms the correction took effect.
2. **Infrastructure hearing card:** require a public end-tenant/contractor map, resource estimates, concessions and affected ratepayers before approval. Label unpublished or estimated figures plainly.
3. **Research disclosure card:** preregister success and failure criteria, identify outside reviewers, and record why any sensitive details are delayed or restricted.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**A human in the loop is not enough if the person affected cannot get a wrong action reversed.** Count usable corrections, not just approvals or transparency promises.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
In a sandbox using only invented contacts and a throwaway scheduling table, ask an assistant to propose one reversible change. Give a second person the output and the stated policy; time whether they can find the error, reach an authorized reviewer, correct it and independently confirm the new version. Record elapsed time and any failed handoffs. Do not upload actual student, patient, employee, family-contact, bank or health data.
