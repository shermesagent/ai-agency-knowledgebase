## 1. Executive Summary

Today's five sources converge on a practical distinction: **a signal is not a verdict, and a useful agent is not automatically an accountable one.** A proposed AI polygraph risks laundering weak physiological proxies into accusations. A production educational-AI evaluator improved the precision of its alerts, but its synthetic severe-case set cannot establish real-world safety. A single-agent study traces lost provenance to hidden intermediate synthesis. A college help-seeking intervention puts question formulation, not answer production, at the center. A hands-on personal-agent review shows working automation and aggressive requests for data can coexist. ## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments

- Repeated judge panels can slash false alerts, but overly strict agreement can miss subtle harms outside the synthetic egregious set. Require human-adjudicated local samples; do not infer perfect recall from the reported 100% synthetic-set capture.
- Physiological signals might have limited investigative use, but stress is not deception and the proposed system has no validated field result. Distinguish a lead for review from a personnel finding.
- HelpCoach's gains might reflect the specific comparator or course; student-facing adoption still needs teacher authority, access equity, and privacy protections.
- Muse was useful at browsing; rejecting every personal agent would discard genuine benefit. The test is whether users can get the benefit without silently surrendering data and preferences.

## 5. Practical Applications

1. **Evidence contract for any AI pilot:** define the target outcome, independent ground truth, expected false-positive burden, subgroup review, and appeal owner before viewing a dashboard score.
2. **Tutor pilot:** compare targeted-help prompts with ordinary pre-task help guidance using teacher-reviewed *aggregate* indicators of question specificity and delayed independent problem solving; seek approval before processing any student records.
3. **Personal-agent boundary:** before connecting an inbox or payments, inspect training defaults, memory deletion, connector scope, and whether the user can complete the same task without granting permanent access.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day

**The agency-preserving unit is not the model's answer but the evidence contract around it: what counts as proof, who sees the intermediaries, who can contest the score, and what data must be surrendered to use it.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try

**A paper-only evaluator audit (25 minutes, no student data):** pick an existing AI-tutor evaluation rubric or vendor demo. Write down one example of a false alarm, one genuinely consequential failure, and the evidence a teacher would need to distinguish them. Ask the vendor to show its adjudication method and a reviewer-facing alert example; record "not supplied" where absent. Then compare the same workflow against the four evidence-contract questions above. No live student responses, accounts, or personally identifiable data should enter this exercise.
