# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-05-25

## 1. Executive Summary
- Today’s strongest pattern is the “agency equation”: as AI and agents take over bounded execution, humans can gain more room for judgment, direction, taste, relationship, and accountability — but only if organizations redesign workflows instead of simply speeding up existing ones.
- The work sources converge on augmentation over replacement: MIT Sloan’s EPOCH framing, Springer’s human-AI augmentation review, Microsoft WorkLab, and Bain all point to complementary human capabilities, process redesign, and human-in-the-loop operating models.
- Education sources became more practical today. Microsoft’s special report, TeachAI’s toolkit, and MIT Teaching Systems Lab’s guide all argue for teacher/student agency, school-level policy, and learning-process norms rather than tool bans or uncontrolled adoption.
- Responsible deployment is becoming more operational: WEF, Google DeepMind, Oxford/ITU, and MIT Sloan frame governance as infrastructure for innovation — not a brake on useful adoption — when it includes metrics, controls, accountability, and adaptation.
- The best counterarguments sharpen the optimism: SSIR warns that “automating innovation” can hollow out the human experience of creating; State of AI 2025 warns that reliability, cyber resilience, autonomous systems, and governance capacity are now concrete rather than abstract concerns.
- A new positive frame emerged: “positive alignment” asks whether AI systems can actively support pluralistic human flourishing, not merely avoid harm. That fits [[Superagency]] but should be treated as a design agenda, not proof.

- **Relevance score:** 5/5

It summarizes the EPOCH framework — Empathy, Presence, Opinion/Judgment, Creativity, and Hope — as groups of human-intensive capabilities that AI struggles to replicate. ## 3. Superagency Connections
- **Agency expansion:** Microsoft WorkLab and Positive Alignment both push the wiki beyond “AI helps productivity” toward “AI should increase people’s ability to direct, choose, create, learn, and own outcomes.”
- **Copilot use:** MIT Sloan’s EPOCH frame and Springer’s augmentation review reinforce that AI’s most valuable role is often complementing human empathy, presence, judgment, creativity, and hope rather than replacing them.
- **Responsible use beating fear-based avoidance:** WEF, DeepMind, Oxford/ITU, TeachAI, and MIT Sloan all support the idea that institutions can use AI by building governance, metrics, and review infrastructure instead of freezing adoption.
- **Humans actively shaping AI:** Education sources emphasize that teachers, students, parents, and school leaders should shape AI norms before defaults harden into classroom practice.
- **Practical adoption learning loops:** Bain, MIT Sloan, and the school guidance sources all favor bounded pilots, domain-specific workflows, human-in-the-loop builds, and explicit evaluation.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- **Job displacement:** Augmentation evidence does not eliminate displacement risk. MIT Sloan’s EPOCH framing suggests many tasks are complementary, but organizations can still choose substitution if incentives reward headcount reduction over capability growth. Balanced response: redesign roles around human-intensive capabilities and track worker agency, not just throughput.
- **Overreliance:** Agentic systems may make decisions feel settled because the system took action. Balanced response: use [[Human Review Checkpoints]], uncertainty reporting, logs, and outcome metrics before expanding autonomy.
- **Surveillance:** More agent instrumentation can become worker or student monitoring. Balanced response: collect the minimum data needed for safety and improvement, publish retention rules, and separate learning metrics from punitive surveillance.
- **Bias:** Governance playbooks cannot prove fairness by themselves. Balanced response: local testing, affected-user feedback, appeal paths, and stop conditions must be part of deployment.
- **Concentration of power:** Corporate reports show serious investment in responsible AI but also reveal that much governance capacity sits inside large vendors. Balanced response: support open standards, public-sector capacity, school procurement discipline, and local workflows where feasible.
- **Education misuse:** AI can increase student agency or shortcut learning. Balanced response: assignments should emphasize process, reflection, oral defense, revision, and teacher-guided AI use.
- **Regulation concerns:** Poor regulation can entrench incumbents or delay low-risk experimentation. Balanced response: use risk-proportional governance, sandboxes, evaluation, and clear human decision rights.
- **Innovation hollowing:** SSIR’s critique warns that AI-driven simulation can detach creators from lived markets and relationships. Balanced response: use AI to expand exploration, but keep humans in direct contact with customers, communities, constraints, and craft.

## 5. Practical Applications
- **Personal productivity:** Add a “human checkpoint” column to your AI task list: publish/send/buy/delete/decide items require review; brainstorm/summarize/draft items can move faster.
- **Education:** Have students submit an AI-use reflection with assignments: what they asked, what they accepted/rejected, what they learned, and what remains their own judgment.
- **School district leadership:** Use TeachAI + MIT TSL to convene a 60-minute educator policy workshop before piloting a new AI tool: goals, allowed uses, privacy, parent communication, evaluation, and incident handling.
- **Small business:** Pick one workflow and define a long-term “agentic architecture” plus a two-week human-in-the-loop version; measure quality, time, customer impact, and review burden.
- **Home-server AI agents:** Keep agents read-only by default; require approval before write actions, purchases, deletions, emails, public posts, or changes to infrastructure.
- **Writing/creativity:** Use AI to generate alternative paths and critiques, but require one human “lived observation” or concrete example before accepting the thesis.
- **Family life:** Use AI for tutoring hints, schedules, and explanations, but set family norms around privacy, truth-checking, and when a human conversation is required.
- **Civic life:** Use AI to summarize public documents and draft questions for meetings, with links back to primary sources and explicit uncertainty notes.
- **Work transformation:** Map tasks against EPOCH capabilities: where empathy, presence, judgment, creativity, or hope matter, use AI to support humans rather than remove them.

## Top Insight of the Day
The most agency-expanding AI systems are not the ones that make humans unnecessary; they are the ones that move execution downward while moving human judgment, authorship, relationship, and accountability upward. That requires deliberate checkpoints, metrics, and workflow design — otherwise “more machine agency” can quietly become less human agency.

## Practical Experiment to Try
For one recurring workflow this week, build a two-column “agency map.” Left column: what the AI may execute autonomously. Right column: what the human must still decide, verify, personalize, or own. Run the workflow three times, record errors and review burden, then tighten the [[Human Review Checkpoints]] before expanding autonomy.
