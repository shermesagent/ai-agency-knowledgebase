## The Terms-of-Participation Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Two domains, one question: **who can actually shape the terms of an AI-enabled system?** Google opened a conversational path to playable games, but WIRED's hands-on test found iteration limits and template-like results. WIRED's data-center election reporting shows communities using ballots and campaigns to contest the physical costs of AI infrastructure. These are **five implications from three sources**, not five independent studies. ## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Creativity]]: the new bottleneck after prompt-to-prototype is affordable revision, independent play-testing and distinction from other outputs.
- [[Compute and Agency]]: the compute behind creative tools has a physical host; that host's neighbors need the ability to inspect and alter the permit's terms.
- [[Government and Civic Life]]: participation is real when an identifiable person can change a design, a permit condition or a vote before a lock-in point.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Template-like output can be an excellent entry point for novices; sameness in one review does not mean beginners gain no agency. Equally, a playable prototype is not a tested classroom activity, sustainable game business or original finished work.
- Data centers can produce tax revenue, construction jobs and useful compute. Moratorium promises may foreclose these benefits; unverified developer promises can also shift costs onto residents. Compare project-specific evidence rather than assuming every build or ban is good.
- Google's announcement and WIRED's review concern the same product; the two political findings concern the same article. The source count is **three**, not five.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. For a non-sensitive creative prototype, keep a dated log of the initial mechanic, AI-generated build, user-reported defects, revisions, remaining faults and any usage cap. Ask a tester whether this game is recognizably different from two others.
2. For an AI infrastructure hearing, request the project name, applicant, power and water contracts, ratepayer exposure, tax concessions, permanent jobs and the official amendment/vote path **before** the decision date. Compare the eventual permit with campaign promises.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**The point of AI access is not just to let people start; it is to let them revise the result and contest the costs before the decision is locked.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
On a public, non-sensitive game prototype or public siting document, run a ten-minute **terms-of-participation check**: identify the decision owner, the last date changes are allowed, the cost of one additional revision and the evidence a participant can use to challenge the result. If any answer is missing, document it before declaring the process participatory. Do not upload student work or private district data into a public prototype service.
