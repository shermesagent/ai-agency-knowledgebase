## The Visibility-Asymmetry Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Three directly read WIRED sources (two published Sunday, one Friday and not previously indexed) expose the same agency problem at different scales: the operator sees the capital, camera feed or case file; the affected community, bystander or observer may not see the terms, recording or label until after it matters. The constructive question is **what must be visible and contestable before the consequence?** Today is a three-source, five-finding digest—not five independent studies. `web_search` returned empty on two different topic probes; publisher RSS and full publisher pages supplied the sources. ## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[AI and Inequality]]: compute growth can widen the gap between who hosts its infrastructure and who receives its gains. Test the **conversion** from eligibility and investment to jobs, fiscal return and resident voice.
- [[Public Trust and AI]]: capability and visibility must be assessed separately. A helpful wearable may still create an agency cost for people in its field of view.
- [[Government and Civic Life]]: the core risk is label travel—when source material can become a consequential downstream flag without a usable correction route.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Opportunity zones may attract investment to underserved areas; the report does not prove the expanded incentive caused siting decisions or that any named firm claimed it. Request actual claims and local outcomes, not blanket condemnation.
- Wearable cameras have accessibility and hands-free benefits. This particular reviewer praised some hardware while finding the assistant unreliable; do not generalize a single test into a population failure rate.
- DHS disputes the plaintiffs' characterization and says policy bars the conduct. A case-management record and a terrorism watchlist are not synonyms. Nevertheless, the system's sharing and appeal design merits scrutiny regardless of the litigation outcome.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. **Infrastructure-benefit card:** for a proposed data center, separate potential incentive eligibility, actual incentive claimed, projected construction jobs, lasting local jobs, ratepayer impact, water use, and an independent annual read-back. Mark unknown fields rather than estimating them.
2. **Wearable-use boundary:** before authorizing cameras or assistants in a school, workplace or public service setting, identify where capture is prohibited, how bystanders are notified, who can request removal, and how assistant mistakes are corrected. Keep sensitive environments camera-free by default pending policy review.
3. **Record-provenance audit:** in a civic system, trace one hypothetical case label through every data recipient, retention rule, human reviewer and contest channel. No real protester, student or employee records are needed.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**The party with the least access to the underlying record is often the party who bears the consequence.** Give that party timely notice, a way to test the claim and an effective correction path before calling a deployment agency-expanding.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Using only a blank template and publicly available project descriptions, draft a seven-column infrastructure-benefit card: project, eligible incentive, incentive actually claimed, expected construction jobs, expected permanent jobs, projected household utility impact and public correction contact. Enter “not disclosed” wherever the source lacks evidence. Then have a second reader distinguish an eligible benefit from a received benefit without verbal coaching. Do not upload student, patient, protester, personnel or other individual-level data.
