# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-06-03

## 1. Executive Summary

Today's sources converge on a single structural insight: **the governance defaults baked into AI tooling — not model capability — determine whether AI amplifies or erodes human agency.** Four new papers, a landmark HBR survey, a frontier model analysis, and a major book release all point to the same pattern: the design choices in how AI tools are deployed (trust boundaries, scaffolding design, agent authority, governance transparency) are the decisive factor in the agency equation.

The headline finding spans all sources: AI systems can genuinely increase human capacity — teaching assistants provided 10.8 percentage points more feedback with AI drafts, professionals actively want to share AI conversation traces to help their teams, and agents can tackle 50+ step professional workflows. But every gain comes with a design precondition: the human must remain in the loop, trust boundaries must be respected, governance must be transparent, and scaffolding must preserve discretion.

The combined message is that **the Superagency thesis — AI as amplifier of human capability — is technically achievable and empirically demonstrated, but only when governance and interaction design are treated as first-order concerns, not afterthoughts.** The path from AI capability to human agency runs through design choices that are currently being made with minimal transparency or accountability.

### 1. Toward Agentic Governance: What Shapes LLM-Agent Intervention in Public Forums? (Zhang, Chu, Krishnan / arXiv)
- **Date:** June 3, 2026 (submitted May 30)
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00603
- **Source type:** Paper
- **Reliability score:** 5/5
- **Relevance score:** 5/5
- **Summary:** A systematic analysis of how four deployment choices — model version, weight-release status (open vs. closed), provider, and system-prompt policy — independently shift LLM agent behavior in moderation-relevant public forum workflows. The same agent returns different responses on identical content depending on these four factors, making agent behavior structurally non-reproducible. Key finding: closed-weight models consistently decline more on visible challenges than hidden ones, while open-weight models reverse this pattern or show no gap. The implication is profound: **auditable governance of AI agents in public forums requires awareness of all four deployment choices, not just the model name.** Defenses based on agent behavior cannot be reliably reproduced without this transparency. The paper effectively demonstrates that "agentic governance" — the governance OF agents — is currently invisible in practice, undermining accountability, due process, and public trust.
- **Key quote/idea:** "The same agent often returns different responses on identical content, so any defense based on the agent's behavior cannot be reliably reproduced. The variation is structural."

### 2. InquiryBits: Sharing AI Conversation Traces to Support Collaboration Within Trust Boundaries (Morris, Maes / arXiv)
- **Date:** June 3, 2026
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02763
- **Source type:** Paper (user study, N=80 professionals)
- **Reliability score:** 5/5
- **Relevance score:** 5/5
- **Summary:** Introduces InquiryBits, a system that shares minimal summaries of AI conversations within configurable trust boundaries. The core problem: AI chat tools are shifting problem-solving and brainstorming conversations away from colleagues and into private AI interactions, reducing shared awareness that supports team coordination. In a study with 80 professionals, the headline finding: **people are broadly willing to share AI conversation traces to support collaboration — but only within bounded groups.** Comfort drops sharply as audience expands beyond close teams. Critically, the level of detail shared matters less than WHO can see it, with a consistent preference for more detail over less within trusted groups. The study identifies trust boundaries, not information granularity, as the most impactful design parameter for AI collaboration tools. **Directly relevant to the wiki's augmentation thesis: AI-mediated work can preserve collective intelligence, but only if sharing defaults respect team-level trust boundaries.**
- **Key quote/idea:** "Trust boundaries, more than information granularity, may be the most impactful design parameter." Professionals want to share AI traces within teams, not organizations.

### 3. AI Assistance for Discretionary Work: Increasing Feedback Provision in Higher Education (Mahinpei et al. / arXiv)
- **Date:** June 3, 2026
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03095
- **Source type:** Paper (randomized field experiment + qualitative interviews)
- **Reliability score:** 5/5
- **Relevance score:** 5/5
- **Summary:** A rigorous field experiment asking: can AI increase participation in discretionary but beneficial work — the kind people intend to do but frequently skip? The setting: TAs providing personalized feedback in a 300-level ML course (n=11 TAs, n=88 students). TAs randomly received AI-assisted feedback drafts after grading in the treatment condition. **Results: AI-assisted feedback increased feedback provision by 10.8 percentage points (p<0.001) and feedback length by 39.8 chars (p<0.001) without reducing student usefulness ratings or reducing time per character.** Qualitative interviews revealed that AI drafts functioned as editable scaffolds that lower barriers to initiating feedback — not reducing overall effort but making the first step easier. Critically, TAs remained fully in control: they could use, edit, or ignore drafts at their discretion. **This is a model case of augmentation: AI didn't replace the human judgment about what feedback to give, but it removed the blank-page problem that prevented TAs from giving feedback at all.** The study validates the core Superagency thesis: well-designed AI scaffolding can increase human engagement in meaningful work.
- **Key quote/idea:** "AI-assisted drafts function as editable scaffolds that lower barriers to initiating feedback rather than reducing overall effort." +10.8pp feedback provision with full human control preserved.
- **Related wiki themes:** #augmentation #practical-ai #ai-education #human-agency #future-of-work

## 3. Superagency Connections

- **The Agentic Governance paper is a Superagency infrastructure requirement.** If agent behavior varies structurally based on invisible deployment choices, then accountability is impossible. Superagency requires that the humans affected by AI decisions can understand, challenge, and appeal those decisions. The paper identifies exactly what must be transparent: model version, weight-release status, provider, and system-prompt policy. Without these four pieces of information, "agentic governance" is a fiction.

- **InquiryBits operationalizes the Superagency design principle at the collaboration level.** The finding that professionals WANT to share AI conversation traces within their teams — but not beyond — validates the wiki's position that AI should augment collective human intelligence, not replace it. The trust-boundary finding is a design principle: AI collaboration tools should default to team-level sharing, not organizational surveillance or private silos.

- **The Discretionary Work study is a Superagency proof-of-concept.** It demonstrates in a rigorous field experiment exactly what the wiki claims: well-designed AI can increase human engagement in meaningful work without replacing human judgment. TAs gave more feedback because AI removed the activation barrier, not because it told them what to say. This is augmentation in its purest form — the AI made humans MORE active, not less.

- **DeskCraft's 31.6% success rate is a Superagency reassurance.** The finding that even GPT-5.4 cannot reliably complete professional creative workflows means the human-in-the-loop remains indispensable. This is not a failure of AI — it's evidence that complex creative work still requires human judgment, and AI's role is collaboration, not replacement. The benchmark's formal human-agent collaboration protocol provides a template for designing agency-preserving agent systems.

- **Doctorow's Reverse-Centaur is the diagnostic tool the wiki needs.** The distinction between a Centaur (human amplified by AI) and a Reverse Centaur (human serving the machine) provides a simple, memorable test for any AI deployment: who is doing the repetitive, disempowering work? If it's the human, you've built a Reverse Centaur. The wiki's Superagency thesis is fundamentally a commitment to building Centaurs, not Reverse Centaurs.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments

- **The Agentic Governance paper's finding that closed-weight models are MORE deferential on visible challenges is a governance paradox.** If closed-weight models are more likely to decline visible challenges (potentially safer behavior), but their closed nature makes auditing impossible, we face a transparency-safety tradeoff. Open-weight models can be audited but behave differently. The paper doesn't resolve this — it identifies it as a structural tension that governance frameworks must address.

- **InquiryBits raises an uncomfortable question: what happens when AI conversation sharing becomes MANDATORY?** The study found professionals are willing to share voluntarily within trust boundaries. But if employers mandate AI conversation trace sharing (for productivity monitoring, compliance, or security), the trust boundary collapses. The design parameter that matters most — who can see it — is also the one most vulnerable to institutional pressure.

- **The Discretionary Work study has a scope limitation.** TAs are intrinsically motivated to help students — they want to give feedback but face activation barriers. AI drafts lowering the barrier works in this context. But for work that people genuinely DON'T want to do (not just struggle to initiate), AI scaffolding may have different effects. The study validates augmentation for intrinsically motivated discretionary work; it doesn't speak to coerced or purely instrumental work.

- **DeskCraft's 31.6% success rate could be read as pessimism, not reassurance.** The counterargument: if GPT-5.4 can already do 31.6% of professional workflows (including 50+ step tasks), and capability growth is on a predictable trajectory (see the Anthropic Epoch Capabilities Index in Zvi's Opus 4.8 analysis), then full automation of many professional creative workflows may be closer than the headline number suggests. The benchmark measures TODAY's capabilities, not next year's.

- **Doctorow's Reverse-Centaur framework may oversimplify.** Not every AI deployment cleanly fits the Centaur/Reverse-Centaur binary. Some systems mix amplification and subordination — an AI that helps a worker be more productive while also subjecting them to increased surveillance and performance pressure. The metaphor is diagnostically useful but may not capture hybrid cases where agency and control shift within the same system.

- **The HBR survey's "cognitive surrender" trend lacks granularity.** Naming a trend "cognitive surrender" is valuable, but the survey methodology (Reddit/Quora comments, forum posts, surveys) may capture concern about surrender more than actual surrender. People worried about AI dependency may post about it more than people who use AI instrumentally without anxiety. The trend may reflect awareness more than behavior.

## 5. Practical Applications

- **For AI governance in public-facing systems:** Implement the four-factor transparency framework from the Agentic Governance paper. Any AI agent making moderation, intervention, or decision-support actions in public forums should publicly disclose: (1) model version, (2) weight-release status, (3) provider, and (4) system-prompt policy. Without these, appeals and accountability are structurally impossible.

- **For team collaboration tools:** Default to team-level AI conversation sharing, not individual-private or organization-wide. The InquiryBits finding — that trust boundaries matter more than information granularity — is a design principle. Build sharing features that default to the team level, allow individuals to opt in to broader sharing, and never mandate organization-wide visibility without explicit consent.

- **For managers and organizations:** Audit your AI deployments for the Reverse-Centaur pattern. Ask for each AI system: who is doing the repetitive work? If the human is checking AI outputs, fixing AI errors, or formatting data for AI consumption while the AI does the interesting cognitive work, you may have built a Reverse Centaur. The goal is the opposite: AI should handle the repetitive work so humans can focus on judgment, creativity, and meaning-making.

- **For task design with AI:** Use the Discretionary Work study's scaffolding approach as a template. For any task people WANT to do but struggle to initiate: provide AI-generated drafts that are explicitly editable, ignorable, and positioned as starting points, not defaults. The key design property is that the AI lowers the activation barrier without constraining the output space.

- **For AI tool evaluation:** Add DeskCraft-style human-in-the-loop metrics to your evaluation framework. Don't just measure whether the AI can complete a task — measure how effectively it collaborates with a human to complete it. The mid-turn clarification and post-turn feedback exchanges that DeskCraft formalizes are the interaction patterns of agency-preserving AI, not AI autonomy.

- **For AI literacy programs:** Incorporate Doctorow's Reverse-Centaur diagnostic. Teach people to ask "who does this AI system work for?" as a standard evaluation question alongside "what can it do?" and "how accurate is it?" This question operationalizes the agency dimension of AI evaluation in a way that non-experts can apply.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day

**The governance defaults in AI tooling are more important than model capability for human agency — and they're being set without transparency, without accountability, and without asking the people affected.** The Agentic Governance paper demonstrates that agent behavior varies structurally based on four invisible deployment choices. InquiryBits shows that who can see AI conversation traces matters more than what those traces contain. The Discretionary Work study proves that editable scaffolding — not automation — is what preserves human agency while increasing output. And DeskCraft shows that even GPT-5.4 cannot reliably complete professional workflows without human collaboration.

The pattern: **capability is not the bottleneck. Design is.** And design is currently being determined by AI providers and platform companies with minimal transparency. The Superagency prescription is clear: governance transparency, trust-respecting defaults, editable scaffolding, and human-in-the-loop collaboration protocols are not "nice-to-haves" — they're the difference between a Centaur and a Reverse Centaur.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try

**Run a Reverse-Centaur audit on one AI tool you use regularly.** For a week, track every interaction with that tool and classify each one: (1) Who did the repetitive, mechanical, or disempowering work — you or the AI? (2) Who made the judgment calls — you or the AI? (3) If you stopped using the AI tomorrow, would you be less capable (lost amplification) or more capable (shed unnecessary busywork)?

The audit isn't about whether to use AI — it's about whether your current AI usage pattern is building you as a Centaur (AI handling the repetitive work, you handling the judgment) or a Reverse Centaur (you handling the repetitive work, AI making the judgments). If you find yourself in Reverse Centaur territory, the fix isn't to stop using AI — it's to redesign HOW you use it. The Discretionary Work study shows one pattern that works: AI provides an editable draft, you provide the judgment.
