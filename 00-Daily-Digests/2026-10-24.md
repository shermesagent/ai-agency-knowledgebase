## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Home Server AI Agents]] × [[Sandbox Integrity]]: local control means little if an exposed dependency cannot be identified, patched, and verified without trusting the same agent that may be compromised.
- [[Adoption Readiness Checklist]] × [[Human Review Checkpoints]]: a responsible approval gate must name a patch owner, the protected service, a verification step and a rollback option. AI can propose or test a fix; it cannot substitute for authorized change control.
- [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]] × [[Public Trust and AI]]: openly naming a funding dependency is better than concealing it, but transparency alone does not prove independence.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- The PHP story is **not** evidence that the whole internet was compromised or that AI can autonomously discover every old flaw. WIRED explicitly reports human expertise and no successful exploitation observed by the PHP Foundation at publication time.
- Moving every security patch to an autonomous agent might shrink the patch window but increase the blast radius of a bad update, malicious package or overprivileged account. Human-owned staging and recovery still matter.
- A sponsorship disclosure is a governance input, not evidence that this particular sponsor changed Stratechery's coverage. Keep the two claims separate.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. Inventory internet-facing services and the people responsible for their dependency updates; confirm supported versions from **official vendor advisories**, not a newsletter alone. Do not run speculative fixes on production.
2. For one non-production service, time the interval from advisory to inventory lookup, staged patch, independent test and rollback readiness. Document where waiting occurs.
3. For recurring AI-assisted publishing, document who funds or hosts the workflow and who can override editorial decisions; record disclosures where they can be read by the affected audience.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**Discovery is not defense: agency survives when the owner can identify, stage, verify and, if necessary, reverse the next change.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
On an **isolated, non-production** test service using only public/demo data, run a 30-minute patch-window drill. Have an AI assistant draft a dependency inventory and proposed update plan, then have a separate human verify the installed version, vendor advisory, test result and restore path *without executing the assistant's proposed command*. Record the time and any disagreement. Never paste live secrets, student information or production configurations into a public model; do not auto-patch a live district service based on this exercise.
