## Executive Summary
The useful human role is not simply to approve an AI-produced answer at the end. In creative writing, a human planner changed the diversity of a chain's stories; in secondary-school mathematics, the *change* in a student's requests predicted unaided performance. At the organizational boundary, a reported personal-agent deployment on a defense network and a separately reported monitoring pilot warn that a worker's ability to install an agent must not be confused with authorization to access systems. **Give people control upstream over the plan and question, and enforce technical access downstream.**

## Top 5 Findings
1. **Put a person where they shape the trajectory.** Li et al. report that a human planner in an otherwise AI writing chain preserved the all-AI creativity level while increasing across-story diversity; the result depends on the chain and task. → [[Creativity]], [[AI Writing Partners]].
2. **More AI help is not the same as stronger independent learning.** Abdelghani et al. found limited planning and monitoring among 112 secondary students; help-seeking became less understanding-oriented on average. → [[AI Tutors]], [[Education]].
3. **The direction of the student's questions matters.** In the same math study—not a second independent result—students whose help requests became more understanding-oriented performed better later without AI, beyond pretest and aggregate patterns. A pilot should test unaided transfer rather than chatbot fluency. → [[AI Tutors]], [[AI Tutor Evaluation Checklist]].
4. **Personal-agent installation is a system-access decision.** WIRED's sources describe a personal agent on a defense network for two months before detection and patching. We do not know its precise reach or whether sensitive information left the network; inventory and scope connectors before allowing them. → [[Responsible Deployment]], [[Sandbox Integrity]].
5. **Monitoring claims need a coverage test.** Zvi relays METR's pilot findings about visibility gaps in OpenAI internal agent monitoring and OpenAI's stated fixes. A separate, controlled replay should test whether actions are recorded and blocked where intended; public summaries are not that test. → [[Responsible Deployment]], [[Human Review Checkpoints]].

## Superagency Connections
Human capacity expands when the tool drafts and helps explore. Human freedom depends on keeping the author of the plan and learner of the concept in charge. Platforms need enforceable identities, scoped connectors and inspectable action traces. Value includes originality across people and the ability to solve a problem *without* the tool. Wisdom is knowing where to place the human contribution—and which environment the agent should never enter. This connects [[Superagency]] to [[Human Agency]] and [[Responsible Deployment]].

## Tensions / Counterarguments
- The creative-writing study's result is specific to stories and its evaluation; it does not say every human must plan first, or that AI planning always reduces quality.
- The education paper's observed association may reflect prior motivation or ability; changing the tutor's wording alone has not been shown to cause an unaided learning gain.
- The defense-network report's opening is source-attributed journalism but its gated remainder prevented independent reconstruction. Avoid claiming a confirmed data breach.
- Monitoring telemetry can itself become surveillance; access, retention and redaction need limits. A pilot's patched findings do not prove a current system is either safe or unsafe.

## Practical Applications
Run one **direction-and-access audit** in a sandbox. For writing, compare human-planned and AI-planned prompts against blind-rated story quality and diversity; use new, consenting adult-created material. For learning, test the coding rubric on invented *questions only* as a rubric dry-run, then seek approved de-identified classroom data and an independent unaided task before drawing any student-learning conclusions. For agent security, use a non-sensitive test account and a harmless canary endpoint to confirm connector scope, logging and revocation; never probe a real district or production network without authorization.

## Top Insight of the Day
**Human oversight is strongest at two boundaries: before the AI sets the direction, and before it gains access.** A signature after the fact cannot recover either a lost learning opportunity or an unauthorized connector.

## Practical Experiment to Try
Choose five public-domain writing prompts with no student work. For each, have one adult write a short plan and let the AI complete a story; separately let the AI plan and draft under the same constraints. Blind-rate distinctness across the resulting stories and whether the author's intended idea survives. Independently, dry-run a read-only agent against a test workspace: document allowed files, denied files, captured action events and an actual successful revocation. Report results as a local workflow test, **not** as evidence that the preprints replicated or that the real network is secure.
