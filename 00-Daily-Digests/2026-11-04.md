## Executive Summary
A human override is only meaningful if the person receives the right information in time, the evaluator measures what matters, and the system makes changing course possible. Today's sources connect communication design, judge validation, institutional incentives, and user control. **Agency is not a stated value or an approval button; it is an intervention that works under real constraints.**

## Top 5 Findings
1. **The best alert may be shorter, conditionally.** In the 114-person task, participants resolved listener-aware underspecified alerts almost as reliably and sooner than fully specified ones, while detecting more problems. Prior knowledge was controlled; do not extrapolate to an unfamiliar operator. → [[AI as Copilot]], [[Human Review Checkpoints]].
2. **Two different AI judges are not a human validity check.** Median verdict agreement with a human vote was κ = 0.22; ranking correlation varied widely by judge. Cross-provider replication improved precision against the human conclusion by 0.10 for a specific low-cost pair, largely through filtering a weak judge. → [[The Judge Problem]], [[Agentic Verification]].
3. **Incentives can supplement visibility, not replace it.** MIT Technology Review describes solver–auditor incentives, possible human arbitration and collusion checks as mechanism-design proposals. Whether a real deployment would respond as intended is unproven. → [[Balanced Governance]], [[Responsible Deployment]].
4. **An agency principle needs a control surface.** Mowshowitz describes the revised spec's commitment to facilitating agency and supporting oversight; he also identifies uncertainty around hard cases and user-set limits. A declared principle should be tested with override, revocation and stop scenarios. → [[Human Agency]], [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]].
5. **The institutional incentive is the outer loop.** MIT Technology Review asks whether markets reward a slower or costlier safer model. This is a second inference from source three, not a separate finding of measured market effects: no prompt-level or agent-level incentive can stand in for procurement and deployment accountability. → [[Balanced Governance]], [[The Judge Problem]].

## Superagency Connections
**Capacity:** concise help can free human attention for the next problem. **Freedom:** the user can redirect and stop the action. **Platforms:** alert timing, judge selection and instruction hierarchy determine who actually has leverage. **Value:** measure correct timely interventions and human-anchored ranking decisions, not approval clicks or model agreement alone. **Wisdom:** build human arbitration and independent checks into the surrounding institution. Read with [[Superagency]] and [[Human Agency]].

## Tensions / Counterarguments
- Short alerts may be efficient for familiar operators yet dangerous for new staff or accessibility needs; favor a visible detail expansion and an explicit fallback.
- Human votes are not infallible. One vote per Arena battle limits how confidently the judge paper can attribute shared errors.
- Auditing auditors can reduce some strategic misbehavior, but agents may collude or optimize an unintended scoring proxy; no demonstrated deployment guarantee is claimed.
- Making every user-set boundary irrevocable can also reduce autonomy. Differentiate a reversible preference from a deliberate self-binding rule; require informed confirmation rather than silent override.

## Practical Applications
A district considering an AI alert or evaluator should write an **intervention contract** before a pilot: (1) who receives the alert and what prior knowledge is assumed; (2) the deadline and safe fallback; (3) independent human anchor examples; (4) who can pause, reverse or appeal; and (5) the decision threshold and evidence of success. Do not use real student records, private staff data or live integrations for this design exercise. Any actual district pilot needs privacy, accessibility, procurement and instructional review first.

## Top Insight of the Day
**A person does not have control merely because an AI system promises it; control exists when their timely correction survives the alert, evaluation and action pipeline.**

## Practical Experiment to Try
With two adult reviewers and a *fictional, non-student* process diagram (no person-level data), present one verbose and one concise alert for an expiring approval. Ask each reviewer to identify the required action, deadline, evidence and stop path without help, then reveal the full explanation. Record correct-and-on-time responses and disagreements, not just which version they prefer. If either alert hides a critical fact, redesign it before any live trial.
