## 1. Executive Summary
Four newly indexed sources—two research preprints announced in the October 23 arXiv RSS cycle, an October 22 WIRED interview and an October 22 Zvi newsletter essay—offer five findings. Findings two and three come from the **same** scientific-ideation study; this is not five independent studies. ## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Family and Personal Life]] × [[Co-Intelligence]]: bringing your own problem to a trusted peer group may lower the *question-framing* barrier more effectively than another prompt template. Whether that works at scale needs evaluation.
- [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]] × [[The Judge Problem]]: assisted output, revision gains, judge preferences and actual human-rated quality are different outcomes. A single AI score cannot collapse them.
- [[Agentic Verification]] × [[Human Review Checkpoints]]: preserve a readable version-linked evidence trail; reserve human review for actions and contested consequential claims, not an endless stream of indistinguishable pop-ups.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Peer-led access is not universal: the reported dinners are invite-only and small. The WIRED interview reports a care-insurance packet uploaded to AI; it does not establish privacy authorization or the correctness of the coverage answer. Never upload real health/financial records as a workshop exercise.
- A community-verification ledger can distribute checking work, but also entrench gatekeepers or reward performative reviews. No field deployment or error-reduction result is reported by the proposal.
- The ideation study's null advantage is specific to its task and comparison; its positive revision result does not tell us whether AI or human collaboration is always best.
- Fewer permission prompts might reduce fatigue but could increase unauthorized action. Zvi's proposed tradeoff is a design hypothesis, not a certified safe setting.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. Design a 30-minute, peer-led **public-data-only** problem clinic: each person states a real question, one person plays verifier, and the group records the original answer, checked source and final decision. No student, patient, employee, financial or proprietary documents.
2. For a research or curriculum-writing pilot, blind first-pass human scoring to AI-use labels; record draft-to-final revision, ranking agreement with AI judges and what participants can check without assistance.
3. Replace repetitive approval prompts with a scoped action card: what tool, what data, what effect, who may approve, how to undo; stop on out-of-scope actions. Test the workflow with non-sensitive material before real deployment.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**Verification should be a social capability and a traceable process, not a second model's vote or a button nobody reads.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
With a *publicly available, non-sensitive* short document, pair two colleagues for one 20-minute AI-assisted task. Person A proposes an answer and cites exact passages; person B checks two consequential claims against the source *before* seeing the AI's confidence or a second model's score. Record changes after review and whether B could reproduce the conclusion. Compare with one unaided attempt on a comparable public task only if the tasks and scoring are predefined. This tests the team's checking workflow, not a claim that AI improves outcomes generally.
