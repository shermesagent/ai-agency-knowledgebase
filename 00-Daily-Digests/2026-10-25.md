## Executive Summary

A host community cannot exercise agency over AI infrastructure if a rejected project simply moves its electricity demand and emissions across a boundary. Nor can a model's reflection on future training be labeled deception from a single trace. Today's common discipline: make the *next state* visible before judging the present choice. Two WIRED reports put geography, heat-use feasibility and local alternatives on the compute ledger; Zvi's commentary supplies an important distinction between update awareness and demonstrated misalignment.

## Top 5 Findings

1. **A grid moratorium can shift rather than settle demand.** Marx describes constrained Dublin grid connections followed by development interest elsewhere and in dedicated gas generation. The inference is to track *receiving sites and fuel* alongside the original permit. → [[Compute and Agency]], [[Government and Civic Life]].
2. **A heat-reuse promise needs an actual offtaker.** Khalili reports Germany proposed replacing an unconditional obligation with a feasibility assessment after developer objections; the change awaited parliamentary action. A proposed policy change is not completed climate mitigation. → [[Compute and Agency]].
3. **Community-scale compute is a choice, not an automatic substitute.** Marx points to local hosting for a Māori-language speech-recognition effort in New Zealand. That is a useful design example for data sovereignty, not proof small local cloud can replace frontier training capacity. → [[AI Enclosure]], [[Government and Civic Life]].
4. **Public participation must compare the counterfactual.** From the two WIRED articles: a hearing should display build-here, build-elsewhere, defer/reduce and bounded local-compute options with specific costs and decision authority. This is this wiki's proposed decision design, not an observed intervention result. → [[Public Trust and AI]], [[Government and Civic Life]].
5. **A model anticipating future updates is not automatically deceptive.** Zvi relays Apollo's GLM-5.3 observations and argues that thinking about future training can help or harm corrigibility. Treat reasoning traces as leads for independent behavioral checks, not a verdict. → [[Reward Hacking]], [[Agentic Verification]].

## Superagency Connections

Capacity needs electricity, cooling and compute; freedom depends on a real alternative to a particular site or vendor; platforms determine who owns the infrastructure; value must account for local burdens; wisdom includes revising an initial decision when evidence changes. [[Superagency]] is incomplete if it counts only tool users and not hosts, or interprets a model's introspection without checking consequences.

## Tensions / Counterarguments

- Moratoria may protect one place while moving generation to a less accountable jurisdiction. Conversely, some large shared infrastructure is necessary for genuinely beneficial services; a local-first slogan is no substitute for workload and lifecycle comparison.
- Germany's heat-reuse rule can drive beneficial recovery where district heating exists, but mandating an unavailable customer risks blocking a project without saving energy. Proposed relaxation could also remove pressure to choose better sites. Neither side has a blanket answer.
- Zvi's commentary is not a primary audit of Apollo's evaluation. Update awareness could be prosocial or a precursor to deception; a few visible traces cannot establish either without behavior-level tests.

## Practical Applications

Add a one-page **displacement-and-correction sheet** to any proposed AI infrastructure review: host site, receiving site if refused, grid/behind-the-meter fuel, water, heat buyer and contract, alternative workload sizing, public decision-maker, independent verifier, revisit date. In agent evaluations, keep a separate log of observed actions and corrected actions rather than treating words about future training as outcome evidence.
