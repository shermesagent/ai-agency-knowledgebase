# AI Agency Daily Digest — 2026-11-18

## Executive Summary
Five source-grounded findings from arXiv preprints converge on a practical rule: **an AI system expands agency only when people can check what it did, retain a real stop right, and distinguish real human evidence from plausible simulation.** Four papers were submitted November 17; the governance paper was submitted September 15 and is newly reviewed here. Preprints and small exploratory studies are not deployment validation.

## Top 5 Findings
1. **Rollback is action-specific, not a generic “undo” button.** Waghmare and Sufi classify agent actions by reversibility and test a session guard on recorded runs. Capturing prior state and identifying irreversibly observed consequences matter more than a broad read/write permission label. Recovery still has failure cases such as concurrent edits and browser state. [*Can You Undo It?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22075) (2026-11-17; preprint). → [[Human Review Checkpoints]]
2. **Shopping agents can get more confident without making better choices.** Across three product-choice datasets and ten frontier models, extra reasoning did not yield a statistically meaningful accuracy gain but did increase reported confidence; model errors also overlapped. These are benchmark product selections, not live checkout data. [Krishnamoorthy and Han, *Thinking Harder Doesn't Lead to Better Choices*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22033) (2026-11-17; preprint). → [[AI for Small Businesses]]
3. **Student-built agents may engage without proving learning.** A work-in-progress undergraduate computing-education study reports student experience building conversational agents or videos, including novelty, engagement and substantial complexity/time costs. It does not establish learning gains or K–12 effectiveness. [Zangari et al., *WIP: Exploring Student-Created AI Agents and Instructional Videos*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23058) (2026-11-17; preprint). → [[Education]]
4. **Synthetic stakeholder interviews are scaffolds, not substitutes for people.** In a design-course study of eight teams and 21 participants, interview sequence was associated with how teams used simulated stakeholder voices. Small, exploratory, single-institution evidence cannot establish the best sequence; keep actual human experience distinguishable and decisive. [Mokryn et al., *AI as Anchor*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22740) (2026-11-17; preprint). → [[Education]]
5. **A review board without an effective stop right may be cosmetic.** Frimpong's comparison of public governance materials for four technology companies proposes that competition can suppress ethics oversight in practice. It is qualitative interpretation, **published 2026-09-15**, not evidence of a new November incident or a causal effect size. [Frimpong, *Ethics Suppression in AI Development*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21629) (2026-09-15; newly reviewed; preprint). → [[Balanced Governance]]

## Superagency Connections
[[Human Agency]] requires meaningful intervention, not a decorative human-in-the-loop: a reviewer needs recoverable state, accurate evidence about an agent's choice, and authority to pause it. In schools, [[Education]] also requires students to articulate their own reasoning and to recognize that synthetic interviewees are not real stakeholder testimony.

## Tensions / Counterarguments
- Action gates add friction; the reversibility paper's recorded-run counts cannot tell us the net benefit in a live small-business workflow.
- Simulated stakeholders can help generate questions when access to people is limited; banning them outright would erase a possible scaffold, but treating them as testimony would erase real voices.
- Confidence/accuracy results from shopping benchmarks may not generalize to an owner's inventory or local prices. Test the actual purchasing workflow.

## Practical Applications
Keep purchase actions behind a budget cap and explicit human approval. For an educational pilot, require an independent student explanation and label synthetic versus human evidence. For governance, test whether the designated reviewer can actually delay or stop launch. Use approved systems and non-sensitive examples; never upload student, applicant or customer data to an unapproved AI service.

## Top Insight of the Day
**“Human oversight” is a verb: inspect evidence, stop the action, and recover when possible.**

## Practical Experiment to Try
On a throwaway, non-sensitive purchasing scenario, ask an AI agent to select a product under a stated budget without placing an order. Record its source listing, SKU, price, confidence and reason. Independently check those fields on the seller's listing. Then simulate a changed price or unavailable item: can the reviewer stop the action, identify what state was captured, and specify a recovery route? Compare actual correct selections and review time against doing the task manually. No real payment credentials, student records or customer data.
