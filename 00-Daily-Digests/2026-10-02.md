## The Authority-to-Effect Gap

## 1. Executive Summary
Five directly read sources, three reported/analytical articles and two research preprints, converge on a practical test: **who can authorize an AI action, verify what actually happened, and retain independent capability afterward?** Local app privileges, in-cabin surveillance, attractive narratives of machine reasoning, agent execution receipts, and learning after advice withdrawal are different pieces of that test. Web search returned no results in two independent probes; publisher feeds, publisher articles, and live arXiv abstracts supplied the evidence. These are not district deployment results.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Agentic Technical Debt]]: an agent's claimed success should be distinguished from an externally verified change. A missing read-back is a missing fact, not a harmless documentation gap.
- [[AI Coding Agents]]: even a perfectly written permission prompt cannot prevent a local trusted-process boundary from being abused. Give local assistants fewer keys.
- [[AI Field Experiment Evidence]]: agency is not just better output while assisted; it includes accurate understanding and capability after the tool leaves.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Cabin video may genuinely protect riders, drivers outside the vehicle, and staff; the issue is scope, retention, secondary use, notice, and appeal, not whether cameras are inherently illegitimate.
- Inspectable epistemic state is a useful design target, but Graepel's narrower use of “reasoning” should not erase demonstrated usefulness of present LLMs or imply every reported trace is faithful.
- Praxa offers a stronger claim taxonomy but its own measured pilot did not improve strict task success; adding gates can impose material cost. Welsch's simulation supports more nuanced evaluation, not a blanket finding that AI advice harms learning.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. **Authority/effect card:** for one low-risk agent action, record requester, permitted tool and target, approving person, execution receipt, independent read-back, and rollback owner. If read-back disagrees, mark the action unresolved.
2. **Consent-scope card:** list why data are collected, who sees them, retention period, secondary uses, and an accessible opt-out/appeal path before enabling a camera, mic, or AI connector. No live student/personnel data for a trial.
3. **Withdrawal test:** compare a task performed with assistance, a fresh unaided task afterward, and self-rated confidence. Record when the user changes the assistant's recommendation; do not infer causality from that correlation.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**Permission to act, evidence that an action occurred, and human ability to function afterward are three separate claims.** A good AI workflow makes each independently testable.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
In a sandbox with public, non-sensitive information, ask an assistant to propose a reversible edit to a throwaway document. Approve or reject the exact edit yourself; if approved, inspect the exact target directly rather than trusting the tool success message. Later, complete a related short task without the assistant and record what you could still explain. No production system, financial account, student data, camera feed, or privileged connector involved.
