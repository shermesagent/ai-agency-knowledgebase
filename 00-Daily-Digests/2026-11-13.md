# Daily AI Agency Digest — November 13, 2026

## Executive Summary
**Bottom line:** More AI participation is not automatically more human agency. Representation quality, the ability to contest an agent's position, and the breadth of futures people can imagine matter as much as throughput. Today's five sources are arXiv preprints surfaced in the November 13 RSS feeds (the arXiv pages for several show earlier submission dates). The educational finding comes from a post-CS1 course, **not** a K–12 effectiveness study. None warrants unsupervised policy or instructional deployment.

## Best Sources Found — Top 5 Findings

1. **Scale civic input without impersonating consent.** [Scaling public input on AI policy with pluralistic agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16028) tested agent representatives and a preregistered study with 746 people. Participants preferred direct participation overall, though represented participation rated better than a single-model input baseline. Persona instructions affected the agents' influence; the authors report recovery of 68% of the satisfaction gap and 82% of the fairness gap between baseline and direct participation. These are measured perceptions in a study, not a validation of electoral or public-policy legitimacy. [[Human Agency]] · [[AI and Inequality]]
2. **More dialogue may help more than more bots.** [Understanding the Effects of Multi-Agent Deliberation for Human-AI Decision-Making](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14901) reports two user studies in answer verification and fact-checking: multi-agent deliberation beat single-round help, with multi-round interaction driving much of the benefit. A devil's-advocate persona reduced accuracy and user experience; active human participation helped accuracy in one task but increased frustration. Do not assume adding an adversarial agent improves decisions. [[Agentic Verification]] · [[Human Review Checkpoints]]
3. **Students' AI metaphors merit investigation, not prescription.** [Who (or what) are they prompting?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14798) analyzes end-of-course responses from 283 consenting post-CS1 students: human-assistant metaphors and context-rich prompts were common. Reported associations between mental models and use are exploratory, not causal, and the setting is not Texas K–12. A school could ask what students believe the model does before teaching verification strategies. [[Education]] · [[Human Agency]]
4. **Creative abundance can hide uniform imagination.** [One Future Fits All](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16089) audits 907 Bing Create images across 15 languages and 12 countries, finding convergent future-city visual tropes in its exploratory sample. This does not measure every image model, but it provides a concrete test for whether creative tools expand or compress local possibilities. [[Creativity]] · [[AI and Inequality]]
5. **Agency includes care, not just retained expertise.** [Care, Human Enfeeblement, and the Existential Implications of AGI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14520) argues that sustained disengagement from caring relationships and the world is a distinct long-term risk of AI dependence. It is philosophical analysis rather than measured prevalence. [[Human Agency]] · [[AI and Human Flourishing]]

## Superagency Connections
The common design rule: let people formulate, revise, and challenge the objective. [[Superagency]] needs both accessible assistance and meaningful authorship; [[Agency Expansion Framework]] should examine whether an agent actually broadens choices and preserves the human's capacity to care and decide.

## Tensions / Counterarguments
- A representative agent could include people unable to attend a meeting, but opaque prompting and unequal verbosity could magnify existing inequalities. Direct participation remains the comparison standard, not an inconvenience to optimize away.
- Extra rounds of AI deliberation take time; the fact-checking results do not generalize to school discipline, hiring, or clinical decisions.
- The image audit identifies a bounded visual pattern, not evidence of a uniform effect across all tools, countries, or users.

## Practical Applications
For an **internal, nonbinding** policy consultation, compare direct human comments with agent-assisted summaries on fidelity to stated preferences, ability to correct misrepresentation, and differences across groups. Do not upload identifiable student, family, or employee comments to a public model; use approved infrastructure and an authorized reviewer.

## Top Insight of the Day
**The participation paradox:** a tool can multiply the number of voices counted while narrowing how much each person actually controls the words, influence, or imagined outcome attributed to them.

## Practical Experiment to Try
In a staff-only, opt-in, low-stakes hypothetical discussion, have each participant write a short position, then ask an approved AI to summarize it. Let each participant score fidelity on a simple 1–5 scale, identify omissions, and revise the summary before it is shared. Compare the initial and corrected summaries; record whether disagreement was preserved rather than averaged away. **Success criterion:** participants approve their own corrected summaries and no personal or student information leaves approved systems. This tests representation quality, not policy legitimacy or student outcomes.
