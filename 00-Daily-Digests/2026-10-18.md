## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Education]] × [[Compute and Agency]]: infrastructure literacy expands student agency only if learners can compare sponsor claims with ratepayer, environmental and career evidence.
- [[AI and Creator Rights]]: access to distribution, training permission and synthetic-persona licensing are different rights. Treating them as one toggle lets platforms define both the rule and the remedy.
- [[Balanced Governance]]: a transparency rule does not substitute for enforceable audit access, state participation and authority to halt a dangerous deployment.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- School tours can provide real technical and career learning; prohibiting all industry contact would remove those opportunities. Require disclosure, independent comparison and teacher-controlled instruction instead.
- A private, age-gated text agent is not the same service as public account promotion. Unequal treatment is a question for a documented policy and appeal audit, not an automatic inference from the two products' different rules.
- Uniform federal law can reduce patchwork and protect against overbroad state or executive intervention. Whether this *draft* strikes that balance requires the actual text, comparison with existing law and multiple legal readings—not Zvi's essay alone.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. For a proposed sponsored school visit, request the handout, sponsor, instructional goal and independent evidence on jobs, utilities, water and emissions before approval. Check district privacy and student-photography rules.
2. For an AI creator platform, ask separately for moderation appeals, training-data provenance, likeness licensing and opt-out/compensation terms.
3. For an AI safety proposal, build a side-by-side map of state rules removed, federal audit powers added, emergency stop threshold and appeal rights before labeling it stronger or weaker.

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Run a 20-minute *document-only* terms audit for one proposed AI-related school activity: list the sponsor's three main claims, one independent source for each, who bears a cost, and the student question the sponsor cannot preapprove. Have an educator and a facilities/finance reviewer sign off before considering a visit. Use only public documents—no identifiable student, employee or family data, and no automatic invitation to a vendor.
