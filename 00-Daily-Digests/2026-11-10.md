# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-11-10

## Executive Summary
Five sources connect model capability, real-world collaboration, educational engagement, civic participation, and faster AI R&D. The common rule: **measure what people can still understand, do, and contest after AI enters the workflow.** Three research papers are preprints, one essay is a hands-on demonstration, and the R&D acceleration piece interprets another organization's investigation. None alone proves a long-term outcome.

## Best Sources Found — Top 5 Findings
1. **Impressive general-looking performance remains jagged.** [Ethan Mollick, *Fable 5.2 and Jagged AGI*](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/fable-52-and-jagged-agi) (Nov 9; reliability 4/5, relevance 5/5) shows one-shot creative and coding examples while limiting his AGI claim to computer-based intellectual tasks and identifying remaining gaps. Demonstrations are not an independent evaluation of performance across whole jobs. See [[Intelligence Amplification]].
2. **Collaboration is more varied than delegation.** [Shao et al., *Human-AI Collaboration at Scale*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09902) (preprint submitted Aug 17; newly surfaced in this review; reliability 4/5, relevance 5/5) analyze 249,834 conversations: human-led collaboration predominates; active teaching appears in 67%, and friction in roughly half. Conversational traces do not prove learning gains or causality. See [[Intelligence Amplification]] and [[Cognitive Surrender]].
3. **Student chat depth and return-to-task are separate signals.** [Zhuang et al., *From Dialogue to Task*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11435) (preprint submitted Nov 8; reliability 4/5, relevance 5/5) examine 1,681 students across 40 university courses, coding 829 dialogue threads and 2,969 task sequences. Higher engagement correlated with better resource-creation artifacts; in practice, AI dialogue after an incorrect attempt was linked to fewer later attempts but higher next-attempt correctness among students who continued. These are associations in university contexts, not K–12 causal effects. See [[Education]] and [[Cognitive Surrender]].
4. **Retrieval of public comments can make participation more legible.** [Yan et al., *Public Perceptions of Public Participation in Local Government Mediated through a GenAI Tool*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10047) (preprint submitted Nov 5; reliability 4/5, relevance 4/5) study 72 people in Ann Arbor and Seattle using VoicesView: the share answering a procedural question correctly rose from 53% to 74%, and the share agreeing their voice is heard rose from 28% to 46%. Small, short-term perceptions and knowledge measures do not prove increased participation. See [[Government and Civic Life]].
5. **AI R&D acceleration deserves scrutiny, not a calendar certainty.** [Zvi Mowshowitz, *AI R&D Is Accelerating Towards Potential RSI in 2027*](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-r-and-d-is-accelerating-towards) (Nov 9; reliability 3/5, relevance 5/5) interprets a METR investigation at Anthropic as roughly 2× faster capability progress relative to an earlier period, with a broad uncertainty interval. His 2027 recursive-self-improvement scenario is a projection; some bottleneck analysis is redacted. Do not equate pace at one lab with proof of a self-sustaining loop. See [[Automated AI R&D]] and [[Strongest AI Risk Arguments]].

## Superagency Connections
[[Intelligence Amplification]] is not simply high model output quality. The collaboration and education studies suggest that retained human initiation, revision, and subsequent task activity matter; [[Automated AI R&D]] adds a speed pressure on the institutions that verify new systems. The civic example suggests a constructive role for AI in making existing public records accessible without replacing people's testimony.

## Tensions / Counterarguments
- Observational conversation and student traces can misclassify the quality of thinking; active-looking dialogue is not proof of transfer.
- A spectacular demo can obscure jaggedness; an alarmed interpretation of acceleration can overstate a forecast. Both require independent measurement.
- A civic search tool may amplify the views already present in public-comment records, which may not represent the whole community.
- Student or civic records require consent, minimization, and human review before any high-stakes use.

## Practical Applications
- For learning, assess an independently completed next step and artifact quality, not message count or generated fluency alone.
- For public-records interfaces, show the original comment, date, speaker context, and correction path; do not present generated summaries as the public's unanimous view.
- For fast-moving AI deployments, set review cadence and stop conditions in advance; revisit them when capability or workflow changes.

## Top Insight of the Day
**Evaluate the human after the AI, not just the AI in isolation:** can the person produce, verify, or contest the next consequential step?

## Practical Experiment to Try
With a non-sensitive staff training task, compare two conditions using the *same real task*: one group reviews an AI-generated answer, the other states an initial hypothesis before seeing AI. Have each person complete one short follow-up step without AI; score the result against a prewritten rubric and collect time spent. Treat this as a local process check, not a controlled efficacy study. Do not enter student work or personal records into a new tool without district approval.
