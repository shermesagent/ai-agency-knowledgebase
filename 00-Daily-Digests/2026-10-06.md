## The Participation-to-Decision Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Four sources—one research newsletter, one interview and two arXiv preprints—make the same distinction from different directions: hearing people, asking them to approve, or producing a technically persuasive result is not the same as giving them consequential authority. Today's five findings come from **four sources**, not five independent studies. Web search returned empty on multiple publisher-specific probes, so publisher full text and arXiv RSS abstracts supplied the evidence. The research papers are preprints; neither evaluates a school-district deployment.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[The Participation Problem]]: an invitation to comment is meaningful only if dissent, abstention and non-deployment requests survive translation into the decision record.
- [[AI Use Case Evaluation Rubric]]: outcome ownership needs more than a prompt; document the reviewer's decision rights and trace action through to a final effect. Parallel agents add a latency-versus-total-cost choice, not free productivity.
- [[Optimism Without Naivety]]: take the upside of assistive research seriously while distinguishing a practitioner's account and a second-hand poll from independently validated outcomes.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- Public agencies need categories to process comments consistently. The preprint's critique is not a case for abandoning records; it is a case for preserving verbatim dissent, publishing disposition and identifying who can change the proposal.
- Requiring a human decision on every agent micro-action can erase speed advantages. Put high-stakes or externally visible effects behind named gates and measure reviewer capacity, rather than imposing blanket friction.
- Journalistic AI-assisted research can find leads and challenge drafts; it can also launder unsupported assertions through fluent copy. Keep primary-source verification and disclose the contribution where it matters to readers.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. For a public-sector AI proposal, maintain a response matrix: community concern (including 'do not deploy'), unchanged text, agency disposition, design change, decision owner and appeal route. Do not count comment volume as decision power.
2. For an agent-assisted process, record immediate action, downstream outcome, named owner and intervention window; ask the owner to explain their responsibility without relying on a generic approval click.
3. Compare a single-agent and parallel-agent run on the **same non-sensitive task**: elapsed time, total tokens/cost, review minutes, duplicate errors and final quality. Report both latency and total resource use.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**Participation becomes agency only when the affected person's input can alter the decision—and when somebody is answerable for the final effect.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Use an invented, non-student scenario: a district is considering a public FAQ assistant. Draft three fictional comments—one requests a language option, one asks for an appeal route and one asks not to deploy. Ask a colleague to trace each into a hypothetical decision log and identify what changed, what did not, who decided and how a commenter could challenge the response. Do not enter real student, parent, employee or public-comment records into an external model.
