## The Independent-Review and Response Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Four newly indexed sources—two October 20 research *proposals/frameworks* and two October 19 newsletter essays—point to a distinction: collecting more signals is not the same as giving people more effective judgment or voice. Five findings below include a second, explicitly labeled implication of the employee-voice proposal; they are **not five independent studies**. Web search returned no results on two distinct queries, so this digest uses directly inspected publisher RSS full text and arXiv RSS abstracts. None of the proposed studies establishes a treatment effect.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Work]] × [[The Participation Problem]]: AI can increase the throughput of listening without increasing workers' say over outcomes. A response and appeal route matters more than a suggestion counter.
- [[Human Review Checkpoints]] × [[Agentic Verification]]: two agreeing reasoners may share the same error. Independence is a property to audit, not a label applied to a second checker.
- [[Positive Alignment]] × [[AI and Human Flourishing]]: faster science and better insight into model internals are valuable prospects, but flourishing requires distribution, usable correction and outcomes that affected people recognize.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- AI conversational listening may surface ideas that human surveys miss; a proposal does not show it will improve *or* weaken worker well-being. Preserve both measures.
- Human–model error overlap can vary by site, task and how the second answer is shown. An overlap audit may impose cost and still miss rare harms; use it to inform—not replace—clinical governance.
- Mechanistic interpretability may produce genuine safety advances. Conversely, an interpretable description without causal validation may overstate control. Clark's urgency is a reason to test institutional capacity, not to treat a singularity forecast as settled fact.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. In a public-document-only workplace pilot plan, list how suggestions are acknowledged, acted on, declined and appealed; specify a human owner and a non-AI channel for sensitive concerns.
2. Before using a second AI reviewer on consequential decisions, propose blind, independent human/AI judgments on consented or de-identified historical cases and count **shared misses**, not only each reviewer's standalone accuracy.
3. For an interpretability or science-acceleration claim, demand the independent replication/intervention, the actual human beneficiary, and the institution accountable if the claim fails.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**A second channel expands agency only if it adds a different kind of correction: a worker gets an answer, a reviewer catches a different mistake, and an explanation survives an independent test.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Take one *non-sensitive, publicly documented workflow description* and draft a two-column review plan: **what the AI notices** versus **what an independent human can challenge**. Add the stop owner, one plausible shared failure and an escalation response deadline. Do not feed identifiable employee, student or patient material to a model. This is a design exercise, not a clinical or workplace intervention.
