# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-08-22

**Theme: The Provenance Question — yesterday asked what we delegate; today asks whether we can tell what came back. Watermarking makes machine origin measurable at near-zero cost (and the case for it is now being made in the open), while Ulanqab shows where the compute behind the delegation physically lives — and both resolve to the same fork: provenance is one of the first world-building choices we actually get to make.**

## Executive Summary

**Story one — watermarking is cheap, shipped, and being quietly adopted; the backlash rests on misunderstandings.** [AI Text Watermarking Is Free And Good](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-text-watermarking-is-free-and) (Zvi Mowshowitz, 2026-08-21): the Aaronson–Kirchner watermark (secret-key pseudo-randomness, public check API, output scored against the keyed source) has essentially zero impact on output quality — Google has shipped it in Gemini 3.7 Flash since 2024 with a 20-million-message A/B test showing no user-feedback difference — and marginal cost ≈ zero. The EU Code of Practice requires watermarks (signed by the major Western labs); Anthropic quietly began rolling out watermarking to everyone about a week ago, deliberately not differentiating traffic sources; OpenAI intends to follow but will miss the deadline. Zvi rebuts the standard objections one by one: privacy concerns are overblown (it is a statistical test, nothing is stored or tracked; the real risk is the *checking service*, e.g., Turnitin); it does not poison outputs, personalization, or workflows; and the much-criticized property that the watermark survives *in proportion to how many AI detail-choices you keep* is the feature, not the flaw — it measures how much of the text is yours versus the machine's. Anyone building systematic watermark removal, he writes, \"needs to be filed under 'need to ask ourselves, are we the Baddies.'\" The alternative is a no-provenance world of scam calls, fake text, deepfakes, forged documents, and fake reviews. Closing line: *\"We are at the stage where we get to choose which world we are building. The choice to watermark or not to watermark is one of the first forks in the road that we actually get to choose, and we should choose to watermark.\"*

**Story two — the compute behind the boom is moving to the steppe.** [The Unlikely Place at the Center of China's AI Boom](https://www.wired.com/story/the-unlikely-place-at-the-center-of-chinas-ai-boom/) (WIRED, 2026-08-21): Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia (~1.5M people) has seen ~100 data centers opened or begun since 2016, and the 12.5 GW of pledged capacity — more than 70% of it announced in just the last year — exceeds OpenAI Stargate's 10 GW (Goldman Sachs research note, last week). DeepSeek, ByteDance, Alibaba, and Xiaohongshu are building their own infrastructure: the first time Chinese AI companies have invested in their own compute rather than renting cloud capacity. The economics: high elevation and long cold winters for cheap cooling; cheap wind and solar plus abundant coal for power. The constraint: water — Ulanqab gets only ~14 inches of rain a year (as dry as Denver), and the local water company cut waterworks seven hours a night last month; extra cooling water is needed only two months a year. \"Eastern Data, Western Compute\" has been state policy since 2021; Huawei opened the first center in 2016, Apple followed in 2019. One local expert: \"Inner Mongolia has long been the West Virginia of China. It's a coal country\" — but also: \"in three years, maybe it will be completely powered by renewables.\" The geography of frontier compute is being redrawn by a city most of the industry has never visited.

**Story three — the two stories are one question.** Provenance has two registers: *what made this text* (watermarking) and *where was it made* (compute geography). Yesterday's Delegation Decision asked what we hand over; today's evidence asks whether the handoff can be audited at all. The drug-credit case in yesterday's digest (MIT TR) was the authorship version of the same problem — a molecule the press release said the AI \"discovered,\" but five humans on the patent. Watermarking answers it for text: the signal survives in proportion to how many of the machine's detail-choices you kept, which is a direct measurement of how much of the output is yours. Ulanqab answers it for compute: when Chinese labs own their silicon, the West's provenance instruments (watermarks, export controls, geolocation enforcement) apply to Western models only — an asymmetry that is the material condition for everything the export-controls debate assumes away.

## Superagency Connections

- **Provenance is the audit layer of delegation.** The Delegation Decision (08-21) showed trust — not capability — is the binding constraint on institutional delegation. Watermarking is the trust infrastructure for text: it lets a verifier check origin without asking the model or the user anything. Superagency at scale needs exactly this: delegation you can *audit* is delegation you can *keep*.
- **The detail-choices property makes the watermark an agency instrument, not just a compliance device.** If the signal survives in proportion to how many of the machine's detail-choices you kept, then the watermark measures the human share of the output. That turns provenance from a forensic label into a self-audit tool — the same move as Dizon's ICC ≈ .50 (delegation as a measurable trait) and Biswas's visible verification: make the invisible share visible.
- **Compute geography is the material base of the whole Superagency project.** Superagency claims scale with compute access; Ulanqab shows the map is being redrawn — 12.5 GW pledged vs Stargate's 10 GW, with Chinese labs moving from renting to owning. Who owns the physical substrate determines who can build, audit, and benefit (the sovereignty discount from 08-21, applied at the national scale).
- **Watermarking is the rare case where the choice is actually in front of us.** Zvi's framing — \"we get to choose which world we are building\" — is the Superagency thesis in one move: the fork is real, the costs are known, and the decision is being made now by a handful of labs. The opposite of the liability-trap rhetoric from 08-21 (\"too advanced to control\"), where the choice is narrated away.

## Tensions / Counterarguments

- **Stratechery's critique stands on the table.** The wiki already carries the other side (Stratechery 08-12: watermarking is \"a terrible idea, first and foremost for philosophical reasons\"; Zvi AI #181 08-13). Zvi's rebuttal is strong on cost, privacy mechanics, and the removal-arms-race, but the philosophical objection is not fully answered: watermarking makes provenance enforceable *for those with checking access* — it centralizes the authority to verify in the checking services (Turnitin, platform APIs) and does nothing for the person who receives text with no ability to check it. The asymmetry Zvi himself notes (checking is where the power sits) is the same asymmetry critics worry about.
- **The privacy case is genuinely two-sided.** Zvi is right that the scheme itself stores nothing, but the ecosystem question remains: a public check API is a new attack surface (per the 08-13 Withholding Problem thread, every new verification instrument is also a new discrimination instrument — e.g., employers checking whether a job applicant's cover letter was AI-written).
- **Ulanqab is a pledge, not a buildout.** >70% of the 12.5 GW was announced in the last year — announcements, not kilowatt-hours. The water constraint (7-hour nightly shutoffs, ~14 in/yr rain) is a real physical brake on the desert-data-center model, and the coal component cuts against the renewables narrative. The direction is clear; the magnitude is not yet earned.
- **Export-control leverage is eroding, not dead.** The Jailbreak Fallacy page's encryption lesson cuts the other way too: China's own buildout is the strongest evidence that controls motivate domestic substitution — but Ulanqab's compute is domestic *capacity*, not frontier *capability*; the gap between 12.5 GW of inference/training capacity and frontier algorithmic access is exactly what the controls debate is about.

## Practical Applications

- **Treat watermarking as a procurement default.** For district/org AI tooling: prefer providers that ship watermarking (Google's Gemini has had it since 2024; Anthropic is rolling out; OpenAI is coming), and write \"verifiable provenance\" into vendor checklists — it costs nothing and preserves the option of auditing student/AI-assisted work later. The 08-12 Anthropic explainer (Articles.md) is the implementation reference.
- **Use the detail-choices test as a personal audit.** For any delegated text you keep, identify the specific detail-choices the model made (the examples, the ordering, the phrasings) and rewrite the ones you care about. The share you keep is the share that is yours — the watermark (where checkable) is a mirror for the Delegation Journal.
- **Map your compute geography.** For anything you rely on, ask where it runs: US cloud, EU, China, or your own rack. The sovereignty discount (08-21) at the org level has a geography: latency, jurisdiction, export-control exposure, and provenance-instrument coverage all change with the physical location of the inference.
- **Watch the checking-services power concentration.** The real risk Zvi flags is not the watermark — it's the checker. Any provenance regime should specify who may check, under what standard of evidence, and with what appeal (the Agentic Verification / Human Review Checkpoints frame from the wiki's framework section).
- **School context:** the provenance question is the student-writing question. Watermark detection is a statistical test, not an accusation — a positive signal should trigger a conversation, never a grade (see the AI Tutor Evaluation Checklist and the 08-06 Comprehension Bottleneck thread on why detection-based policing fails).
