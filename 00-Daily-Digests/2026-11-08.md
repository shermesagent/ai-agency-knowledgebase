## Executive Summary
The best AI systems don't merely do more: they make changes visible and reversible, reserve creator credit, disclose when a simulation is not real-world proof, and keep physical systems under an independent stop path. A useful recipe adaptation is a case of careful augmentation; an internet-exposed refrigeration controller shows what happens when the actuation boundary is missing. Human mathematical collaboration remains a counterpoint to the assumption that more automated proofs always mean more scientific understanding.

## Top 5 Findings
1. **Adaptation can be legible without impersonating an author.** NYT Cooking displays the original, marks changed instructions and warns the customized version is untested by the original creator. Its edited changes still led the reviewer to an overcooked chicken texture. Useful personalization is not equivalent to tested quality or creator consent. → [[AI and Creator Rights]], [[Creativity]].
2. **World-model training has a human data supply chain.** WIRED describes visual simulations for robotics and driving, plus a reporter's paid household video capture. A simulated success is not permission for home camera retention or physical action. → [[Home Server AI Agents]], [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]].
3. **Human proof-making is a collaboration outcome.** The Graham-conjecture solution joined partial approaches and a later anti-concentration argument; the result applies to sufficiently large primes, not every prime. The resulting explanation and scope matter alongside speed. → [[AI-Augmented Scientific Collaboration]], [[The Comprehension Bottleneck]].
4. **Physical limits beat a chatbot's promises.** WIRED reports manipulated refrigeration temperatures; French blood-service alarms and staff reportedly intervened before samples were damaged. It remains unclear who carried out the incidents or whether they are linked. → [[Home Server AI Agents]], [[Responsible Deployment]].
5. **Public safety guidance is operational, not magical.** Joint federal guidance recommends removing internet exposure, segmenting controllers, patching, logging and maintaining safe setpoint ranges. This corroborates the *threat class* in finding 4, not every detail of WIRED's incident reporting. → [[Home Server AI Agents]], [[Sandbox Integrity]].

## Superagency Connections
**Capacity:** adapt an expert recipe, rehearse physical tasks in simulation and extend mathematical search. **Freedom:** retain the unmodified artifact and the ability to decline capture or stop a machine. **Platforms:** the cooking app, data marketplace and controller network determine who sets boundaries. **Value:** a meal that works, a proof people can check and inventory that stays safe are better outcomes than feature counts. **Wisdom:** distinguish reported demonstrations, theorems with limited scope and primary operational guidance. See [[Human Agency]] and [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]].

## Tensions / Counterarguments
- A disclaimer does not compensate a creator or guarantee a dietary substitution is safe. The iOS beta is promising as an interface pattern, not a blanket approval of AI recipe rewriting.
- Simulation can reduce dangerous real-world experimentation, but training on household video requires consent and a separate physical test before deployment.
- A human proof is not evidence against AI-assisted mathematics. Nor does a machine-checkable proof by itself guarantee that its statement answers the question humans care about.
- The refrigeration reports concern conventional connected-device weaknesses. Do not market this incident as evidence of an AI-enabled cyberattack; use it as a caution for AI agents proposed to control similar devices.

## Practical Applications
Before allowing an assistant to adapt an authored artifact or control a device, demand a **visible boundary card**: original and diff; attribution and permission; data-capture consent and retention; independent real-world validation; human action owner; external stop and restore path. Keep consequential data and commands outside exploratory tools.

## Practical Experiment to Try
With an approved tool and a *public-domain or self-authored* sample instruction sheet, ask one person to make a safe, non-dietary substitution. Have a second person compare the revised version with the original and identify every material change, its author and whether it was tested. Next, run a **tabletop-only** physical-agent scenario using an imaginary temperature-controller diagram: mark which components would be internet-accessible, who receives an independent alarm and who can stop an unsafe setpoint. Record whether the reviewer can reverse the adaptation and identify the external stop owner. Do not connect the exercise to live refrigeration, cameras, student data or health records.
