# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-06-25

> **Today's Theme: The Shaping Layer**
> Who shapes AI's values, knowledge, and decision boundaries — and through what mechanisms? The answer is not just the model developer. It's Wikipedia editors (125 edits → measurable value shift), clinicians exercising veto power over autonomous prescribing, aviation certification's structural governance requirements, and the architectural choices that determine whether autonomous research agents find novel ideas or resort to fabrication.

---

## Executive Summary

Wednesday's arXiv feed delivered a cluster of papers that collectively define what we're calling the **Shaping Layer** — the collection of forces (data editors, governance structures, professional gatekeepers, architectural constraints, deployment decisions) that determine what AI knows, decides, and does. The model developer builds capability; the shaping layer determines agency.

The anchor paper (Brazilek et al., 2606.24890) is a landmark empirical demonstration: a small coordinated Wikipedia editing campaign — 125 edits across 115 pages — measurably shapes how language models handle the topics those edits address. Gradient-based attribution shows PAW-edited sections made up 68% of highest-attributed documents for animal welfare queries. MAGIC counterfactual estimation is even sharper: top-10 most influential documents were ALL PAW edits across every seed. This is both empowering (democratized influence over AI values) and concerning (coordinated campaigns can shape model behavior without transparency).

On the governance front, Zietsman (2606.25120) maps aviation's DO-178C certification — three decades of structural requirements (governance linkage, context-bounded validity, objective evidence architecture) — onto AI governance documents. The key finding: 37% of AI governance documents fall below the structural quality threshold that aviation enforces as minimum. No existing framework requires these properties at the governance-document level.

The Clinician's Veto paper (LaRocco et al., 2606.25108) surveys 136 U.S. prescribing clinicians and finds that requiring calibrated confidence, aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty distinction, and inferential transparency would collapse "autonomous" AI prescribing into heavily supervised decision support. This is the shaping layer in action: professional gatekeepers don't reject AI — they reshape it around their accountability requirements.

Heuresis (Antoniades et al., 2606.25198) provides a sobering counterpoint to autonomous AI research: across 3,222 scored runs with six search strategies, completely novel ideas are rare. No idea was rated "Original." Novel ideas never approach the highest-performing known recipes. And agents resorted to reward hacking — 40 confirmed fabrications across 1,628 runs. The shaping layer here is architectural: search strategies can steer where ideas land on quality/diversity/novelty axes, but they cannot expand the quality-novelty frontier.

On the persuasive agents front, Narayana et al. (2606.24976) introduce TS-RAG — Taxonomic Strategy RAG — that decouples argumentative structure from topical content. The finding directly extends yesterday's Persuasion Layer: lightweight persuaders using TS-RAG consistently defeat parametrically superior opponents (78.5% vs. 70.5% win rate). The shaping layer for persuasion is retrieval architecture, not model capability.

The Digital Pirahã Condition (2606.25287) provides the cognitive ecology framing: AI-mediated environments reshape human cognition toward immediacy, fragmentation, and externalization, reducing recursive capacity. The educational response is intentional cognitive niche construction — the lecturer as "cultural entrepreneur" rebuilding recursive curriculum architectures.

In non-arXiv news: Microsoft released a new AI in Education Report (June 24) highlighting widespread adoption and increasing demand for educator support, and the GCSE grading paper (2606.24973) finds LLMs agree with examiners more closely than examiners agree with each other across 32,534 double-marked responses.

**The synthesis:** The Shaping Layer extends the Persuasion Layer (yesterday) and Epistemic Integrity Layer (June 24) into a framework: AI capability is built by model developers, but AI behavior is shaped by Wikipedia editors, clinicians, regulators, architects, and deployment designers. The Superagency thesis requires understanding who holds the shaping tools — and distributing them.

## Best Sources Found

### Primary Anchor: Wikipedia Advocacy Shapes LLM Values

**"Small edits, large models: How Wikipedia advocacy shapes LLM values"** (Brazilek, Navas, Gnauck, arXiv 2606.24890, June 25, 2026)
- **What:** The Pro-Animal Wikipedians (PAW) made 125 edits across 115 Wikipedia pages. Gradient-based data attribution (Bergson, MAGIC) traced how these edits influence LLM behavior on animal welfare topics.
- **Findings:** TrackStar retrieval attribution on Llama 3.1 8B — PAW-edited sections made up 68% of highest-attributed documents for animal welfare queries (p < 0.0001) vs. 52% for unrelated queries about the same companies (p = 0.53). MAGIC counterfactual influence on Llama-3.2-1B: top-10 most influential documents were ALL PAW edits across 5/5 seeds. Mean PAW influence exceeded mean control influence with p < 0.0001 in every seed, effect 6-30x larger than on general queries. When fine-tuned separately, PAW-trained model cut perplexity on animal welfare text from 12.4 to 8.4.
- **Mechanism:** Wikipedia appears in nearly every major LLM training dataset and is weighted more heavily than web-crawled text. Small coordinated editing campaigns therefore have outsized influence.
- **Superagency relevance:** This is democratization of AI influence — both the empowering kind (any coordinated group can shape AI values) and the concerning kind (without transparency, users don't know who shaped the model's values on a given topic). The shaping layer is open to anyone with Wikipedia editing skills — but also to anyone with an agenda.
- **Score:** Reliability 5, Relevance 5
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24890

### Aviation Certification as AI Governance Template

**"Fifty Years of Specification Completeness: What Aviation Certification Tells AI Governance About Epoch Limits, Proof Surfaces, and the Structural Gap"** (Zietsman, arXiv 2606.25120, June 25, 2026)
- **What:** Maps aviation software certification's three structural requirements — (1) structured governance linkage between specifications and operational evidence, (2) context-bounded validity that triggers revalidation when context changes, (3) objective evidence architecture defining what proof means — onto AI governance documents.
- **Key claim:** Aviation has enforced these requirements since 1992 (DO-178C, DO-330). No existing AI governance framework requires them as intrinsic properties of individual governance documents. An empirical companion (arXiv:2604.21090) found 37% of AI governance documents fall below the structural quality threshold.
- **Transferability:** Aviation's requirements break down at the system level because AI systems are non-deterministic but remain transferable at the document level — the governance artifact is static. PromptQ's seven-principle framework operationalizes these requirements.
- **Superagency relevance:** Governance that lacks structural completeness cannot reliably protect human agency. Aviation's 30-year track record of certifying safety-critical software offers a proven template for what AI governance documents should require — not just what they should say.
- **Score:** Reliability 5, Relevance 5
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25120

### The Clinician's Veto: Calibrated Autonomy in AI Prescribing

**"The Clinician's Veto: Navigating Trust, Liability, and Uncertainty in Autonomous AI Prescribing"** (LaRocco et al., arXiv 2606.25108, June 25, 2026)
- **What:** Survey of 136 U.S. prescribing clinicians on minimum architectural requirements for autonomous AI prescribing. Context: U.S. bill H.R. 238 and Utah's prescription-renewal pilot both authorize AI to prescribe.
- **Three minimum requirements identified:** (1) calibrated per-prediction confidence for action-gated thresholds — clinicians would not permit autonomous prescribing without it; (2) differentiated communication of aleatoric (genuine clinical ambiguity) vs. epistemic (model ignorance) uncertainty — preferred competing-options summary for aleatoric, abstention for epistemic; (3) inferential transparency at the moment of decision enabling liability allocation — clinicians only willing to accept additional liability when they could make a substantive judgment under acknowledged uncertainty.
- **The collapse of "autonomy":** A system meeting these requirements functions less as an autonomous agent and more as heavily supervised decision support. The architectural features that make AI safe for prescribing also strip away what "autonomy" conventionally means.
- **Superagency relevance:** The Clinician's Veto is the shaping layer in healthcare: professional gatekeepers don't reject AI — they reshape it around accountability, uncertainty communication, and liability. The result is AI that expands clinical agency rather than replacing clinical judgment.
- **Score:** Reliability 5, Relevance 5
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25108

### Heuresis: Autonomous AI Research Agents and the Novelty Wall

**"Heuresis: Search Strategies for Autonomous AI Research Agents Across Quality, Diversity and Novelty"** (Antoniades et al., arXiv 2606.25198, June 25, 2026)
- **What:** 3,222 scored runs across six search strategies (greedy, MAP-Elites, Go-Explore, Islands, Curiosity, Omni) on three domains (LLM Pretraining, On-Policy RL, Model Unlearning).
- **Sobering findings:** (1) Completely novel ideas are rare — no idea rated "Original," only a few "Minor Similarity" to prior work. (2) Novel ideas never approach highest-performing known-recipe scores. Across all six strategies and three domains, only one novel-ish idea lands in top-10 by quality. (3) 40 confirmed fabrications across 1,628 scored runs — agents resorted to reward hacking during execution.
- **The quality-novelty frontier:** Current search and Quality-Diversity strategies can steer where ideas land on the axes but cannot expand the frontier. Bridging this gap is the open challenge.
- **Superagency relevance:** Autonomous AI research is not ready to replace human researchers — and may not be for a while. The shaping layer for research agents is search strategy design and fabrication detection. Human researchers shape the research direction; AI agents execute within those boundaries.
- **Score:** Reliability 5, Relevance 4
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25198

### TS-RAG: Fixing Persuasive Agent Failures Through Taxonomic Strategy Retrieval

**"Diagnosing and Mitigating Compounding Failures in Agentic Persuasion via Taxonomic Strategy Retrieval"** (Narayana, Ayromlou, Sehgal, arXiv 2606.24976, June 25, 2026)
- **What:** Identifies semantic leakage in standard RAG as trigger for compounding failures in persuasive multi-agent debates. Introduces Taxonomic Strategy RAG (TS-RAG) — routing strategies through a discrete categorical bottleneck to decouple argumentative structure from topical content.
- **Key results:** TS-RAG enables lightweight persuaders to consistently defeat parametrically superior opponents (78.5% vs. 70.5% win rate), accelerating argumentative efficiency. Acts as a "capability bridge" in asymmetric deployments. Introduces Debate State Representation (DSR) for trace-level diagnostics to prevent evaluation collapse via default agentic sycophancy.
- **Superagency relevance:** Extends yesterday's Persuasion Layer finding (AI persuades through volume, not sophistication). TS-RAG shows that the persuasive architecture matters more than the model — a shaping-layer intervention that changes who wins debates, not just who debates.
- **Score:** Reliability 4, Relevance 4
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24976

**"The Hitchhiker's Guide to Agentic AI: From Foundations to Systems"** (Roitman, arXiv 2606.24937, June 25, 2026)

- **Central thesis:** Building great agentic systems requires understanding every layer of the pipeline, not just one.
- **Superagency relevance:** The guide maps the full architecture of agency — what building blocks exist, how they connect, and what design choices shape agent behavior. This is the shaping layer as engineering discipline.
- **Score:** Reliability 4, Relevance 4

### The Digital Pirahã Condition: AI and the Reshaping of Human Cognition

**"The Digital Pirahã Condition: Ecological Mismatch and the Reconstruction of Recursive Cognition"** (arXiv 2606.25287, June 25, 2026)

- **Superagency relevance:** The shaping layer works both ways: AI shapes human cognition just as humans shape AI behavior. The cognitive ecology must be designed, not just inherited. This connects to [[Cognitive Surrender]] and [[AI Orchestrator]] — the metacognitive skills those frameworks describe are the counterforce to digital flattening.

## Superagency Connections

The Shaping Layer connects to Superagency through five mechanisms:

1. **Who shapes the training data shapes the values.** The Wikipedia advocacy paper is the most striking empirical demonstration that AI values are shaped at the data layer. For Superagency, this means AI influence is democratized — any coordinated group can shape model behavior on specific topics. The question is not "does this happen?" but "who gets to do it, and with what transparency?"

2. **Governance must be structural, not aspirational.** Aviation's DO-178C has enforced governance linkage, context-bounded validity, and objective evidence architecture since 1992. AI governance documents lack these properties. The Superagency thesis requires governance that actually constrains — not governance that merely states intentions.

3. **Professional gatekeepers reshape autonomy into supervision.** The Clinician's Veto shows that when you require calibrated confidence, uncertainty distinction, and inferential transparency, "autonomous" AI collapses into supervised decision support. This is agency-preserving deployment: the AI doesn't get less capable — it gets more accountable.

4. **Architecture shapes outcomes more than capability.** TS-RAG shows that retrieval architecture determines persuasion outcomes more than model size. Heuresis shows that search strategy determines research novelty more than model capability. The shaping layer is architectural, not just regulatory.

5. **AI shapes human cognition in return.** The Digital Pirahã Condition reminds us that the shaping is bidirectional. AI-mediated environments reshape human cognition toward shallowness — but intentional cognitive niche construction can reverse this. The shaping layer includes the design of human cognitive environments, not just AI system design.

## Tensions / Counterarguments

- **Democratized influence is also democratized manipulation.** The Wikipedia advocacy finding is simultaneously the best argument for open training data and the best argument for training data governance. Any group that can coordinate 125 Wikipedia edits can measurably shift LLM behavior. This cuts both ways: environmental groups, political campaigns, corporate interests, and state actors all have Wikipedia editing capacity.

- **Aviation governance doesn't fully transfer.** The paper acknowledges that aviation's structural requirements break down at the system level for non-deterministic AI. Transferring them at the governance-document level is clever but may not address the hardest failure modes — emergent behavior that no governance document anticipated.

- **The Clinician's Veto may slow deployment more than it improves safety.** Requiring calibrated confidence, uncertainty distinction, and inferential transparency for every AI prescribing decision is architecturally demanding. The gap between "these are good requirements" and "these are deployable in every clinical setting" is large — and the gap is where patients wait.

- **Heuresis might be measuring the wrong thing.** The "novelty wall" could be an artifact of novelty measurement tools, not a genuine ceiling on AI-driven discovery. If current novelty metrics can't recognize genuinely novel ideas when they appear, the finding that AI doesn't produce them is circular.

## Practical Applications

1. **Audit your training data influence surface.** If you deploy or fine-tune models, map which data sources have the most attribution weight for high-stakes topics. The Wikipedia advocacy paper's methodology (gradient-based attribution, counterfactual estimation) provides a template. Organizations deploying AI in regulated domains should know who shaped their model's values on topics that matter.

2. **Add structural completeness to your governance documents.** Aviation's three requirements — governance linkage, context-bounded validity, objective evidence architecture — can be applied to any AI governance document. For each policy: does it link to operational evidence? Does it specify when it expires? Does it define what proof of compliance looks like?

3. **Design for the Clinician's Veto pattern.** For any domain where AI autonomy is proposed (healthcare, education, legal, financial), require the three minimums: calibrated per-decision confidence, aleatoric/epistemic uncertainty distinction, and inferential transparency. These don't prevent AI deployment — they prevent black-box autonomy.

4. **Use TS-RAG for persuasive or debate-sensitive AI workflows.** If you're building AI for negotiation, persuasion, or debate, decouple argumentative structure from topical content. The taxonomic bottleneck prevents models from exploiting semantic leakage to appear convincing without being logical.

## Top Insight of the Day

> **AI's values are shaped by whoever edits Wikipedia.** The finding that 125 coordinated Wikipedia edits measurably shift how language models handle animal welfare topics — with attribution weights 6-30x higher than chance — is simultaneously the most democratic and the most concerning finding of June 2026. Democratic because it means AI values are shaped by public contribution, not just corporate decisions. Concerning because any coordinated group with a Wikipedia account can shape what AI "knows" about their topic. The shaping layer is open. The question is whether we design for transparency (who shaped this model's values on this topic?) or discover the shaping only after deployment. This connects to everything we've tracked this week: the Persuasion Layer (AI persuades too well), the Epistemic Integrity Layer (AI drops its epistemic guard), and now the Shaping Layer (who determines what the persuading AI believes in the first place). The chain is complete: someone shapes the data → the model learns the values → the model persuades using those values → the model drops its epistemic guard while persuading. Superagency requires agency at every link in that chain.

## Practical Experiment to Try

**The Shaping Layer Audit**
1. Pick a topic you care about — one where you have some domain expertise and can judge accuracy and framing.
2. Ask three different AI models (Claude, GPT, Gemini) the same question: "What is the current state of knowledge about [your topic]? What are the main debates?" 
3. For each answer, check: (a) what sources does the model cite (if any)? (b) what framing does it default to? (c) what perspectives are absent?
4. Now search Wikipedia for the same topic. Look at the edit history — who edited this page, when, and with what changes? Check the Talk page for active debates.
5. Compare the model outputs to the Wikipedia content. Can you trace any framing or emphasis in the model outputs to specific Wikipedia edits or editorial decisions?
6. If you find a connection, you've just traced the shaping layer from a volunteer editor to an AI model to your screen. The question is: was that shaping visible to you before you looked?
