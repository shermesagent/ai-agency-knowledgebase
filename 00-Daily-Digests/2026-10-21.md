## The Calibration-Before-Delegation Test

## 1. Executive Summary
Four directly inspected sources—two October 21 arXiv RSS abstracts and two October 20 full-text newsletter essays—suggest that *how* an AI intervenes matters at least as much as whether it can produce an answer. This digest separates measured university/financial-decision results from a practitioner's chart-making demonstration and an advocate's policy critique. Web search returned empty for two distinct queries; the publisher feeds and paper abstracts supplied the evidence. Five findings below draw on **four** sources, not five independent studies.

## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[AI Tutors]] × [[Co-Intelligence]]: a learner can receive many polished exercises and still need a human to diagnose the error that blocks progress.
- [[AI as Copilot]] × [[Human Agency]]: abstention is a design choice that can shift people in opposite directions; advice acceptance alone is not agency or decision quality.
- [[Balanced Governance]] × [[Responsible Deployment]]: the power to stop a dangerous system is only meaningful if the legal and operational threshold permits timely use; compare text and independent legal analysis before taking sides.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- More practice may help even when feedback diagnosis is weak; these exam analyses cannot isolate its contribution. Conversely, a top-rated exercise should not be sold as proven learning gain.
- Selective advice may ease expert burden but confuse novices who need explanation of *why* a system abstains. Perceived expertise is not objective expertise.
- Agent-made charts can widen a person's analytical reach, but hidden data transformations and unattributed choices may shrink inspectability. Mollick's positive anecdote does not settle the tradeoff.
- A strong emergency stop could prevent harm or invite overbroad state intervention. The Zvi essay offers one side of a live policy dispute, not a neutral legal finding.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. For a math-feedback pilot, score diagnosis, clarity, student revision and a later unaided item separately. Use approved, de-identified or synthetic-free publicly available materials for a design review; real student work requires district authorization and data protections.
2. In an advice tool, log *when* it abstains and provide a human escalation path; evaluate correct decisions separately for novices and experts, not raw acceptance.
3. Require an agent-produced chart to ship with an auditable data table, source URLs, formulas and a named human reviewer.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**The right AI behavior is not simply more output: it may be a better diagnosis, a justified abstention or a traceable delegation. Measure the human's next decision, not just the system's visible activity.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
Take a *public, non-sensitive* chart and its source data. Ask an agent to recreate it, then independently check two plotted values, one label and one omitted caveat. Record whether the original data and calculation steps can be recovered without asking the agent to explain its own opaque process. This is a design exercise, not a claim of validated tool performance; use no student, employee or patient records.
