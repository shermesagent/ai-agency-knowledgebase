# Daily AI Agency Digest — 2026-09-23

## The Delegation Boundary — who gets to decide what the work means?

## 1. Executive Summary

Five new sources point to one practical distinction: AI can make execution cheaper without transferring the right to define the goal, interpret the result, or impose costs on someone else. This is especially visible in wearable surveillance, workplace restructuring, synthetic market research, classroom tools, and data storytelling. **Today’s sources: two reported articles and three arXiv preprints; no claim that a preprint is independently replicated.** `web_search` returned empty results; source discovery came from publisher/arXiv RSS, direct article extraction, and arXiv RSS abstracts.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments

- Wearable cameras can improve accessibility and hands-free work; bystander consent and exposure are still separate design obligations. The MIT TR piece reports competing claims about whether capture lights can be evaded; do not treat the technical dispute as resolved.
- AT&T’s copper retirement can lower energy use, but lower operating costs do not prove AI caused job loss or that rural customers have comparable alternatives.
- Persona simulation may help ideation or qualitative exploration; the headline study tests *prediction of aggregate click behavior*, not every possible persona use.
- A governance framework is not a learning-gain study. A district pilot needs student work and teacher review, not a six-box certification.
- Expert storytellers’ practice is not necessarily novice practice; verification may impose new labor even when generation saves time.

## 5. Practical Applications

1. For an AI-supported public-facing workflow, list **users, bystanders, workers, and affected nonusers** separately; document who benefits, who can object, and where the appeal goes.
2. For a K–12 tutoring purchase, record permitted role (coach/assessor), teacher approval, student data boundary, learning outcome, escalation, and revision owner before a pilot. No student-level data should enter a vendor or this wiki during the exercise.
3. For AI-generated visuals or messaging, retain the original evidence, a no-AI or simpler baseline, and a named human who signs off on the interpretation.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day

**The delegation boundary is not where AI stops generating. It is where a human must still have the authority—and evidence—to say what the output means, who it affects, and whether it should be used.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try

**The Boundary Card (one workflow, one meeting):** pick an AI-supported district task *without entering student information*. Write four lines: (1) What can the AI draft? (2) What decision stays with a named person? (3) Which student, teacher, family, or bystander could be harmed or misrepresented? (4) What baseline observation would falsify the promised benefit? Run a small pilot only if all four answers are clear; compare a reviewed AI draft with the existing human workflow on the same rubric. Record time **including review**, errors, and any loss of human discretion.
