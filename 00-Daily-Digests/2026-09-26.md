## Optionality Needs a Decision Gate

## 1. Executive Summary
Five independently checked sources—not five versions of the same announcement—connect three questions: **What should people attempt with more capable agents? Who authorizes the attempt? Who can reverse a bad decision?** Zvi Mowshowitz argues that Claude Opus 5.5 should raise users' ambitions, but his evidence mixes vendor benchmarks, anecdotes, and personal testing. RAND recommends preserving strategic options under five pivotal uncertainties. A divided court allowed one Pentagon supply-chain designation of Anthropic to stand; that is a specific procurement dispute, not an all-purpose AI ban. WIRED reports that Meta's adults-only Muse has childlike branding, contested age appeal, and an opt-out training default. Steven Levy disputes the story that the technology industry was always indifferent to public purpose. The useful synthesis is not “go faster” or “stop”: expand ambition **with explicit permission and independently checkable stop rules**. ## 3. Superagency Connections
- [[Pacing the Frontier]] and [[The Artificial State]]: strategic optionality without a trigger, independent review, decision owner, and appeal route risks becoming either permanent delay or arbitrary power.
- [[Export Controls and the Jailbreak Fallacy]]: procurement designation and export licensing are different legal instruments; collapsing them produces bad analysis of who actually lost access.
- [[Cognitive Surrender]] and [[Digital Fiduciary Duty]]: attractive agent experiences can be genuinely useful while still demanding clear opt-out, age, memory, and connector boundaries.
- [[Human Agency]]: more achievable projects are valuable only when the person retains the authority to define the goal and cancel execution.

## 4. Tensions / Counterarguments
- More capable agents can make formerly impossible work practical, but anecdotal “it just works” reports are not an incident-rate estimate; separate *talk about a task* from authorization to act.
- Preserving options is prudent under uncertainty, but a government can use “optionality” to avoid making a decision. RAND's strategy needs dated gates and public accountability, not just more capacity-building.
- The court majority accepted a procurement-risk rationale while another proceeding reached a different outcome on a separate designation. Neither resolves the broader ethical question of military AI or the June export-control dispute.
- A friendly mascot can lower the barrier to useful tools. Adults may welcome it; children's appeal remains a concern to examine, not evidence of intentional targeting.
- Levy's defense of the industry's idealist tradition can counter fatalism without excusing contemporary choices about military contracts, concentrated power, or consent.

## 5. Practical Applications
1. **Two-key agent work:** a user may approve research and drafting, but external actions (sending, purchasing, system changes, data transfers) require a separate visible confirmation showing scope and rollback path.
2. **Policy gate card:** write one line each for trigger, evidence, independent reviewer, accountable decision-maker, reversal procedure, and date for reassessment.
3. **District procurement hygiene:** never let an appealing student-facing avatar stand in for verified age controls, training-use defaults, retention/deletion terms, and teacher oversight. No student-level data enters a vendor trial before approved privacy review.

## 8. Top Insight of the Day
**Capability widens the set of possible actions; agency depends on keeping the authorization, evidence standard, and reversal authority outside the model.**

## 9. Practical Experiment to Try
**30-minute no-data delegation drill:** choose a hypothetical agent task such as summarizing public board packets. In writing, authorize reading public files and drafting a summary, but not sending emails or changing records. Have a reviewer mark every point at which the task might cross into external action. Add one approval prompt and one stop/rollback step for each crossing. Use only public or invented task instructions—no student records, personnel files, or live connectors. Success is a clear boundary map, not a claim that the agent succeeded in production.
